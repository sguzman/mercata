# Hell Forge: one developer's low-gross first-game disclosure versus work and cash

This case is a *voluntary, unverified author disclosure*, not Steam's actual transaction ledger or a sample of all first-time publishers.

## A date-bound first commercial release

In a [May 25, 2026 first-person postmortem](https://www.gabylab.com/blog/hell-forge-retrospective), the developer of *Hell Forge* reports **450 copies sold and around USD $1,500 gross** by that point. [The public Steam listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4118220/Hell_Forge/) corroborates the **March 4, 2026 release** and a distinct named developer and publisher. It does **not** confirm any copy total, gross revenue, contractual share or bank receipt. The author's accompanying screenshot is their own disclosed material, not an independently authenticated financial audit.

The author's reported **20 reviews in the first month** must not be divided into the **450 copies reported by May 25** as if the measurements covered identical windows. Approximate gross divided by reported copies is **$3.33 per unit** for this single disclosure, but does not identify a uniform transaction price, refunds, regional discounts, tax treatment or a payout percentage. The public listing's subsequently displayed review count or price is not automatically the March release-window value.

## Intensive work does not turn gross into hourly pay

The author describes working *roughly* **11 hours per day for six months, weekends included**. No exact day count, labor log, equipment expenses, outsourcing, bank deposit, personal taxes or profit statement appears in the account. A back-calculated hourly wage would imply precision and a personal gross allocation that are not documented. The Steam listing labels **Gaby Rla as developer** and **LiberateGames as publisher**; that role separation requires further rights and payout evidence before attributing any share of reported game gross to an individual. Neither the legal payee nor revenue-sharing agreement was accessed.

## Inference limits

At most, this is one first-game example of low reported gross after several months of work. It cannot establish the population's mean, median, proportion with zero sales, the author's true profit, a typical review multiplier, or a first-game-versus-established-studio causal effect. The [synthetic guard](../../site/steam-firstgame-selfreport-guard.mjs) validates safe aggregate arithmetic but refuses to compare mismatched measurement windows or to report actual take-home from gross.

**No private publisher, customer or tax records were accessed.** No observed actual bank cash, representative earnings distribution or zero-sale rate is claimed.
