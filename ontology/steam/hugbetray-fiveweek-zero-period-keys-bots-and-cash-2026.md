## One first paid launch, local calendar differences

The [first-party developer postmortem published October 13](https://note.com/yattaaaaaaa777/n/nc92813e9b597?hl=en) dates *HUG＆BETRAY* to September 4, 2026. The [Steam storefront](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5051910/HUGBETRAY) shows **September 3**, while [SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/5051910/info/) reports **September 4 at 03:01 UTC**. Those calendar dates can describe **the same one paid release** in different time zones, not two first-paid launches. The public developer and publisher label is THBdesigns, not an independently authenticated personal bank payee.

## The reported zero is one interval, not a zero-selling game

The author reports **zero sales September 17–27** and **one sale September 27–October 12**, following a store-page messaging change. The two periods have different lengths and no controlled exposure assignment. A before/after observation does not establish that the page change caused an extra purchase or longer engagement; the author also reports median playtime of 12 versus 18 minutes without an independently harmonized test-free player cohort.

Across roughly five weeks, the author reports **four Steam copies including one refund**, plus **one copy via key**. A key transfer is not necessarily a transaction paid through Steam, and the note's informal language of five total copies cannot automatically be equated to five paid orders. The author reports about **$18 total revenue** and **$12 called net earnings**; without the bank and tax ledger, this is a voluntary money label, not audited personal take-home or after-cost profit. The current US $5.99 storefront price is not a historical per-order reconciliation.

## Traffic and player counts measure different events

The author lists **124,000 impressions**, **7,100 visits**, **3,291 direct visits described as mostly automated**, and **753 labeled as bots**. The post does not prove that these categories are mutually exclusive or that impressions are unique people. Wishlists move from **66 → 69 → 75**, which are stocks rather than matched purchase conversions. Only **eight people reportedly played, including test accounts**.

The [store describes a host-pays, free-browser-participant design](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5051910/HUGBETRAY). Consequently even a game with eight genuine participants need not have eight paid game owners; there is no one-buyer-per-player relationship. The storefront's absence of user reviews also does not show zero actual paid sales.

The developer's one positive low-sales title contributes an observed **zero-order time interval** but **not a zero-sale title in a complete paid-release cohort**. It cannot identify representative first-game income, causal marketing effectiveness, after-cost profit or the true market-wide zero fraction.
