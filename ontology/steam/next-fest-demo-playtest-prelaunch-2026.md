# Steam Next Fest, demos and Playtests: policy and research boundaries

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured findings](next-fest-demo-playtest-prelaunch-2026.json). This checkpoint covers public rules and a potential experiment, **not** an actual game launch, festival campaign, observed sales or causal conversion.

## Three distinct product and interest units

Steam's [Demo guide](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos?l=english) assigns demos a separate AppID associated with a base game's AppID, with distinct build and release checklists. Its [Playtest guide](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest?l=english) defines a separate, free child AppID that can accept limited or open signup requests. Playtest participants do not thereby obtain the base game, change the base game's wishlist/review figures, or add hours to its refund playtime counter. **Neither an installed demo nor a Playtest signup is a paid base-game sale.** Valve explicitly prohibits monetizing Playtest access and selling Playtest keys.

## Eligibility and scheduling

Valve's [Steam Next Fest documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest?l=english) requires a published, public base-game page, a public playable demo, and a game that remains **unreleased through the end** of the selected festival. Participation is limited to **one edition per title**. Prologues, previews and re-releases of existing games do not qualify. Upcoming games planning paid Early Access or free release can participate **before** release; entering Early Access before/during the fest disqualifies them. A previous Playtest does not block participation; running one simultaneously with the public demo may split testers.

Valve sets no universal demo-length or exclusivity requirement. The historical [October 2026 edition](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/2026october?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) ran **October 19–26** at **10 AM PDT**; its required demo-review submission was due **September 28** and an optional press preview began **October 8**. These are **not** dates for future editions. Registration belongs to the base game.

## Discovery without false sales conclusions

Steam's [Tag guide](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/tags?language=english) explains that weighted genre tags influence browse-page placement and similar-game recommendations. Demo release can also interact with the **two-week per-AppID wishlist notification cooldown** documented by Valve. None of these mechanisms guarantee a notice, visit, wishlist addition, purchase or profit.

A future low-risk experiment could specify comparable pre/during/post observation windows, channel-split traffic, demo installs, wishlists and paid orders as **separate units**, and account for seasonal and tag-placement confounding. This would be a design rather than an executed causal test. No demo, Playtest, account, campaign, merchant record or observed conversion metric was used in this research.
