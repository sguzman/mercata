# Steam versus Epic Games Store: distribution terms, optional exclusivity and integration costs

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured comparison](steam-versus-epic-pc-distribution-2026.json). This is a **comparative study within Mercata's existing Steam research**, not the admission or economic census of another marketplace. No private partner agreements, game sales or consumer records were accessed.

## The published share percentages are not net publisher earnings

| Dimension | Steam | Epic Games Store |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Submission fee | **$100 per app**, recouped after **$1,000 adjusted gross** from the eligible Steam sales/in-app categories | **$100 per game**, described as recoupable, with separate governing terms |
| Public platform share | Valve reports a **2025 aggregate 76% paid across non-Valve games**; historical 2018 marginal tiers exist, but actual signed contract terms are not public per publisher | **100% on first $1m of Epic-processed net revenue per title, per calendar year**, then **88% developer / 12% Epic** |
| Scope of share threshold | Specific contract and Steam-reporting terms must be checked; historical $10m/$50m marginal brackets aren't universal partner actuals | Applies across all participating Epic-processed product offers, PC/mobile/Web Shop, even across publisher transfers |
| Optional exclusivity | No universal Steam-PC-only exclusivity established by the basic public self-publishing guide | **Epic First Run**, six months of qualified Epic PC-store exclusivity, with 100% share during the term |
| PC multiplayer | Steam supports matchmaking and third-party game infrastructure; actual obligations depend on implementation and contracts | Online multiplayer games must support crossplay across all PC storefronts at all launch stages |
| Achievements | Steam offers achievements tooling | If enabled on other PC stores, Epic requires achievement parity for products onboarded since March 2023 |

Steam evidence: [Steam Direct](https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect/) and [Valve's 2025 annual review](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/steamworks/announcements/detail/528746884222682053). Epic evidence: [distribution FAQ](https://store.epicgames.com/distribution), [annual title revenue-share FAQ](https://store.epicgames.com/distribution/revenue-programs/revenue-share), and [June 2025 announcement](https://store.epicgames.com/news/epic-games-store-updates-revenue-share-keep-100-of-the-first-1m-per-product-per-year).

**Epic's 100% tier does not mean the developer receives 100% of consumer checkout dollars as after-tax personal income.** The base is Epic-processed **net** product revenue and can be subject to separate recoupment agreements, refunds, taxes, costs and private publishing shares. The current $1m title-level threshold resets on January 1 of each year starting 2026, not 365 days after launch; a publisher transfer does not restart it.

Steam's overall 2025 reported 76% payment share is **revenue weighted across many games and contract conditions**, whereas Epic's 100%/88% is a **published marginal schedule**. Taking a direct arithmetic difference between those figures would create a bogus per-game profit estimate.

## Exclusivity is a separate business decision

[Epic First Run](https://store.epicgames.com/distribution/revenue-programs/epic-first-run-program) is an **opt-in** six-month program for eligible newly launched games, including paid Early Access time in the six-month clock. Epic offers 100% net share in the qualified period, with editorial visibility opportunities. A publisher generally cannot sell that same full PC game on Steam while honoring its Epic PC-store exclusivity. First-party direct sales and authorized Epic keyless third-party retail fulfillment are treated as separate exceptions.

If a publisher ends exclusivity early, Epic says standard share terms may be applied **retroactively** from launch; it may offset shortages against amounts payable. Neither an exclusivity payment nor its opportunity cost can be modeled responsibly without demand by store, signed agreement and expected sales across all channels.

## Cross-store networking and achievements can be real engineering costs

Epic's [crossplay guidelines](https://dev.epicgames.com/docs/epic-games-store/requirements-guidelines/distribution-requirements/multiplayer-reqs?lang=en-US) require games with online multiplayer to enable crossplay by default across **all PC storefronts** where they are distributed; this covers multiplayer releases including demos, Early Access and open betas. The developer must submit a compatible build **at least four weeks before launch** for validation. Epic accepts Epic Online Services, another provider or a self-built approach. This requirement doesn't apply to a simple single-player game.

The [Epic distributor FAQ](https://store.epicgames.com/distribution) also says post-March-2023 self-published games supporting achievements on other PC storefronts must implement corresponding Epic achievements. Storefront-specific features, online session compatibility, support effort and release QA can affect costs even when share percentages look favorable.

## The valid decision

The comparison is a **menu of conditions and constraints**, not a ranking of commercial outcomes. Choosing a store for a first paid indie title requires the same game's relative buyers, eligible paid-order prices and discounts, refunds, taxes, developer contractual share, porting work, support costs and exclusivity opportunity costs. Neither store's total registered users, game catalog size or revenue percentages identifies that net result.

**Still unknown:** typical first-game net proceeds on either store, the incremental effect of simultaneous launches or exclusivity, after-cost developer hourly income, and which store would maximize profit for any specific project.
