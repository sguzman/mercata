# Steam publishing economics: executive synthesis and decision gates

**As of October 28, 2026.** [Structured evidence and decision map](executive-economic-evidence-and-decision-gates-2026.json). This is a research synthesis of Mercata's Steam studies, **not a forecast or recommendation to release a game**.

## Main conclusion

Steam is a functioning, documented route for distributing paid games, but available public data **does not establish a reliable median first-time indie developer's net income or profit**. The best open full-release-month evidence we have is a third-party *modeled gross game revenue* distribution with a very large lower tail. Gross game revenue is not the developer's take-home pay, and game-level data is not company-level data.

The [developer-site cash cases](developer-operated-site-revenue-cases-2026.md), [first-game launch audit](first-commercial-launch-paid-funnel-case-2026.md), [historical zero-proceeds cases](historical-zero-proceeds-versus-zero-sales-2026.md), and [publisher profit-sign study](publisher-profitability-partial-identification-2026.md) expand **bounded observations** but do not change the population-level conclusion. One published developer's reported net sales and another's claimed bank receipts are separate accounting concepts; a 2018 early zero personal payout did not mean zero sold games.

A later [Studio Ortica portfolio account](steam-three-title-studio-and-firstyear-gross-net-selfreports-2026.md) provides **three games from one reporting studio**, with USD gross and a separately defined EUR figure after the author's tax and foreign-exchange assumptions. [RogueCraft's first-year account](steam-three-title-studio-and-firstyear-gross-net-selfreports-2026.md) gives paid units and refunds but an ambiguous net-revenue label. A [2019 and 2020 historical cash comparison](steam-historical-received-net-versus-development-return-2026.md) additionally distinguishes received partner money from estimated future-month amounts and self-reported income per development month. Notably, the 2019 source's **$6,422 combined net headline** conflicts with its printed by-store net amounts totaling **$6,442**, an explicitly unreconciled **$20 difference**. These are selected accounts, not representative estimates.\n\nThe most important next independent evidence is not another list of successful releases: it is a **historically complete cohort with low/no-sales games, retired titles, authentic paid-order records, and cash/labor accounting**.

## Five evidence layers

| Evidence | What can be stated | What cannot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Official Steamworks** | $100 application fee; $1,000 adjusted-gross fee recoupment threshold; current published 30-day/14-day release prerequisites; financial report categories | Actual publisher contract, royalties or sales |
| **GameDiscoverCo January 2026 model** | Provider-defined **1,230** paid release entries, roughly **$1,000 modeled gross median** and a strongly hit-concentrated distribution | Audited true sales, full-year medians or net developer profit |
| **Developer-authored public case studies** | Reported first-game gross, self-described net labels, some claimed bank receipts, production outlay and launch-window orders | Authenticated publisher remittance, a complete zero-selling sample or representative after-cost income |
| **Gamalytic partial October 2025 snapshot** | **8,388/12,732** tracked 2025 launches below **$1,000 modeled gross** | Final-year paid-only benchmark or directly comparable January 2026 cohort |
| **Explicit hypothetical budget scenario** | At assumed inputs, **1,000 gross units** cover cash costs; **5,410 gross units** include 500 hours of labor at $20/hour | A real publisher's costs, break-even odds or recommended sales target |

[Steam Direct and onboarding](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding) currently both say **30 days after app-fee payment** for first titles, plus a **separate public Coming Soon page for 14 days**, potentially overlapping; review approvals must also be completed. Our [historical-source recheck](current-first-release-wait-recheck-2026.md) documents an earlier 21-day text discrepancy without concealing it.

The [January 2026 provider-model release study](january-2026-modeled-paid-release-distribution.md) reports approximately **100 modeled copies** at the median and only **16 of 1,230** games exceeding $1 million modeled gross, while the mean is near **$108,000 gross** because the top is extreme. Egmatic reports **412 modeled zero-copy entries** among **1,211 with estimates**. There were **19 missing models**, so this is neither a measured zero-sale rate nor an income result. [Gamalytic model validation](model-validation-and-low-end-bounds-2026.md) excluded games under 1,000 disclosed sales, leaving the weakest publishers unvalidated.

The [synthetic first-game financial model](synthetic-first-game-break-even-scenarios-2026.md) assumes an $8 realized checkout price, a refund rate, taxes, commercial shares, a total $2,268 cash burden and 500 hours at $20/hour. It obtains 1,000 cash-cost-covering or 5,410 labor-inclusive gross orders. Those are **arithmetic consequences of chosen numbers**, not a likelihood of making money. Different prices, taxes, publisher agreements, time invested or distribution bundles would shift the threshold materially.

## Five practical decision gates, without commercial action

**1. Confirm publication eligibility.** Verify identity/bank/tax eligibility, current legal terms, Steam Direct fee, available release controls, Coming Soon date, reviewed store page and working build. An official guide does not guarantee a specific account's release privileges.

**2. Budget cash and labor separately.** Require dated, traceable expense and effort assumptions. Model refund/tax, regional discounts and a conservative publisher share; do not treat the historical 2018 additional revenue-share tiers as a universal flat agreement.

**3. Demand signals are not customers.** Store impressions, wishlist counts, a creator's offered review copy, Playtest signups and UTM attributed events all require different denominators. A high-wishlist study that filters out games with few reviews cannot validate a small game's forecast.

**4. Put a downside limit on the experiment.** Define maximum affordable cash loss, time cap, minimum acceptable validated paid orders, customer-support obligations and a no-go rule **before** committing significant resources. Do not budget from a top-hit mean when the typical game underperforms it.

**5. Keep empirical claims behind an evidence gate.** Any marketwide income median, actual zero-selling share or publisher profit requires a complete originally-paid AppID sampling frame, historical delisting and transfers, consenting reconciled merchants across unsuccessful and successful publishers, valid response weights, and true legal publisher identities. The [consented ledger coverage protocol](permissioned-ledger-coverage-and-zero-identification-2026.md) enforces the no-missing-equals-zero rule.

## Explicitly unresolved findings

No representative after-cost developer earnings, exact-zero-sale share, legal partner take-home distribution, universal marketing return, baseline salary-equivalent hours, or reliable probability of break-even has been measured. Developer-authored public financial claims were reviewed, but **no independently authenticated publisher statement, private customer data, merchant API key, ad experiment or new game release** was used. Public self-reports are not audited financial data.

**Research conclusion:** the official publishing steps are substantially documented; the decisive economic outcomes still require genuine eligible seller data. It is preferable to leave these unknown than to turn model estimates or hypothetical budgets into misleading claims of profitability.
