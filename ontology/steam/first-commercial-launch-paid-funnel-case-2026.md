# First commercial Steam game: launch sales, refunds and ad funnel

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Machine-readable case](first-commercial-launch-paid-funnel-case-2026.json) · [auditable descriptive arithmetic](../../site/steam-firstlaunch-funnel-audit.mjs) · [tests](../../tests/steam-firstlaunch-funnel-audit.test.mjs). This is **one self-selected, unaudited developer case**, not a first-game sales distribution.

## Observed launch windows

The developer of [Only Way is Down](https://onlywayisdown.com/post-mortem/) reports launching on **April 1, 2025**. The first-person retrospective is not clearly dated; it describes the game about ten months after launch. The author reports **1,248 copies and $8,232 gross on launch day**, **3,091 copies and $19,851 gross in week one**, and **4,810 copies and $35,862 gross in month one**. The post's label for **about $41,000 later net revenue after the reported Steam cut** is not a remittance record, and cannot be aligned directly with the gross first-month figures without a monthly payment statement.

The source's cumulative windows imply about **40.4%** of reported first-week units occurred on launch day and **64.3%** of first-month units during week one. Similarly, reported week-one gross was **55.4%** of first-month gross. These are descriptive single-game fractions, not an industry launch curve. Because copy reporting may treat refunds differently by period, no cohort-adjusted lifetime refund trajectory is inferred.

The author additionally reports **8,656 retained copies and 1,712 returns**. Under the specific assumption that these form disjoint units of a single accumulated purchase population, **1,712 / (8,656 + 1,712) ≈ 16.5%** were returned. This is not the same as Steam's platform-wide return rate or a probability for an eligible independent first release.

## Paid marketing: cost per tracked wishlist is not cost per incremental sale

At launch the developer reports **17,821 wishlists**, rising to **33,044** at a later snapshot. They report a **$700 Reddit ad campaign, approximately 12,000 clicks and 237 wishlists** associated with that channel. This reconstructs a roughly **$0.058 per reported click**, **$2.95 per reported wishlist**, and **1.98% clicked-to-wishlist ratio**. These are descriptive, author-attributed funnel quantities, not independent validations of unique users or causal conversion rates.

The author reports roughly **$4,000** spent on a creator-marketing platform and **$5,000** total advertising, plus about **4,100** wishlists associated with a June 2024 demo/Next Fest period. Video view counts, complimentary key requests, wishlists, store traffic and game purchases are different denominators. The post explicitly describes simultaneous organic exposure, storefront promotion and creator attention; there is no holdout by which to isolate incremental paid orders produced by one channel.

The retrospective mentions a **7.2% wishlist conversion rate** and a claimed comparison average of **15.3%**. Neither is converted here into the game's units sold divided by total wishlists: the live wishlist stock includes later additions and the author does not provide the conversion numerator, denominator eligibility or period-matched purchaser cohort. The comparison average is likewise not independently accepted as a Steam benchmark.

## Cash and profit boundaries

The author reports **about $41,000 as net after an assumed platform share**, not a verified deposit or after-tax personal income. The approximate **$5,000** advertising budget is only one cost category. Subtracting those two numbers does **not** measure net game profit: labor, development tools, legal/tax liabilities, banking, QA, localization and update/support costs remain unreported. Refund percentages and regionally different sale prices further undermine a single universal net-per-unit conversion.

The game reportedly received 113 reviews. Dividing total units by reviews would merely give a ratio for **this** self-reported case, not a representative sales-per-review estimator. More broadly, this postmortem reflects a publicly described moderately selling title and cannot support a Steam-wide first-game median, exact zero-sales rate or causal marketing return. Only authorized comparable financial ledgers and a release cohort including failures would resolve those targets.
