# Steam legal publisher identities, collaborative bundles and free game licensing

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured findings](legal-partners-bundle-payouts-and-free-licenses-2026.json). Public Steamworks documentation, not legal advice or verified developer income.

## Legal counterparty differs from public studio credits

[Valve's onboarding FAQ](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/faq?language=english) and [onboarding overview](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding) require the game rights holder or authorized publisher to enter their **legal** name, sign Steam's distribution agreement and supply matching banking and valid tax information before releasing. A sole proprietor can onboard without first forming a corporation. The legal payee may therefore be an individual whose storefront developer label is completely different. Partnership tax conditions and sanctions screenings vary by entity and jurisdiction. The public sources reviewed here **do not establish one universal minimum legal age** across all possible counterparties.

This means the units *storefront developer name*, *legal rights holder*, *Steamworks partner account*, *developer employee* and *financial payee* cannot be interchanged when computing income.

## Co-developer access does not transfer cash

[Application Management Sharing](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/managing_apps/sharing) lets studios working together access a shared game AppID without merging Steamworks partner organizations. **Sharing does not change payment rights.** The primary payee can optionally grant finance visibility, subject to separate user permissions; the payment-entitled partner retains pricing and discount controls. It can revoke the access. Neither having editing rights nor being credited on a store page guarantees direct payment from Valve or a specified private royalty split.

## Cross-publisher bundles settle by purchased package

Valve's [collaborative bundles guide](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/bundles/collaborative) says Steam sells the individual packages within a bundle at the discounted prices and pays **each package's partner**. If a customer already owns Game A and completes a bundle by buying Game B, A is *not sold again*, even if the bundle advertises both A and B. Participating partners approve inclusion and discount changes. Steam does not support editing those package-level payout splits through the bundle tool.

A bundle **checkout**, a charged **package unit**, a licensed **AppID**, a unique **customer**, and a developer's **bank proceeds** are distinct denominators. Counting every game displayed in a purchased bundle as a paid unit inflates sales.

## Free licenses and monetized extras are distinct again

Under Valve's [Free To Play guidance](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/freetoplay), the base game is a **free license**, not a paid package purchase. Free and paid games can sell DLC or microtransactions using Steam Wallet; developers can also change a game from paid to free. None of those later events converts an initially free product into an original-paid-release cohort member.

## What remains unknown

The research establishes official role and package-level rules, but **not** actual co-development contracts, payee identities, unique legal publisher counts, paid transactions, royalty allocations, tax liabilities, or publisher profit. A source-backed public methodology cannot automatically substitute for permissioned financial records.
