# Steam capsule artwork and store-page review: compliance before conversion claims

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured findings](capsule-store-page-compliance-and-review-2026.json). Rules and launch-gate documentation, **not** a visual A/B experiment or measured earnings evidence.

## Standard artwork is deliberately constrained

Valve's [Graphical Asset Rules](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/assets/rules?l=english) restrict ordinary store capsule imagery to product artwork, the game's readable name and an official subtitle. Standard capsules must **not** contain review scores, award logos, discount messaging, generic promotional copy or cross-promotion for other products. Library capsules, heroes and logos have their own constraints; Library hero backgrounds may not include words. Capsule artwork must meet Valve's PG-13 presentation guidance, independent of the age rating attached to the actual game.

If a developer wants to advertise a major patch or seasonal event **inside the capsule image**, [Artwork Overrides](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/assets/overrides?l=english) offer a temporary route. These overrides may last **up to one month**, must describe new or seasonal game content and must localize additional text into the languages supported by the title. They are not a way around the permanent standard-capsule restrictions. Valve warns that violations can reduce discovery eligibility or exclude a game from official events. This is **a documented enforcement condition**, not a measured chance of reduced sales.

## Screenshots and advertised features must describe the real build

[Steam's Store Review Process](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/review_process?l=english) requires **actual gameplay screenshots**, not concept art or copy-heavy advertisements. Features depicted or described must be available at release, or clearly identified as future plans. Valve reviews the store checklist before the build, and [Coming Soon](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/coming_soon) public visibility is required for at least **two weeks** before a new product may release. Store review is separate from actual game-build acceptance.

A product can therefore comply with correct graphics and timing yet sell poorly. Valve does not publish a representative change in paid sales resulting from using a particular color, subtitle, logo design, or capsule layout.

## A bounded future experiment

A credible experiment would test high-quality compliant capsule variants on equally eligible games or controlled traffic exposures, preserve genre, price, release age and source channel, report unique eligible impressions and qualified visits, and observe **actual refund-adjusted merchant paid orders** before asserting revenue lift. Observational popular-vs-unpopular capsule comparisons would otherwise conflate creative design with marketing spend, genre, brand recognition and review count.

**No images generated or analyzed, no store page reviewed for submission, no game submitted, and no real sales or marketing outcomes measured.** The practical value here is a preflight compliance checklist and a clear boundary against unearned sales claims.
