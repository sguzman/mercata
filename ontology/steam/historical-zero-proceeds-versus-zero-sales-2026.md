# Steam zero labels: no developer receipts does not mean no game sales

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured historical casebook](historical-zero-proceeds-versus-zero-sales-2026.json) and [evidence test](../../tests/steam-historical-zero-proceeds-cases.test.mjs). These public anecdotes are deliberately selected; neither describes an unbiased sample or audited seller ledger.

## Historical 2018 game: sold copies, zero developer proceeds at snapshot

In a [March 30, 2018 first-party postmortem](https://johnnemann.medium.com/where-the-water-tastes-like-wine-postmortem-211a1f9d791a) for *Where the Water Tastes Like Wine*, the developer says copies were sold in the first few weeks but **does not disclose the exact count**. Around a month after its February 28, 2018 release, the author also says they had **earned $0 personally** from the game and spent approximately **$140,000 paying contractors and collaborators** during nearly four years of development.

The game was supported and financed in part by an outside publishing partner. The developer's personal revenue-share proceeds, the publisher's Steam customer revenue and the collaborators' production payments are therefore **not the same accounting unit**. The $0 figure is not proof of zero Steam purchases. The $140,000 is a historical author-reported major cash outlay, but not a complete set of all costs, taxes and payee allocations. It would be incorrect to compute a verified $140,000 profit loss from those two reported amounts without knowing the entity, financing arrangements and complete expense base.

The original author also distinguishes an artistic achievement from disappointing early commercial performance and planned additional updates. This is a short post-release snapshot, **not a lifetime outcome**. Nor is a 2018 creative and publisher-funded game an estimator of an ordinary 2026 first-time solo publisher's costs.

## A 2025 launch: seven family purchases versus unverified wishlist attribution

A [March 8, 2025 developer account](https://www.reddit.com/r/GameDevelopment/comments/1j662w4/nobody_who_wishlist_my_game_bought_it/) reports about **150 launch wishlists**, **seven paid game copies** described as purchases by friends and family, and around **$10 earned**. The writer says no outside wishlisters purchased it, and mentions about **300 hours** of work and a **$100 Steam Direct fee**.

These are unverified, self-reported values. In particular, no buyer-level or wishlist-cohort financial export has been checked. Dividing seven reported purchases by 150 wishlist accounts cannot reliably reproduce the author's claimed outside-wishlist conversion, because purchase timing, account matching, gifts and eligible cohorts are unknown. Their early cash shortfall after the fee and heavy uncompensated labor cannot be extrapolated into a final tax profit or lifelong developer earnings.

## Distinct zeros that must remain separate

- **Zero paid game orders:** requires a fully observed original paid-game AppID and sales window, including transfers and retired listings.
- **Zero developer proceeds:** may arise even when a publisher has sold copies, depending on rights, costs, time and what the author calls 'earned'.
- **Zero purchase conversion among wishlisters:** requires independently joined and consented buyer cohorts, rather than subtracting two dashboard stock counts.
- **Zero after-cost profit:** requires the complete publisher net income, costs, labor valuation and period.

These two sources show why claiming an exact Steam-wide zero-sale fraction from publicity, reviews, postmortems or empty personal payout figures would be unsound. Real population-level publisher profits remain unknown.
