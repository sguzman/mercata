# Steam release catalog counts and success-conditioned long tails

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured findings](public-catalog-and-conditional-long-tail-2026.json). Public independent counts are useful for **market supply**, but they cannot be turned into verified buyer counts, paid seller averages or exact-zero sale rates.

## Catalog snapshots: different tracker populations

The independent [SteamDB release tracker](https://steamdb.info/stats/releases/) displayed **21,224** games for release year 2025 in an opened page on October 28. A contemporaneous search result for that same page showed **21,228**, consistent with an actively changing catalog or retrieval-time discrepancy, not proof of any particular delisting event.

The [steamlense statistics page](https://steamlense.com/stats), last recalculated October 27, placed **17,651 in paid-status** and **2,985 in free-status** categories for 2025, **20,636 total** under its rules. These are separate tracker populations; their difference cannot be attributed to a known missing subset without comparing underlying AppIDs. Current paid status may not equal positive minimum price at first release.

Steamlense also reports **61% of its 2025 releases had fewer than ten reviews**. That is **not** 61% unsold, zero-revenue or zero-player games. Review authors and paid checkouts are different units, and free games are in the denominator.

## Long-tail evidence that excludes unsuccessful games

The [GameDiscoverCo analysis (July 21, 2026)](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-long-tail-should-you-expect) selected approximately **5,000 titles** released from July 2023 to July 2025 that had **more than 1,000 estimated copies sold in month one**. Its modeled median cumulative unit multiplier relative to month-one units was **1.46× by month six**, **1.79× by year one**, and **2.27× by year two**. Relative to first-week units, its median was **2.59× by year one**. The corresponding first-week-to-year-one **gross revenue** multiplier was **2.46×**, not the same as copies.

These are medians of success-conditioned model estimates, not a random sample of every developer. They cannot estimate how often a new Steam game sells zero, whether a first-game publisher breaks even, or how much the median publisher receives after VAT, Steam's contractual share, refunds, withholding and production costs.

## Why this matters for Mercata

Comparing release supply and long-tail economics requires an eligible, deduplicated paid launch cohort including unpopular, delisted and zero-order games. We also need identical valuation horizons, revenue definitions, original paid status and seller grouping. **No true platformwide paid-game mean, median, zero-sales fraction, unique buyers or creator profits have been measured.** The independent researchers' underlying per-game estimates were not downloaded or republished.
