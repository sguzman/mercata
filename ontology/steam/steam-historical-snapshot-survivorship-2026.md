# Steam historical storefront snapshots: denominator survival and contradiction rules

**Reviewed October 29, 2026.** [Machine-readable audit](steam-historical-snapshot-survivorship-2026.json) · [pure snapshot reconciler](../../site/steam-snapshot-survivorship-guards.mjs) · [regression tests](../../tests/steam-snapshot-survivorship-guards.test.mjs). Every row used by the helper is **invented**. This study neither requests a Steam API key nor downloads a real historical game catalog.

## Why a perfectly paginated current catalog is still not a launch census

Valve's [IStoreService/GetAppList](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/webapi/IStoreService?l=english) can page current Steam Store apps with a general Web API key, with up to 50,000 per page, continuation by last AppID, and a `last_modified` timestamp. The timestamp records changes to app information or prices; it is **not the initial paid launch date**. Valve [deprecates the older ISteamApps/GetAppList](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/webapi/ISteamApps) as not scaling to the current catalog. Changing API routes therefore does not magically restore historical delistings or original price status.

Valve's [release-date guide](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/release_dates?language=english) separates the intended Steamworks date from the player-facing scheduled display, which can be just a year, month, quarter, or Coming Soon. None establishes the date when a player first could actually pay and play. [Retirement guidance](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/retire_app?l=english) says retired games remain in owners' libraries and on accessible store pages but leave store listings. The absence of an AppID from a later catalog is not **proof** of retirement: filtering, partial pagination, availability or data errors may also explain it.

Valve [describes app transfers](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/managing_apps/transfer?l=english) that preserve AppIDs while splitting default historical financial-report visibility by partner payee dates. A game roster alone never establishes the distinct legal publishers or their receipts.

## A conservative synthetic reconciliation

The helper keeps a union of **synthetic fixture IDs**, rather than silently dropping a title missing from the latest list. It rejects duplicate snapshot IDs, duplicate game rows, invalid calendar dates, mixed region/type filters and real-looking AppIDs. Different source roles are explicit: `current_store_list` provides **presence and metadata only**; `first_release_attestation` provides an independently established first publicly paid playable date, original paid status, and product type. The module cannot verify an attestation's external provenance; a real study must audit that before using it.

A repeated identical first-offer attestation counts once. Contradictory first-offer assertions are **unresolved**, not majority-voted or resolved by later metadata. A missing original price, date or launch mode also remains unresolved. An initially free game is not retroactively classified as a paid first-release game because it is paid today. A paid Early Access title's later version 1.0 is not a second first-paid launch.

For invented examples, the earliest catalog contains games A and B; the later list contains B and C. Two independently fabricated initial-offer attestations establish A and C as eligible in 2025. A is **absent from the later complete catalog**, but **retirement is still unverified**. B has no initial-offer evidence and remains unresolved. Thus *two known eligible among three observed fixtures* is a test of the method, **not** a Steam 2025 release count or a market percentage.

A latest snapshot is used for absence comparisons only when its operator-declared pagination is complete and filter scope matches prior snapshots. Because the current fixture records only calendar dates, **two catalog captures on the same day cannot be reliably sequenced**. Their IDs are not timestamps: a fabricated lexicographic tie-break could otherwise create an artificial disappearance. The reconciler now rejects that ambiguous case rather than selecting a false "latest" catalog. It also rejects unexpected fields on snapshots, rows, and the cohort-window selector so extraneous or private-looking properties never silently pass as part of a published observation. Even then it is merely a snapshot of an **asserted** list, not verified whole-market completeness. The module returns `null` for missing-market counts, zero-sale fractions, paid order medians and publisher profits.

## External evidence needed to measure the real market

A rights-cleared versioned AppID archive, reproducible original-purchase and initial playable-release evidence, genuinely complete aligned geographic/product filters, a documented treatment of withdrawn or restricted apps, independent validation of archived errors and permissioned payment reports. Any percentage derived from a current-only list must report its denominator as **currently observed listings**, not all historically first-paid games.

This complements [the first-paid-release protocol](fixed-paid-release-cohort-2026.md) and [the consented zero-sales ledger gate](permissioned-ledger-coverage-and-zero-identification-2026.md). It improves the observation instrument; it **does not identify an empirical survivor fraction or zero-paid-sale rate**.


## Public historical archive source-selection audit — October 30, 2026

The [synthetic archive candidate-coverage guard](../../site/steam-archive-candidate-coverage.mjs) and [tests](../../tests/steam-archive-candidate-coverage.test.mjs) distinguish *observed historical presence*, *recorded nonzero price*, *observed playability*, *third-party curated removal status*, and an **independent first-paid-playable-release attestation**. These evidence types cannot be substituted for one another.

The following public-source comparison was reviewed October 30, 2026; the external websites are dynamic. It describes **source capabilities and exclusions, not a downloaded AppID census or a validated probability sample**.

| Archive or provider | What its public description supports | Historical-frame exclusion or caveat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Valve IStoreService](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/webapi/IStoreService?l=english) | Paginated list of currently available Store apps, with modified-since querying | Even a complete current snapshot is not a historical first-paid-launch frame, and `last_modified` is not first paid release |
| [SteamDB price history](https://steamdb.info/pricechanges/) | Recorded price-change histories across Steam currencies; its page states some tracking extends to 2012 | A dated price is a price observation, not a paid checkout or validated first-paid playable launch; history and accessible detail vary by AppID, date and currency |
| [SteamRaw about / methodology](https://steamraw.com/about) | Independent PICS app-info history and selected price/review/player tracking; project states it began September 2026 | Pre-September 2026 data may be missing or imported, and observed current metadata cannot backfill older first-paid launch events |
| [Public removed and banned Steam games catalogue](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1aAbrEDNa2NmgntrKtij0RuxKneiFcTJisGyfMUv2nnM/edit?gid=975765620) | A curated list focused on removed, retired, rejected, unpublished and unreleased content, with source-defined statuses and individual corroborating links | Unreleased/unpublished AppIDs were not necessarily paid releases. Curated subject matter, discoverability and post-2018 collection bias prevent extrapolating its coverage to all Steam releases |
| [Steam Port Index](https://steam-port-index.com/about.html) | Hand-checked historical release relationships for a selected classic-game/port collection; it says delisted games remain in its catalog | Not a representative sample of all original Steam games, nor evidence of the game's initial paid checkout status |

**One inspectable current-list discrepancy.** The public removed-games catalogue's October 30 view said **3,924 catalogued entries**, with displayed category counts **Removed 1,219; Unreleased 703; Delisted 729; Unpublished 1,249**. Those four displayed values sum to **3,900**, leaving **24 entries not accounted for by the four presented counts**. This is a snapshot consistency observation, *not* evidence that 24 paid games are missing or even that the source contains a coding error; the provider has not independently asserted an exhaustive, non-overlapping categorical partition for the reported total. Both headline and category values are mutable and must not be combined with later captures as though contemporaneous. The sheet also notes that it focuses on adult/visual-novel/anime content and that newly unpublished AppIDs became harder to discover after November 2025. No individual sheet entries or sensitive topics are collected in Mercata.

The synthetic model receives only fabricated game and source IDs, explicit source tracking-start dates, observation dates, archive roles, and whether an older record was natively observed or imported. **An archived price following launch cannot establish the initial public paid launch date.** A curated `unreleased` or `unpublished` label cannot be accepted as a confirmed no-sale paid game. Its **observation date matters**: a label observed before an independently attested first-paid playable date is consistent with a legitimate later launch and must not veto eligibility. A label observed on the same day or afterward remains unresolved because day-only captures cannot establish a safe ordering or resolve a genuinely conflicting status. A first-paid-playable event also cannot be attested as already observed before its claimed event date. Duplicate evidence and contradictory first-launch attestations do not create votes. The resulting invented candidate union is only a **lower bound on observed candidate IDs within the input fixture**, never an exact market denominator, an actual count of omitted paid games, a paid-order statistic or an estimated zero-sale fraction.

The project did not download a historical Steam AppID roster, scrape these archives, submit credentials, import protected data or obtain merchant financial evidence. Source descriptions may change; a reproducible empirical census would need versioned, rights-cleared observations, provider coverage histories, a paid playable date audit, and independent checks for never-observed or retired paid releases.
