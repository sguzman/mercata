# Steam languages: storefront discoverability, game localization and non-observed ROI

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured policy record](language-localization-and-discoverability-2026.json). This is official documentation research, not a measured result from translated game launches or paid customer purchases.

## Which language does Steam actually support?

Valve's [localization guidance](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/localization?language=english) distinguishes translating the **store listing** (description, captions, images, trailer metadata and product name) from translating **in-game text, subtitles or full audio**. A game can offer one without the other, although Valve advises doing both where feasible. Steam's support documentation describes **29 fully supported platform languages** and says **more than 60% of Steam users use a language other than English**. This is a broad platform-language claim, **not** the share of Steam paid orders placed by non-English customers, a geographic census or a verified ROI for translating an individual game.

[Valve's language levels](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/localization/languages?language=english) separate **Full Platform Support** (Steam UI, store, community and Steamworks APIs) from **Game Support Only** languages developers can declare for the game. Game-only options are searchable as game-language filters since **October 2022**, but do not imply that the full Steam client or APIs support those languages in the same way.

## Discovery and development effects

Steam says localized game names appear to customers who use the respective language and affect store **search suggestions**, while the original name remains a fallback. That supplies a concrete product discoverability mechanism, not a measured sales increase or guarantee of high search placement.

For small translated assets, a game may use a single Steam depot with language switching; larger per-language payloads can use distinct depots. Translation decisions can also increase QA (font legibility, UI overflow, right-to-left support where relevant, subtitle timing, patch packaging and localization testing). These are *potential* effort factors, not measured prices or hours.

Valve suggests announcing added languages in an update and using language-aware store visits and wishlist interest to prioritize translation investment. These recommendations do not establish the causal value of any new locale.

## Country is not language, and attention is not checkout

An account using Spanish might be located in different countries; a country may contain customers choosing different client and game languages. None of these labels proves nationality, legal jurisdiction, purchaser identity, sales tax or the presence of a transaction. For localization cost/return, a defensible cohort would record verified originally-paid titles, time-specific storefront and in-game locale availability, actual costs, source-controlled qualified visits, checkout and refunds, and equivalent launch age.

**Still unknown:** incremental orders caused by translated pages, true per-language purchasing cohorts, localization net returns, quality-control labor and actual profit for small Steam studios. No account analytics, language tracking, customer records or paid campaigns were accessed.
