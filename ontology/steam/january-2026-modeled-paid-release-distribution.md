# January 2026 Steam paid releases: a full-month modeled distribution

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Machine-readable findings](january-2026-modeled-paid-release-distribution.json). This is the first Mercata Steam distribution checkpoint using a *reported complete release month* rather than only successful publishers. All units and dollars below are **third-party model estimates, not Valve-verified orders or developer profit**.

## Primary evidence and population

On [September 15, 2026](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/is-the-game-biz-sustainable-for-the), GameDiscoverCo's Simon Carless publicly discussed the **1,230 paid games released in January 2026** and published [the source model spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1x8cDd5yXC88zmEBXESxjf4n2AJbVASj1jKVJkerbEmE/edit). The view-only sheet contains game links, names, base prices, modeled lifetime-to-date gross and unit sales, review scores, dates, developer and publisher display labels, and tags. Mercata inspected the accessible public page and article but **did not download, republish or independently sum the spreadsheet's individual rows**.

The observation horizon reaches approximately **mid-September 2026**, roughly seven-and-a-half to eight months after release. Each game's age differs slightly by January launch date. GameDiscoverCo labels the month cohort complete; the first-paid-release and delisting coverage have **not been independently checked** against an archival AppID universe. This is a **game** denominator, not unique publishers, solo developers or all 2026 launches. Some are large-publisher and franchise titles.

## Modeled gross revenue distribution

The public spreadsheet has been independently *analyzed* by [Grishakov (October 22)](https://grishakof.com/blog/en/guides/how-much-do-indie-games-make-on-steam/), who reports these counts. Both this reanalysis and GameDiscoverCo's original article explicitly support the 16 games at $1m+.

| Estimated lifetime-to-date gross sales | Modeled games | Share of 1,230 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| At least USD $1,000 | 619 | 50.3% |
| At least USD $10,000 | 248 | 20.2% |
| At least USD $100,000 | 88 | 7.2% |
| At least USD $1,000,000 | 16 | 1.3% |

These are **nested thresholds**, not distinct groups. The total 619 is not 619 profitable games, because we have no per-game returns, actual sales, taxes, production costs or payout splits.

The [Egmatic reanalysis (October 15)](https://egmatic.com/blog/how-many-copies-does-the-median-indie-game-sell) reports a **median near 100 modeled copies and $1,000 modeled gross**, while Grishakov reports an average of about **$108,000 gross**. Egmatic rounds the mean near $110,000. Approximate reporting and model/aggregation choices differ, but all derive from the **same input model**, not two independent transaction panels.

A skewed distribution explains the mismatch between typical and average games: Egmatic finds the **top 1% capture 72.8%** of the modeled revenue; Grishakov finds **16 of 1,230 games capture about $102m of $133m (77%)**. These are *different numerical groups*: strictly the top 1% is about 12 or 13 titles, while 16 titles represent about 1.3%. Do not label either figure an independently verified share of Steam's actual payouts.

## The crucial low end: modeled zero is not true zero

Egmatic reports **412 games with modeled zero copies out of 1,211 games that had copy estimates**. The public cohort contains **1,230 games**, leaving **19 without reported estimates under that specific calculation**. This yields about **34.0% modeled zeros among games with estimates**, **not** 412/1,230 verified zero-sale publishers or a legitimate exact-zero cash outcome.

A model can round an uncertain low sales count to zero even if a real sale happened. Conversely, a listed game can sell externally distributed keys or earn outside the measured Steam-store checkout. The same analysis describes approximately 74.4% of the cohort below 1,000 estimated copies. The raw sales ledger and complete original-paid eligibility status are not public.

## Hypothetical business viability is not observed profitability

GameDiscoverCo separately models business cases: roughly **80/1,230** would have covered a hypothetical self-funded $100k budget; **16/1,230** grossed more than $1m and could potentially cover an assumed $500k self-funded budget under Carless's payout assumptions; about **7/1,230** would recoup a publisher-funded $500k project under assumed agreement terms. None of these are observed development budgets or individual signed royalty contracts. A positive gross-revenue threshold does not establish an actual profitable company.

## What this resolves, and what remains blocked

This is substantially better evidence about Steam's *modeled low-end full launch cohort* than success-only surveys. It establishes a **bounded, dated publisher-model population** and strongly skewed outcomes within it. However, it does **not** establish population-wide paid orders, a verified zero-sale rate, a representative full-year game median, legally deduplicated publisher incomes, developer profit or an exact launch-era sale percentage.

The next independent checks are: (1) validate historical AppID and delisting completeness, (2) recalculate aggregate totals on a rights-cleared snapshot when permitted, (3) replicate across multiple release months and fixed-age horizons, and (4) compare subsets to consented merchant aggregate statements including unsuccessful titles. **The source sheet was not copied into Mercata**, and this report should not be read as an audit of its proprietary estimates.
