# A public early-launch self-report with an unknown fourteen-day cutoff

**Reviewed October 29, 2026.** [Evidence fixture](steam-monstergirl-first-window-2026.json) · [executable audit](../../site/steam-public-launch-window-guards.mjs) · [tests](../../tests/steam-public-launch-window-guards.test.mjs).

## What was reported

The developer of *Monster Girl Therapy* [posted a March 10, 2026 release announcement and an April 6 retrospective](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4070080/allnews/). The retrospective reports **just under 300 sold units**, **14 refunds** and **approximately USD 1,500 before Steam's platform share**. These are rounded author disclosures, not verified Steamworks merchant records, net bank receipts or profit after taxes and development labor.

The author also reports approximately **3,000 prelaunch wishlistings**, **4,500 total wishlistings** and a described **5% conversion** figure. Wishlist stocks/additions, matched buyer cohorts and the conversion numerator are not specified well enough to derive an independently validated rate from those figures.

## Publication date versus economic observation window

The author calls the account an opening **two-week** report. But it was published **27 calendar days** after the March 10 release and gives no explicit sales-data cutoff. A later publication could still describe a correctly filtered two-week dashboard period; it cannot prove that period was used. The [audit](../../site/steam-public-launch-window-guards.mjs) retains both dates while leaving the exact 14-day sales figure unknown.

An approximate units total cannot support an exact refund rate. One voluntarily disclosed game does not establish representative Steam game earnings, publisher cash, the zero-sales population, or the causal power of wishlists. The original paid-game population frame and legitimate merchant transaction evidence remain necessary.
