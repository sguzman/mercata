# Lost in Art: high prelaunch wishlist stock, low early paid-unit disclosure

**Reviewed October 30, 2026.** [Structured data](steam-lostinart-early-launch-wishlist-window-2026.json) · [regression checks](../../tests/steam-lostinart-early-launch-wishlist-window.test.mjs).

## Identity, horizon and provenance

The [official Steam listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2900050/) identifies *Lost in Art: a Miniature Realm* (AppID **2900050**) as a self-published point-and-click/puzzle adventure by Archaic Game Studio, with a **June 1, 2026** commercial release. That listing is independent verification of the published release metadata, **not** of copies sold.

In a [June 3, 2026 public developer thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/gamedev/comments/1tv9erh/), the first-game development team described approximately **14,000 outstanding wishlists when the title launched** and **187 copies sold so far**. Crucially, a same-author comment places the discussion about **one and a half days after launch**. Calling 187 the first seven days or a complete first 24-hour number would misstate the observation window. The author speculated that approximately **10–15** of those 187 units came from acquaintances; those buyers were not independently identified.

| Published metric | Reported amount | Identification limit |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Prelaunch outstanding wishlists | ~14,000 | Wishlist *stock*, not a matched purchase cohort |
| Copies sold at the early June 3 snapshot | 187 | Unverified author total, around 1.5 days post-launch |
| Simple copies/wishlist-stock quotient | ~1.34% | **Not** a wishlist purchase-conversion rate |
| Possible sales from friends/acquaintances | ~10–15 | Author's estimate only; not independently checked |
| Week-one and month-one paid units | Unknown | No same-author audited fixed-horizon report in this selected source |
| Gross, platform payout and after-cost return | Unknown | No authenticated Steamworks ledger or team labor accounting |

**The 1.34% quotient has incompatible denominators.** The 187 cumulative paid copies need not be attributable to people who wishlisted before launch; the 14,000 launch stock may include users who did not receive a notification or intend to purchase immediately. Neither a low mechanical quotient nor a forum participant's purported typical conversion constitutes a cohort-level market rate or evidence of the causal effectiveness of the Steam page.

## Age of wishlists, demo and outreach

The developer's public comment reported that the store page had been available for approximately **two years**, with **9,000** wishlist additions over the preceding year, including **5,000** over the preceding three months. These time periods overlap; the 5,000 must **not** be summed again with the 9,000. The team described having an older free demo, refreshed for **February 2026 Next Fest**, and usually seeing roughly **300–500** wishlist adds per festival. These event additions are not measured causal effects or matched eventual purchasers.

The author also described contacting approximately **50–60** potentially relevant streamers, getting one positive response but no confirmed publication at the time of the comment. A positive reply, requested key, demo exposure or festival wishlist is not a paid unit. Other commenters hypothesized that game presentation might be mistaken for a city builder because of miniature-art imagery; the author said they had not anticipated that interpretation. This remains **qualitative storefront feedback**, not an experimental explanation of poor early orders.

The project was described as involving **two people over three elapsed years**, of which nearly **two years** were close to full-time development. Person-hours, opportunity costs, cash expenditures, and personal pay were **not** disclosed. The report is not evidence that 14,000 prelaunch wishlists typically yield 187 purchases, nor of the studio's net income.

## Independence and use

Both the post and its author's replies describe one deliberately selected title; the replies are **not new independent observations**. The public Steam listing confirms product identity and date but cannot independently audit the sales report. The case is a **single early snapshot** and must not be pooled with full-month developer postmortems as if the observation windows were fixed and equal.

This study uses public aggregate disclosures only. No account-level buyers, contacts, private finance, or publisher logs were obtained.
