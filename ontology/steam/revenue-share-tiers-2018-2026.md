# Steam's historical revenue-share tiers and their limits

**Review: October 28, 2026.** [Machine model](revenue-share-tiers-2018-2026.json). Valve's [2018 announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/steamworks/announcements/detail/1697191267930157838), independently corroborated by [contemporaneous reporting](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2018/12/steam-now-gives-its-biggest-developers-a-bigger-cut-of-the-revenue/), described these **marginal adjusted-revenue tiers**, with qualifying revenue starting October 1, 2018:

| Eligible adjusted revenue | Publisher share | Valve share |
|---|---:|---:|
| First USD 10 million | 70% | 30% |
| Above USD 10 million to USD 50 million | 75% | 25% |
| Beyond USD 50 million | 80% | 20% |

Higher tiers apply **only to the incremental amount beyond each threshold**. For example, an imaginary game with USD 12 million in qualifying adjusted revenue would receive USD 7 million on the first USD 10 million plus USD 1.5 million on the next USD 2 million, or **USD 8.5 million** before further withholding, splits and production costs. This is **not a real developer's revenue or profit**.

The original 2018 announcement includes game packages, downloadable content, in-game sales and applicable Community Market game fees. The live [Valve financial FAQ](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/payments_salesreporting/faq) confirms a form of additional-revenue-share accounting and attributes DLC-only packages to parent games, while mixed bundles can require manual attribution. The [tax FAQ](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/taxfaq) describes adjustments such as indirect taxes, returns and chargebacks before contractual revenue share.

**Scope and uncertainty:** These are **historically published standard tiers**, not independently verified signed 2026 contractual rates for every publisher. The original Steam Community announcement returns little textual content to the public source viewer, so its tier figures are corroborated with dated contemporaneous reporting. The public FAQ does not expose partner-specific contract exceptions. No private Steamworks agreements, receipts, units sold or profit reports were obtained. An announcement threshold, revenue share and the developer's after-labor profit are different quantities.
