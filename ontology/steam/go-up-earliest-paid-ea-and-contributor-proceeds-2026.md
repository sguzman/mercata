# Go Up: paid Early Access, reported revenue, and contributor allocation

**Reviewed October 29, 2026.** [Machine record](go-up-earliest-paid-ea-and-contributor-proceeds-2026.json) · [regression tests](../../tests/steam-go-up-contributor-proceeds-audit.test.mjs).

## One paid launch, two public release dates

The [Steam listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2934520/Go_Up/) dates **Go Up** (AppID 2934520) to **July 11, 2025** for Early Access and **August 31, 2026** for its later full release, with Noble Whale Studios named as developer and publisher. The [developer's August 31 postmortem](https://www.reddit.com/r/gamedev/comments/1w3rzlc/today_we_are_releasing_our_failed_game_a/) and reply say the 135 reported Steam units accumulated during roughly a year in Early Access. Counting August 2026 as the *first* paid launch would misclassify the earlier observation window. The storefront verifies public product dates and labels, **not** sales or legal payee identities.

## Author-reported game amounts versus receipts to people

The post reports **$675 Steam gross** and **$517 Steam net** cumulatively, plus **135 Steam units**, **23 retail units**, **158 total units**, and **20 returns**. The source does not provide an authenticated transaction ledger or resolve whether retail units are paid units, how returned units intersect the counts, or the exact deductions behind the word "net." Do not subtract 20 indiscriminately from the 158 total or treat $517 as deposited cash. [Valve's reporting and payments FAQ](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/payments_salesreporting/faq) distinguishes sales reporting, partner revenue share, and payments.

The author describes approximately **$500** in proceeds that go toward an artist and musician and says the two core developers **likely will not see money** from this title. This distinguishes revenue earned by a game from compensation received by different contributors. It does **not** prove completed contractor transfers, legally recorded publisher income of zero, or an exact zero-dollar return to either developer. The developer later estimated approximately **two years of work by two developers**; no audited hours, full operating expenses, personal taxes, or bank receipts were released.

## Non-sales quantities and limits

The same post lists **278 wishlists**, **231 demo players**, and approximately **2,000 demo downloads**. They are different observational populations, not sales or matched wishlist conversion. The author's speculation about bot downloads is not a verified bot count. The observed self-report is one voluntarily published cumulative case, with strong disclosure selection and a longer Early Access exposure period than a new full-release-only sample.

**Missing:** authenticated paid orders, reconciled returned purchases, publisher bank remittances, contributor payments, labor and overhead costs, after-cost profit, and representative income or zero-paid-sale fractions. The two dates must not be used as two first-paid launches, and anticipated nonpayment to developers must not become a measured zero-sale game.
