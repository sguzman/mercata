# Steam cash labels: received bank money versus return per development month

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured two-case audit](steam-historical-received-net-versus-development-return-2026.json) · [reconciliation checks](../../tests/steam-received-net-vs-development-return.test.mjs). These historical accounts are voluntarily published and not representative of releases in 2026.

## Rainswept (2019): date-matched received cash cannot include estimated April

A [May 15, 2019 developer postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/3-months-ago-i-released-my-first-game-was-it-worth-quitting-my-job-to-go-indie-detailed-earnings-report-) reports **978 Steam** and **271 GOG** copies from February 1 through May 6, 2019, for **1,249 copies** across stores. The author reports Steam gross **$9,736**, GOG gross **$2,512**, and explicitly describes net as money received: **$4,955 Steam** and **$1,487 GOG**, for $6,422 combined. This is stronger labeling than an undefined dashboard net figure, but the bank and partner statements were not independently reviewed.

There is an important reconciliation point. February Steam net was **$3,966** and March **$988**, summing to **$4,954**, within $1 of the claimed $4,955 Steam net. April's **$565** is explicitly an *estimate*, not an additional validated bank transfer. Adding it as settled funds would overstate cash received. The February–April Steam gross figures total **$9,593**, $143 short of the headline $9,736; the headline includes early May dates, and any fuller explanation would require source-period records. These are **observation-window or rounding gaps**, not proof of author accounting error.

Even with author-described receipts, individual living expenses, full production labor, bank timing and project rights were not verified. A creator's reported ability to live cheaply does not translate into a replicable first-game income forecast.

## Starcom: Nexus (2020): developer-month return is not a paycheck

The [developer-authored April 10, 2020 retrospective](https://www.starcomnexus.com/starcom-nexus-postmortem/) reports approximately **24,000 Steam units** and **$400,000 Steam gross** around four months after the full release, **$25,000 external development expenses**, and **30 equivalent full-time development months**. The author describes earning a little over **$7,000 per development month after expenses, before personal income tax**.

That last value is a **self-reported division of an author's chosen residual** by estimated equivalent months of effort. It is not a monthly bank-payroll record and does not itself establish an employer-paid salary, healthcare/benefits, income-tax liability, complete historical opportunity cost or audited economic profit. The developer previously worked on other titles and spent time on Starcom during pauses; this is a selected success case.

The author also saw a title-specific change from roughly **one review per 30 copies** in Early Access toward **one per 40–45 copies** after graduating to full release. Those are not validated platform-wide review-to-sale coefficients.

## Meaning for the income study

The two postmortems answer different questions: Rainswept gives author-described **received money across two stores with unsettled future months**, while Starcom describes **economic output per estimated developer month before income tax**. These cannot be averaged into a representative Steam developer wage, nor can gross sales replace complete legal publisher costs. A population-level finding would require a complete eligible release frame, reconciled partner/bank records and representative labor observations.
