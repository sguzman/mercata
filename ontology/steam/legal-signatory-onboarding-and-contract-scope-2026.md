# Steam contracting parties, personal legal capacity and unavailable contract terms

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured findings](legal-signatory-onboarding-and-contract-scope-2026.json). Based on Valve's [public onboarding guide](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding) and [onboarding FAQ](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/faq?language=english). This is not legal advice or access to any Steamworks contract.

## Who signs, and who receives the money?

Valve requires digital signature of the **Steam Distribution Agreement** and a separate nondisclosure agreement. The registering party must identify the legal entity that owns or has rights to publish the product, with real legal rather than branded/DBA names. Onboarding can occur as a **sole proprietor**; incorporating is not a mandatory universal precondition. Valve requires matching bank-account holder and legal contracting names and tax/identity verification. Its FAQ says valid tax and bank information is required **before the game can release**. Partnerships in some jurisdictions may require additional partner tax documentation.

**A legal payee is not necessarily a storefront publisher display label.** This prevents Mercata from treating an observed studio name as a complete set of one legal company's rights and earnings. The actual partner agreement, company registrations and ownership transfers must be checked separately, with permission, before determining royalty shares or net payouts.

## Contract text and developer age are unresolved, not invented

The publicly accessible onboarding and FAQ materials confirm that the agreement exists and must be signed, but they do **not** establish the exact language of a particular partner's signed distribution license, termination provisions, royalty percentages or appeals process. No agreement was examined; do not claim that signing automatically transfers game IP to Valve.

Likewise, these pages **do not establish a universal signatory age rule** that could replace applicable capacity law. Legal capacity for an individual publisher depends on jurisdiction, the actual agreement and legal authority to sign. This research does **not** infer that any named age is categorically accepted or refused.

## Concrete research gate

A compliant future rights review would require the responsible contracting party's authorized copy of its agreement, legal identification for the entity, the relevant local law at signature date, a documented transfer/royalty chain, and an appropriately permissioned and confidential review process. Public output should retain only aggregate or redacted permitted conclusions, not private contracts or partner identifiers.

**Still unknown:** representative minor-developer eligibility, individual Steam licensing terms, exact partner royalty splits and dispute remedies. No identity records, signatures, tax information or bank accounts were used.
