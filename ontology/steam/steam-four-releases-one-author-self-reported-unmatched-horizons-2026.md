# One creator, four self-reported Steam releases: unlike sales estimates, still not an audited ledger

**Reviewed October 31, 2026.** [Structured case](./steam-four-releases-one-author-self-reported-unmatched-horizons-2026.json) · [regression checks](../../tests/steam-four-releases-one-author-self-reported-unmatched-horizons-2026.test.mjs). A voluntary [February 21, 2026 game developer report](https://www.reddit.com/r/GameDevelopment/comments/1rb3xrj/failed_at_game_developement_a_report_of_a/) describes four earlier releases; no contributor identity, account IDs, transaction exports or raw personal records were imported.

## Cumulative sales numbers as reported by the developer

| Game sequence (source label only) | Reported release year | Copies reported as of February 2026 | Outstanding wishlists reported then |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| First | 2019 | **276** | 360 |
| Second | 2021 | **253** | 375 |
| Third | 2022 | **131** | 267 |
| Fourth | 2023 | **69** | 147 |

The reported cumulative title-copy numbers add to **729**. This means 729 *reported title copies across four games*, **not 729 unique customers**, 729 first-month purchases or bank receipts. There is no independent financial export, partner remittance, title sale-price history or game-specific refund information. The author does not authenticate the first paid-playable date and launch-price status of each product in the linked account.

Importantly, **all four self-reported copy counts are positive**. The source therefore supplies no observed zero-sale game. It adds a low-volume self-report distinct from the [selected launch-day Gamalytic model bins](steam-selected-launch-day-low-signal-panel-audit-2026.md) and from public review-count thresholds.

## Why the printed descending sequence is not a causal industry trend

These are *as-of-post* counts for titles released in **four different years**. A 2019 game had substantially longer to accumulate sales than a 2023 game. Without matched first 30-day or first 365-day windows, title price and discount histories, genre, regional distribution, publicity, and initial launch status, the sequence 276, 253, 131, 69 cannot establish that the market became more difficult in each year or that a developer necessarily performs worse with successive titles. One volunteering creator is not four independently sampled publishers.

The author reports little prelaunch promotion and describes releasing games without a sustained campaign, but that does **not** prove the cause of the observed copy numbers. A 2026 planned sequel reportedly had **10 wishlists after two weeks of store-page availability**; it is a *prelaunch audience stock*, not a fifth title with zero game sales. Nor can 276 reported first-game copies divided by 360 *later* outstanding wishlists, or any similar pair, produce authentic launch-wishlist conversion: the cohorts and dates are not matched.

## Financial and marketwide limits

This is one public author account, not a representative sample, and source authors may self-select toward reporting their experiences. The legal bank payee and whether the games shared one legal publisher are unknown. Without prices, refunds, taxes, partner splits, business costs and labor valuations, none of the four counts identifies creator income or profit. The case cannot establish an exact zero-order fraction, market median or the effects of marketing; those remain **unknown**.

This additional case adds evidence about **measurement and survivorship of repeat-release portfolios**, rather than a fabricated fifth sold title, guaranteed buyer conversion, or a marketwide distribution.
