# Grime&Gold: first paid Steam release, ambiguous net stage and demo referral claims

**Reviewed October 30, 2026.** [Machine-readable study](steam-grimegold-firstgame-net-stage-demo-window-2026.json) · [regression checks](../../tests/steam-grimegold-net-stage-demo-window.test.mjs).

## Commercial release versus the earlier free demo

The [official Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3165090/) identifies the paid game *Grime&Gold* (AppID 3165090), its **January 20, 2026** release, and the three listed developer/publisher labels: Pemdora, Zakku and Spartaqui. An [October 3, 2024 developer publication](https://pemdora.itch.io/grimegold/devlog/809393/grimegold-demo-on-steam-is-available) documents an earlier free/demo development stage. That earlier page or demo is **not** the original paid Steam game launch. The public store confirms product metadata, not any sales amount or merchant statement.

## Voluntary first-game sales and the net-label mismatch

In a [February 15, 2026 developer postmortem](https://www.reddit.com/r/gamedev/comments/1r5jv4q/what_shipping_our_first_game_taught_us_about/), the three-person indie team reports **642 sold copies** and **USD $2,425** under the unusually important label *Steam net revenue before the 30% platform cut*. The author also cites approximately **2,500 wishlists at release** and a regular US listing price of **$4.99**. The net label **does not mean money received by the studio or a developer**. No authentic Steam monthly report, refund-stage ledger, settled remittance, cost allocation, personal bank income or after-labor profit was accessed. Likewise, dividing cumulative units by a release-time wishlist stock cannot identify which wishlist accounts purchased, net conversion or a fixed cohort.

The team had initially expected about **six months** of production but reports about **a year and a half** in development. This is a duration estimate, not a time-sheet, and three credited creators are not three independent publisher-income samples.

## Prelaunch discovery measurement ambiguities

The author describes an October 2024 Next Fest movement from **151 to 710 wishlists**, with **559 additions**. That arithmetic is coherent as a same-stage count but is not a causal incremental lift without a comparison trajectory. In a list of marketing events, the author reports **about 300 wishlist additions** from a November 2024 GX.Games demo feature. In later explanatory prose, the same author reports **roughly 200** from the GX Games approach and proposes a rough **seven wishlists per 1,000 plays (0.7%)**. The two GX counts do not have clearly matched observation windows or attribution methods; they should not be silently merged or corrected.

The approximately 50 Cooking Fest wishlists are explicitly described as possibly overlapping the GX event. Other author-listed events include a February 2025 Women in Games campaign and a November 2025 trailer release. Those headline changes are not independent causal effects, and their sums cannot identify total unique acquisition or eventual paid buyers. No cross-site user-level matching between free GX.Games/itch demo play and Steam paid checkout exists in the public evidence.

## Limits

This is one self-selected, initially low-gross team report, not a representative first-publisher sample. A small reported pre-platform revenue figure does not imply any particular member's earnings or ability to recover 18 months of work. No publisher-income median or zero-sale frequency can be obtained from the case.

No private platform access, customer identities, bank data or nonpublic financial records were used.
