# Developer disclosures: three titles in one studio versus one first-year Steam title

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured source record](steam-three-title-studio-and-firstyear-gross-net-selfreports-2026.json) and [arithmetic/provenance QA](../../tests/steam-multititle-firstyear-cases.test.mjs). Both accounts are public self-reports, not authenticated partner statements or representative market samples.

## Studio Ortica: three games, one publishing studio, two currencies

[Studio Ortica's September 30, 2026 report](https://studioortica.substack.com/p/one-year-and-a-half-of-studio-ortica) defines its **EUR net revenue** as a rough result after returns, US tax, a stated Steam 30% share, the author's approximate dollar-to-euro exchange impact, and estimated Italian taxes. This **is not the same** as Valve's monthly payable number, observed cash in a bank account or after-labor profit.

| Title | Reported Steam gross USD | Author-defined net EUR | Units, observation date |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Loan Shark | $299,807 | €70,507 | 71,080 by October 26, 2026 |
| Birds Watching | ~$48,000 | €12,075 | 11,127 by September 8, 2026 |
| The Old Flesh of Nuraga | ~$7,150 | €2,100 | 1,946 by September 8, 2026 |

The approximate title totals are **$354,957 USD gross** and **€84,682 EUR net**, compared with the author’s rounded portfolio headlines of about **$350,000** and **€84,000**. Those small gaps reflect reported precision, not verified accounting errors; never add the two currencies together. The net measure includes estimated personal/national tax treatments, but neither complete studio expenses nor payee-specific labor costs. Three titles contribute to **one studio-level commercial case**, not three independent publisher observations.

**Versioning caution:** Although the article is labeled published September 30, its Loan Shark observation states **as of October 26**. That indicates later content changes without a visible edit timestamp. The Nuraga post has a later September 28 update to 2,600 copies, but its $7,150 revenue snapshot belongs to September 8. Do not divide mismatched dates to estimate realized revenue per copy.

The studio also describes festival and viral-video-associated wishlist changes. Without a counterfactual, their before/after fluctuations do not identify the causal impact of participating in Next Fest, Fishing Fest or personalized store discovery.

## RogueCraft: one year of positive sales, no verified after-cost income

A separate [July 28, 2026 creator disclosure](https://www.reddit.com/r/IndieDev/comments/1v9cnyx/one_year_after_releasing_our_first_steam_game/) reports **1,674 copies**, **220 refunds** (about 13.1% of that count), **$14,992 gross**, and **$11,888** described as net after refunds, chargebacks, taxes and VAT. The author does **not** explicitly establish that this last number includes the Steam platform share or is a verified bank deposit. Thus it must not be compared mechanically with Ortica's post-tax EUR net.

The report also gives 1,578 lifetime players, about 900 Next Fest demo players and 1,166 prelaunch wishlists. These are **different units and intervals**. A simple ratio of demo activity to later purchases does not identify individual conversion, and an average playtime of 6h15m is not a seller earning distribution. No complete development cost or labor ledger was supplied.

## What these cases cannot identify

One voluntarily reporting studio and one voluntarily reporting small developer cannot identify Steam-wide mean/median publisher net pay, the zero-selling share or typical profit. The eligible population would need complete original-paid AppIDs, unique legal publisher entities, unsuccessful nonrespondents, matched windows, authorized receipts and full cost accounting. These case studies improve the *precision of what has and has not been observed*, not the certainty of the missing market-wide quantities.
