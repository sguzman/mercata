# Developer-operated website disclosures: Steam revenue labels versus bank money

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured cases](developer-operated-site-revenue-cases-2026.json) and [repository checks](../../tests/steam-developer-site-revenue-cases.test.mjs). This is a compact *deliberately selected* historical casebook, not a probability sample, authenticated financial export, or assessment of typical Steam developer earnings.

## First Early Access game: 370 reported copies and an ambiguously labeled $619

The [developer-authored October 13, 2025 retrospective of Loki’s Revenge](https://owlgame.dev/blog/lokiretro/) records an Early Access launch on **June 23, 2025**. As of **October 10**, it reports **370 copies**, **18 returns**, and **$619** under the label **Net Steam Revenue**. It also describes roughly **8–10 development hours per week for about 18 months**. The schedule is an estimate, not a verified timesheet.

This is valuable as a first-game low-return outcome; it does **not** demonstrate $619 actually deposited to the developer. In [Valve's reporting documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/payments_salesreporting), near-real-time figures, monthly revenue-share amounts payable, and ultimately received bank funds are distinct objects. The author does not provide a signed share schedule, full paid-out amounts, outsourced expenses, tax payments, or support labor; actual developer economic profit remains **unknown**. The $619 value should never be described as either individual take-home pay or a verified studio loss.

## One game on two stores: gross sales and claimed bank receipts

The [Mixolumia developer's June 22, 2021 postmortem](https://mixolumia.com/postmortem/) reports **713 Steam copies / $6,202 Steam gross** and **1,776 itch.io copies / $17,906 itch.io gross**. A later editorial update gives **$3,680 Steam** and **$14,980 itch.io** as money received after deductions, with an explicit notice that those deposits **lag reported sales**.

The printed gross totals reconcile: **713 + 1,776 = 2,489 units** and **$6,202 + $17,906 = $24,108 gross**. The cash claims total **$18,660**. The net-to-gross fractions are **not** comparable exact conversion factors: bank figures lag sales, the stores launched on different dates (**itch.io August 8, 2020; Steam November 30, 2020**), and VAT practices and buyer compositions differ. Nor can one unusually successful cross-store title identify which marketplace gives a first-time developer a better expected profit.

The author explicitly uses *money reaching a bank account* language. This is stronger definitional detail than a vague "net revenue" caption, but it is still **self-reported**, not an externally inspected bank statement, and still excludes unreported development time and other business costs.

## Reconciliation protocol

[Valve's monthly payment FAQ](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/payments_salesreporting/faq) states that the monthly sales report deducts applicable adjustments from gross and applies the partner's revenue share before calculating payable amounts. Banking charges may further separate payable amounts from credited receipts. Therefore:

1. Do not turn the phrase "net revenue" into a verified bank deposit unless the writer expressly defines it that way, and even then mark it self-reported unless inspected.
2. Never use reported receipt-to-gross ratios as universal Steam fees or developer shares.
3. Require the **same game/product, reporting period, currency, payment recipient and settlement date** before comparing payout efficiency.
4. Treat development labor, platform fees, taxes and third-party publisher participation as separate categories. Bank receipt is not after-cost economic profit.

The two first-party sources do **not** represent the games that never sold, failed studios or companies without a developer blog. Steam-wide sales, median publisher take-home, exact zero-sale rate and after-labor profit remain unknown.
