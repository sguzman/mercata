# Steam release months and Early Access: why published winners are not the denominator

**Reviewed October 28, 2026.** [Structured evidence matrix](multi-month-leaderboards-and-early-access-selection-2026.json). New primary reporting for February, March, April and June exists, but it is **top-seller coverage** rather than a complete paid-release-month panel. Steam income medians remain unknown for those months.

A month with one extraordinary hit can lead a ranking even when most of its games make little money. Source report dates and time since launch vary; the March edition also explicitly notes the supplier was revising modeled unit estimates to account for off-Steam key purchases. **The published leaderboards cannot produce the median, mean, zero-sale share or denominator for the other months.** No leaderboard absence is interpreted as zero sales.

The [monthly coverage gate](../../site/steam-monthly-coverage.mjs) requires a claimed complete population, explicit low-end inclusion, an equal valuation horizon and a compatible measure *before* it will accept a distribution estimate. Its synthetic tests deliberately reject top-seller reports. It makes no claim that a provider-claimed complete sample is independently correct.

## Early Access graduation is another selected population

In the [May 19, 2026 GameDiscoverCo assessment](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-happened-to-early-access-graduates), **91** titles that had reached version 1.0 were tracked under a qualifying initial Early Access commercial threshold: paid games with **more than 5,000 estimated copies in their first 30 days**, or free games with **more than $10,000 first-30-day revenue**.

Within those **selected survivors**, the median Early Access period was **15 months**, and **21%** were modeled to earn more during the first 30 days of version 1.0 than during the first 30 days of Early Access. The article cites a **20%** figure for its prior-year selected cohort. Neither figure describes all Early Access games, the probability that an initially unsuccessful title ever reaches 1.0, or true net profit.

The publisher also notes that at least one game’s version 1.0 revenue includes DLC released alongside 1.0. Therefore both dates and product mix must match before comparing incremental base-game orders. **Version 1.0 is a visibility event, not a second first paid launch** for an already commercial Early Access game.

## Next required evidence

An actual multi-month Steam newcomer-income panel needs, for each release month: historical paid-at-first-release AppID frame, delisted and zero-review games, same-age **30/90/365-day** valuation, payment provenance, explicit key and DLC treatment, and compatible model versions. Then resolve legal publisher accounts and incomplete-response bias before reporting publisher-income medians. This checkpoint adds a coverage audit and data-quality tests only. **No multi-month typical-game revenue estimate was generated.**
