# Beauty and fashion topic turnover: dated title-query evidence

**Evidence review: October 24, 2026.** [Machine query-series register](beauty-fashion-query-series.json). This study records what several officially published title-based video observations measure. It does not assert comparable growth, a category-wide audience, or creator income.

## Five different observations, not a time series

| Named format and publisher report date | Observed period and exact available title predicate | Reported value | Inference boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [GRWM history](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/grwm-2023-trends/) — August 28, 2023 | 2023 to publication; variations of GRWM in video title, specific variants unstated | More than 6 billion **video views** | Partial 2023, broad routine format, unknown query specification |
| [Prom and GRWM](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/prom-youtube/) — May 8, 2025 | Full 2024, both prom and GRWM in title | More than 25 million **video views** | Narrow school-event titles, not all prom content |
| [Halloween makeup](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/halloween-makeup/) — October 21, 2025 | January 1–October 5, 2025; both halloween and makeup in title | More than 1 billion **video views** | Partial year, holiday-themed titles; not Halloween month-only |
| [Beginner sewing](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/fashion-design-creators-youtube/) — May 4, 2026 | Full 2025; sewing-machine-related videos with beginner in title | More than 100 million **video views** | Instructional niche, related-content classifier undocumented |

All five are YouTube's first-party global measurements, not independently replicated platform censuses. Title criteria can overlap; one video may enter more than one group. None of these totals counts distinct viewers.

## Research category versus observable title rule

Beauty, fashion, routines and tutorials are overlapping editorial categories, while a title predicate is only a reproducible approximation of one slice of them. A video with a prom makeover could also be GRWM or makeup; a sewing video could be a costume, beginner lesson, fashion design, or fandom tutorial.

For a real repeated monthly series, freeze each predicate (including spelling variants, language/token rules and search fields) before collecting a defined time period; retain dates, video IDs and formatting, then distinguish uploaded-video counts from playback events. Where the publisher's classifier is undisclosed, mark the method **not independently reproducible** rather than pretending an API search returns the platform's complete internal total.

## Novelty, seasonal hypotheses and viewing-metric breaks

Halloween makeup and prom fashion have plausible calendar-linked peaks, but a single annual/partial-year aggregate does **not** measure seasonality. At least two or more comparable annual cycles, month-by-month observations under the same predicate, and a reference content category would be needed before attributing a spike to the event. Morning routine and beginner sewing may persist throughout the year; this too is a hypothesis, not a measured evergreen revenue horizon.

The first-frame public-view counting definition changed **August 24, 2026**. Any later monthly view series crossing that date must harmonize numerator definitions or visibly label the break. The five values here mostly precede that change, and the 2026 routine figure counts uploads instead of view events.

## Still unmeasured

No trend rate, novel-format survival rate, monthly seasonal lift, creator earnings, production costs, distinct people or per-query creator population is established. Each remains `null` in the machine record. This partial result advances the named topic-turnover research task, not an empirical forecast of what to produce.
