# Why creator supply cannot be divided into unrelated topic attention counts

This advances the existing audience-versus-supply question using [CreatorDB channel-index snapshots](creator-index-comparison-2026.md) and [already recorded topic observations](topic-observations.md). It does not create new marketplace specimens or pretend the selected sources measure a matched cohort.

## Observability, not scarcity ranking

The CreatorDB June 27 source counts channels in its **tracked index** with one vendor-coded primary category, not every global uploader or an observed cash-earning business. Public YouTube topic figures report title-filtered videos or views at different times, while the survey has a narrowly recruited fan respondent denominator. No common cohort, matched channel identities, time window, deduplicated viewer total or scope-equivalent category is supplied.

Consequently, the **audience-per-active-creator ratios remain unknown** for all three pairings. Even a numeric expression like '500 million divided by 196,340' would count repeated video views against all indexed Music channels and have no defensible market scarcity, unique-audience or income interpretation.

## Acceptance condition for a genuine matched supply–demand measure

First define the niche membership predicate independently of platform metadata categories; identify a legitimate matched, time-bounded creator sample including small and inactive channels; measure impressions/view events and unique viewers using valid access rules; preserve duplicated memberships; then separately measure receipts, costs, hours and zero earners. Until those observations exist, neither a profitable underserved niche nor the number of viable small creators can be inferred.
