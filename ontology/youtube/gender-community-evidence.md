# Gender-associated YouTube communities: bounded descriptive research

**Reviewed October 26, 2026.** [Machine-readable limitations](gender-community-evidence.json). This describes an owner-only measurement mechanism and a dated vendor sample, **not** an assessment of any individual's sex, gender identity or interests.

## Actual public evidence

The [2025 Tubular gaming/beauty study](beauty-gaming-overlap-2025.md) reports on **74,276 U.S. viewers categorized by the vendor as female, aged 25–34**, who had at least five YouTube Gaming engagements between July 2024 and June 2025. It presents a **5% Beauty/Gaming overlap** and viewing-affinity multiples of **6.4× YSL Beauty, 5.7× Olay and 5.4× NYX Professional Makeup**.

Its publicly available reporting does not establish the matched overlap numerator, the percentage base, vendor gender classification, recruitment probabilities, or weighting. Therefore the panel size is **not the national population**, and multiplying it by 5% is **not a justified unique-person audience count**. Relative affinities are not probabilities of brand purchases or creator income. Reposts and vendor commentary concern the same underlying research, not fresh independent samples.

These observations undermine the assumption that beauty and gaming must be disjoint. They do not establish the inverse stereotype or a complete gender-by-niche population distribution.

## Separate Australian YouTube-watching children: within-group genre reports

The original [Roy Morgan Young Australians survey](https://www.roymorgan.com/findings/10058-australian-kids-youtube-2025), collected **April–December 2025** and published February 17, 2026, surveyed **1,129 Australian children ages 6–13** overall. The paper's public chart reports differences in chosen video genres conditional on **surveyed YouTube-watching children** in separately reported boy/girl groups:

| Reported video genre | Boys who watch YouTube | Girls who watch YouTube |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Gaming | 67% | 39% |
| Fashion | 5% | 31% |
| Music | 27% | 41% |
| Animation | 38% | 36% |

The percentages answer **within each reported respondent gender group, what share chose the video genre?** They do **not** mean, for example, 67% of gaming viewers are boys or that all children in a gender group must enjoy a particular genre. Children could choose multiple genres. Exact YouTube-watching *subgroup* counts, per-cell confidence intervals and overlapping gaming/fashion viewership are not published in the reviewed summary. The overall sample N=1,129 cannot replace those missing denominators.

This Australian survey is **independent of** the Tubular U.S. age-25–34 modeled female Gaming panel; it neither confirms the latter's 5% beauty/gaming overlap nor supplies comparable adult/cross-national audience counts. It also does not sample actual YouTube *creators* or earnings.

The [UK Ofcom 2025–2026 child media survey](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/children/children-and-parents-media-use-and-attitudes-report-2026), separately registered in Mercata, reports **directional** relative preferences in sports/gameplay versus music/how-to/ASMR/self-improvement among child video viewers across **any video apps or sites**. That is not a YouTube-specific genre-by-gender table, and cannot be pooled with the Australian survey. Both studies use source-defined survey labels and aggregate results only; no individual traits or minors' profiles are inferred.

## Platform measures and suppression

The [official YouTube Analytics dimensions reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/analytics/dimensions) defines an owner-authorized `gender` reporting dimension for **logged-in users** with values `female`, `male` and `user_specified`. These are account-report categories, not confirmed identity or sex of real people. They do not measure every signed-out viewer or publicly expose competitor demographics.

YouTube's [data limitations guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9101241?hl=en) says age and gender breakdowns can be withheld for low traffic or filtered dimensions, with undisclosed thresholds, and data for Made for Kids audiences can also be constrained. **Missing demographic cells are unknown, not zero**.

## Acceptance requirements

Cross-community comparison requires common geography, date, age range, inclusion criterion, frame, measurement unit, overlap definition, suppression rules and documented source validity. Commercial panels, owner Analytics and independently surveyed adults are different frames unless reconciled. No person-level inference, profiling of minors, guessed gender based on faces, names, voices or comments, or unauthorized account data is appropriate.

Full YouTube gender distributions, **creator composition**, gender-by-format *global viewing*, signed-out demographics and national beauty/gaming intersections remain `null`. The independent [audience-overlap protocol](audience-overlap.md) retains the same data integrity conditions.
