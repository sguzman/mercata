# Finance and business YouTube content: education, marketing and regulated promotion

**Research date: October 24, 2026.** [Machine content taxonomy](finance-content-taxonomy.json). This is a public source-based classification for YouTube content, **not financial advice, an investigation of a specific creator, or a census of finance channels**.

## Eight research categories with overlapping audiences

| Content family | Examples to code in a dated video sample | Commercial relationship to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal budgeting and cash-flow literacy | Budgeting, savings concepts, spreadsheet methods | Do not assume an offer or income |
| Debt and credit education | Interest examples, credit reports, terms of debt | Do not infer a lender or personal credit decision |
| Investing concepts and market education | Diversification, statements, risk concepts | Separate education from securities promotion |
| Financial-service product comparison | Account or credit product walkthroughs | Sponsored placement, paid links and affiliate commissions may matter |
| Sponsored finance or banking offers | Clearly paid product promotion | Platform declaration, disclosure and country/product restrictions |
| Business skills and entrepreneurship instruction | Invoicing, business planning, cost calculations | Not automatically a “get rich” or income-guarantee promotion |
| Creator-owned courses and business software | Paid training, spreadsheets or software | Owned product/customer relationship, price, refunds and rights |
| Specific stock tips and extraordinary return pitches | Particular securities suggestions, investment chats, claimed guaranteed profits | Verify context, credentials/registration claims and possible misleading promotions |

These categories describe **content and observable commercial posture**, not personal identity. One video may both teach a concept and promote a product. A finance tutorial may also belong to software, video essay or educational categories. Do not sum category viewership as though the people are disjoint.

## Platform moderation and advertiser eligibility

The [YouTube advertiser-friendly content guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en) evaluate the actual video and its context, including dishonest acts, unreliable content and advertiser suitability. Calling a video “finance education” alone does not guarantee ad eligibility, a particular RPM, or eligibility for the YouTube Partner Program. Video ad income is not synonymous with affiliate or sponsorship proceeds.

[YouTube's external-links policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9054257?hl=en) prohibits links sending users to disallowed content such as phishing or malware. Ordinary affiliate links are not categorically banned, but excessive use in dedicated spam accounts may violate separate policies. A product link is therefore neither necessarily prohibited nor necessarily safe or lawful.

[YouTube's branded-content policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/17596007?hl=en) and the [U.S. FTC endorsement guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) address overlapping **but distinct** declarations: platform sponsorship controls and applicable material-connection disclosure law. Financial product promotions can involve other country- and product-specific licensing, advertising or consumer rules. A YouTube label cannot establish complete compliance.

## Securities tips and investor protection are a separate risk question

The SEC's [February 6, 2026 investor alert](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/social-media-stock-scams) warns about fraudulent social-media stock tips, impostors claiming to be investment professionals, investment group chats and guaranteed high returns with little risk. **An official investor warning does not establish how prevalent scams are on YouTube**, nor does discussing investing make a creator fraudulent. The study does not identify a creator, evaluate licensing status or decide an individual content item's legal status.

U.S. investor education sources and FTC guidance are **not global legislation**. Mexican, UK and other country/product law and actual creator-business circumstances require independently researched jurisdiction-specific primary evidence, which is not available in this record.

## Requirements before economics or audience claims

To estimate even a narrow niche, sample published video objects in an explicit date range under platform permissions; record overlapping topic codes, format, country, language, evidence of paid/affiliate connections, definitions of views and data access, and an actual sample-selection rule. Nonparticipating creators, deleted/nonpublic content, unmonetized channels, platform measurement revisions and repeated viewers create coverage and survivorship bias.

No finance niche unique audience, creator population, published-video total, typical RPM, affiliate conversion, representative sponsorship rate, newcomer net profit, or prevalence of financial fraud is established here. All fields remain `null`. Follow the [overlapping-audience protocol](audience-overlap.md), [country scope](geographic-scope.md), [income cohort](newcomer-income-cohort.md) and [sponsorship disclosures](brand-disclosure-risk.md) before asserting such results.

The practical output of this task is an independently testable content taxonomy distinguishing **education**, **marketing**, and **higher-scrutiny financial promotion**, not a ranking of profitable channels or finance products.
