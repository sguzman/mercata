# Promotion costs and attributable attention: YouTube Promote versus organic discovery

**Research date: October 23, 2026.** This is an official product/measurement study, **not a measured marketing return**. [Machine-recorded rules](promote-economics.json).

## The money source is different from the earnings source

[YouTube Promote](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12199105?hl=en) is a simplified paid advertising product created through YouTube and powered by Google Ads. It supports goals such as video views, subscribers and website clicks. It is **money spent by the creator or advertising account** to buy distribution. It is not money earned from normal YouTube advertising inventory.

The [Promote campaign guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16869935?hl=en) reports a channel's actual **spend** (subject to its campaign budget), ad impressions, ad views, new attributed subscribers and website-link clicks. The promotion-attributed subscriber definition requires watching at least **10 seconds** or clicking the promotion and then subscribing within **three days**. This is a publisher-defined attribution window, not an independently estimated incremental gain.

**A selected budget is not actual spend, and spending money does not guarantee delivery**. The [troubleshooting guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16869853?hl=en) says promotional review may take one to two days; campaigns can be disapproved or serve poorly under narrow targeting or insufficient budgets.

## Paid activity is not automatically YPP progress

The [official Promote FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12199105?hl=en) says that subscribers and watch hours generated directly by ads **do not count toward YPP eligibility**. However **Follow-on Views** are organic later video views by people who first saw a promotion, and the platform describes eligible organic follow-on activity as counting toward YPP. An advertising campaign does **not** itself earn an YPP qualification.

The same FAQ says promotions do not change the ordinary organic recommendation algorithm's treatment of the video. That is a platform policy statement; it is not a causal experiment proving that ads cannot affect later human attention or that promoted users would never have watched organically.

## The ad view billing definition changes by format

[Google Ads cost-per-view guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472735?hl=en) uses different **TrueView** billing events:

| Advertised placement | When a TrueView view can be billed |
| --- | --- |
| In-stream | 30 seconds, full duration if shorter, or interaction, whichever occurs first |
| In-feed | Thumbnail click or at least 10 seconds autoplay, with short-video completion rules |
| Shorts | 10 seconds, full duration if shorter, or CTA click |

These are **not YouTube's public playback-start views** after the August 2026 count change. Do not divide typical creator revenue by bought view events to invent advertising return. Google's [video analytics](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6086441?hl=en) also distinguishes ad performance from organic view-performance reporting (channel linkage required for the latter).

## What a credible cost/attribution study would need

For each consenting creator campaign, record campaign goal, target country/language, type and dates, assigned budget **and separately actual spend**, true ad placement format, reported impressions, billed views, attributed subscribers, later organic qualified activity, website clicks, independently documented buyers/revenue, production/support costs and resulting business net profit.

A campaign cost divided by **attributed subscription events** describes only cost per *reported attributed event* and cannot prove cost per *incremental retained audience member*. A control cohort, seasonality, upload cadence, age/geography matching and other promotion exposure are needed for causal inference. The first 3–7 days after launch cannot on their own establish long-term return or cohort retention.

No representative observed promotional campaign budget, typical cost per ad view, cost per organic retained viewer, actual sales, profit margin, or return on investment is available here; all are `null`. Mercata does not operate or purchase campaigns, gather private advertising accounts or assert that paid promotions are profitable.
