# YouTube publishing cadence: observed peer medians and dispersion

**Reviewed October 24, 2026.** [Machine-readable bounded comparisons](publishing-cadence-evidence.json). These studies report different channel populations and cannot justify a universal instruction to upload a fixed number of videos each week.

## Selected niche/size groups with a median *and* spread

[AIR Media-Tech's July 14, 2026 study](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/does-posting-more-on-youtube-hurt-your-rpm) describes 282 channels accessible through YouTube Analytics in 13 niches, and publishes the following **median monthly uploads and 25th–75th percentile intervals**. Its access-selected channel set is not a census of new creators; group-level sample sizes and exact Shorts inclusion are not established in the published table.

| Vendor's channel niche/tier | Median uploads/month | 25th–75th percentile |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Education, medium | 26 | 13–31 |
| Education, small | 15 | 5–25 |
| Music, medium | 5 | 3–16 |
| Crafting, medium | 2 | 1–4 |
| Lifestyle, medium | 9 | 6–27 |
| Music, small | 7 | 4–14 |
| Entertainment, medium | 7 | 3–16 |
| Lifestyle, small | 8 | 4–15 |
| Gaming, medium | 10 | 4–19 |
| Gadgets, medium | 5.5 | 2–12 |
| Entertainment, small | 2 | 1–20 |
| Kids & Teens, medium | 8 | 2–15 |
| Kids & Teens, large | 9 | 5–17 |
| Gaming, small | 3 | 2–8 |

A channel peer median without dispersion is misleading: for example, *small entertainment* channels span 1–20 uploads/month in the reported middle half. The original study also supplies coefficients of variation for irregular schedules. Neither statistic measures creator hours or confirms paid earnings.

The study compares the **same selected channels** in months with different upload rates when analyzing RPM. This removes some fixed channel attributes but does **not** eliminate seasonal advertising demand, production-team staffing or shifts in video quality and audience interest. Do not infer causality, or transform its estimated revenue per video into audited net income.

## A very different general frequency sample

[vidIQ's July 8, 2026 study](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-upload-frequency-study/) covers **10,210,278 channels with at least 1,000 subscribers**, and averages both *long-form and Shorts* published between April 2025 and March 2026.

| Average monthly uploads | Channels in tier | Median monthly views growth | Median monthly subscriber growth |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fewer than 1 | 1,889,839 | 0.53% | 0.00% |
| 1–3 | 2,546,600 | 0.89% | 0.10% |
| 4–7 | 1,952,305 | 1.32% | 0.39% |
| 8–11 | 1,107,457 | 1.70% | 0.63% |
| 12 or more | 2,714,077 | 2.18% | 0.91% |

This is a **correlation**: greater upload cadence is associated with higher median observed growth *within this selection of channels*. More established channels often have audiences, funds, teams and better delivery systems that make higher frequency feasible, so this does not show that publishing 12+ times causes growth. It excludes channels below 1,000 subscribers and is not an all-new-creator cohort.

## What the two datasets cannot prove

The AIR figures condition on niche and size among a small owner-Analytics sample; the vidIQ sample conditions on having 1,000+ subscribers and combines Shorts with long-form. Their medians **cannot be pooled or directly ranked**. Neither observes a representative distribution of production hours, creative costs, zero-earner output or causal profit from one additional video.

A robust next study must separate format, country, channel age, effort and audience scale, collect an independently specified sample of peers, calculate median and dispersion *within each compatible cohort*, and retain nonpublishing and failed entrants when the target is newcomer outcomes. Avoid storing creator identities or unpublished private analytics.
