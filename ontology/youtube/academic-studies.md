# Peer-reviewed creator economics research — selected studies

**Research snapshot 2026-10-23.** These records cover **four academic studies**. Each was checked against its publicly available abstract or publisher summary, not all underlying tables and appendices. The [machine model](academic-studies.json) records this reading limit and deliberately leaves unverified averages, medians and causal effect sizes as `null`.

## Working on an algorithm-controlled platform as a content creator: what explains unequal earnings on YouTube?

**Source:** [European Societies / MIT Press](https://doi.org/10.1162/EUSO.a.42) (2026). **Population:** YouTube channels from Germany, Austria and Switzerland. **Sample size:** not established from abstract.

- Earnings are strongly skewed in the study's channel dataset.
- The authors report a Gini coefficient of 0.89 for the earnings measure they analyze.

**Cautions:** Geography is limited to Germany, Austria and Switzerland. Methods and whether money is observed or modeled were not established by reading the full PDF. No general median or mean for all YouTube creators can be inferred. Channel sample recruitment details not established from this abstract.

## How do users contribute to YouTube channels’ revenue? An empirical analysis of Korean beauty channels

**Source:** [Computers in Human Behavior / Elsevier](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108741) (2025). **Population:** 50 Korean beauty YouTube channels analyzed using channel Analytics and panel fixed-effects regressions. **Sample size:** 50.

- Returning-viewer watch time was the strongest revenue-associated measure examined.
- New-viewer and subscriber watch time were positively associated with revenue; likes were not statistically significant in this analysis.

**Cautions:** Small selected channel sample in one country and niche. Fixed-effects regression associations are not randomized causal effects. No country-generalized beauty niche RPM or newcomer earnings provided. The public abstract does not establish whole-platform selection or profit after production costs.

## Creator strategies in the platform economy: How frequency, consistency, and content type affect YouTube engagement, attention, and revenue

**Source:** [Telecommunications Policy / Elsevier](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.telpol.2026.103252) (2026). **Population:** 47 Korea-based YouTube beauty channels, examining frequency, consistency and video format. **Sample size:** 47.

- Upload frequency was statistically associated with reported engagement and revenue.
- Regular publishing was associated with audience loyalty.
- A higher concentration of Shorts was associated with lower sustained viewing time in the studied channels.

**Cautions:** Channel sample is 47, not all Korean channels or all genres. Cross-sectional and longitudinal confounding may remain; findings do not prove a causal posting schedule. No universal optimal weekly upload frequency or creator-income prediction follows. Full data/method tables are not analyzed here.

## Who Reaps All the Superchats? A Large-Scale Analysis of Income Inequality in Virtual YouTuber Livestreaming

**Source:** [CHI 2025 / Association for Computing Machinery](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713877) (2025). **Population:** 1,923 VTubers and over one million hours of publicly accessible YouTube live-stream records. **Sample size:** 1923.

- Authors analyze the concentration and inequality of monetized live viewer contributions among sampled virtual performers.

**Cautions:** Public live chat/stream records are not total channel income, after-fee receipts or profit. Sampling details and economic statistics beyond the abstract need full-text assessment. VTuber experiences cannot be generalized to all live creators or video formats.


## Comparison to creator income data

The [297-channel monetized cohort](small-channel-income.md) contains limited monthly native earnings for already approved channels. The [Patreon analysis](creator-income-evidence.md) examines off-platform paid supporters and excludes low or zero earners. The four studies above test different questions with different regions, products, formats and inclusion rules. They **cannot be pooled** into one average YouTube creator salary.

The German/Austrian/Swiss study's reported Gini of 0.89 suggests a very unequal earnings measure, but it does not by itself show the probability that an independent new channel will ever earn money. The Korean beauty studies indicate that viewer retention and content decisions are worth studying more carefully, but they supply no guaranteed strategy or RPM. The VTuber study concerns live Super Chat activity, not the total creator economy.

## Research work still required

Read the full methods and supplementary data where legally available; distinguish observed account earnings from modeled public view counts; record sample recruitment, included non-earners, currency, study period, distribution quantiles, production costs and uncertainties. Any future quantitative comparison must preserve the specific population measured.
