# Synthetic media on YouTube: disclosure, monetization, privacy and copyright are distinct

**Official guidance checked October 24, 2026.** [Machine rule matrix](synthetic-media-rights-policy.json), [case illustration auditor](../../site/synthetic-media-audit.mjs), and [tests](../../tests/synthetic-media-audit.test.mjs). These are documented policy boundaries, **not** a verdict on any particular video or a claim about typical AI creator income.

## Four independent questions

**1. Must AI generation or alteration be disclosed?** [YouTube's AI-use help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en) requires disclosure for realistic, meaningfully AI-generated/altered people, events, places or scenes and AI-generated music as central content. Creator assistance with outlines, captions, audio repair, minor aesthetic changes and clearly fantastical video normally does not trigger the specified disclosure. Labeling may be automatic for some internal-tool/C2PA content. Creators can select the **AI use** field in Studio.

**2. Does a label disqualify monetization?** No automatic ban follows **from disclosure alone** according to that Help page. A separate [YPP authenticity policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) can disqualify low-originality, highly repetitive or mass-produced videos/channels, regardless of production tools. YouTube [clarified on July 10, 2025](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/356734251?hl=en&msgid=453057053) that the July 15 rename from “repetitious” to “inauthentic” clarified an older restriction, rather than banning all AI work. Actual eligibility depends on the channel and each relevant policy, not just a disclosure checkbox.

**3. What if someone impersonates a real person?** A proper disclosure is not a privacy exemption. YouTube's [identity complaint guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801895?hl=en) covers realistic synthetic face/voice content; its [likeness-detection feature](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16440338?hl=en) is experimental, may be country-unavailable, requires adult channel permissions and sensitive identity/selfie verification, and yields review candidates rather than guaranteed violations.

**4. Who owns the video, music, image or voice?** [Copyright guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797466?hl=en) describes evolving national legal approaches and the importance of human creation. AI-use disclosure neither transfers others' copyrights nor proves the generated work qualifies for copyright. Licensed assets and local law need independent evaluation.

## Bounded example cases

| Hypothetical example | Disclosure under documented examples | Other questions still separate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clearly fantastical animation | Not required for this reason alone | Rights, originality, YPP and community rules |
| AI help writing an outline or adding captions | Not required for assistance alone | Later realistic deceptive scenes may require disclosure |
| Photorealistic false speech by an actual person | Required | Identity/privacy and misinformation risks |
| Realistic invented event or real-place footage | Required | Accuracy, safety and possible rights questions |
| AI music as the central content | Required | Composition/recording license and originality |

The classification helper reports **policy-illustration status**, never legal compliance or a platform monetization decision.

## Economic conclusions and unknowns

Neither YouTube's documentation nor these examples provide the prevalence of disclosing videos, a channel YPP acceptance probability, median revenue from AI videos, actual enforcement rate, copyright ownership across countries, or profit after labor and tools. Those are **unknown**, not zero, and no generic forecast or universal AI monetization prohibition is claimed.
