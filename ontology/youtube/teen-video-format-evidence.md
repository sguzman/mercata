# Child and teen video genres: age and survey-gender evidence without profiling

**Evidence reviewed October 24, 2026.** Machine observations: [teen-video-format-evidence.json](teen-video-format-evidence.json). This study **does not** provide a measured gender-by-format distribution for YouTube itself or estimate a population of "girl-oriented channels."

## Three different population frames

1. [Ofcom, Children and Parents Media Use and Attitudes Report 2025–6](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/children/2026-children-and-parents-report/children-and-parents-media-use-and-attitudes-report-2025-6.pdf?v=418231), published **May 21, 2026**: UK child/parent media survey covering **video watching on any app or site**, including but not limited to YouTube. The report describes 5,008 parent interviews and 3,426 child interviews in its overall sampling design. Module and age-specific denominators are not automatically those whole-sample counts.
2. The **Barb in-home YouTube viewing measurement**, as reported separately by Ofcom, observes mean minutes per week among UK children aged 4–15 in **2025**, with home-device coverage limitations. It does **not** report YouTube genre by gender in the cited extract.
3. [Pew, Teens, Social Media and AI Chatbots 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/12/09/teens-social-media-and-ai-chatbots-2025/) surveyed **1,458 U.S. teens aged 13–17**. It measures self-reported **YouTube platform use**, not which video genres they watch.

These are separate observations; they cannot be multiplied or pooled to invent a count of girls watching beauty, gaming, animation or lifestyle videos on YouTube.

## Video genres in Ofcom's UK child survey (across video apps)

| Population among children who watch videos on any app or site | Genre | Respondents reporting watching genre |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Age 3–5 | Cartoons or animation | 80% |
| Age 3–5 | Funny videos | 43% |
| Age 3–5 | Learning/help with homework | 38% |
| Age 8–17 | Funny videos | 62% |
| Age 8–17 | Music videos | 46% |
| Age 8–17 | Gameplay content | 43% |

All percentages are self- or parent-reported video genre behaviors **across video-sharing apps** within the specified UK ages; they are **not YouTube-specific viewing shares**. Categories overlap; their percentages must not be summed. Ofcom describes boys in the child video-viewer sample as more likely to watch sports and gaming, and girls as more likely to watch music, instructional/how-to, ASMR and self-improvement. In this report excerpt Ofcom does not supply all corresponding gender-by-genre percentages or cell-level uncertainty. Relative group trends must never become stereotyped, individual-level gender judgments.

## Directly measured YouTube platform usage (Pew U.S. teens)

| U.S. teens aged 13–17 | Boys | Girls |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Report ever using YouTube | 94% | 89% |
| Report using YouTube almost constantly | 20% | 13% |

**These are percentages within self-reported teen gender categories**, not the gender composition of YouTube viewers, and not U.S. genre-specific demand. Pew measures *platform frequency* rather than music, sports, animation or gaming format selection.

## Measured UK in-home YouTube watching (Barb 2025)

| UK age range | Average weekly in-home YouTube viewing |
| --- | ---: |
| 4–15 | 7 hours 7 minutes |
| 4–9 | 6 hours 47 minutes |
| 10–15 | 7 hours 27 minutes |

These estimates exclude out-of-home viewing and some mobile-network use under the device measurement described by Ofcom. They are not time spent on all video apps and cannot be joined to Pew's U.S. 13–17 use rates.

## Classification and publication safeguards

- **Child-directed, mixed-audience and teen/general-audience content are legally and platform-operationally distinct.** An animated or feminine-coded aesthetic alone does not determine made-for-kids classification.
- "Girl-oriented" and "teen-oriented" are **descriptive content hypotheses**, never an individual child's inferred identity. The aggregate evidence cannot support a distinct unique-viewer or creator population in either group.
- Children aged **under 8** were represented through parent/guardian answers in the Ofcom tracker; children aged 8+ could answer directly. New **2025/6 sample-module and interview-mode changes** mean year-to-year trend claims require method audits.
- Never collect minors' channel identities, profile images, comments, watch histories, demographics or cross-platform matching keys. No individual inferred sex, gender identity, vulnerability or intent.
- No income, ad-targeting, earnings, creator count, overlap, cross-country total or causal gender preference is inferred.
