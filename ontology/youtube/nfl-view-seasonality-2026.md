# NFL YouTube content as a bounded multi-year and offseason measurement example

**Research review: October 24, 2026.** [Machine observations](nfl-view-seasonality-2026.json). Source: YouTube Culture & Trends' [February 5, 2026 NFL report](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/nfl-youtube-culture-trends-report/). The published figures are first-party global platform aggregates of **NFL-related video activity**, not a census of NFL fans or creator earnings.

## What the same source actually reports

| Period and unit | Source comparison | Research interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Calendar 2025, global view events** | More than **20 billion** NFL-related views | Total platform-classified viewing events; not unique people |
| **Annual global view totals, 2020 versus 2025** | Increase of **more than 250%** | Relative change of view totals at two endpoints; no annual 2021–24 values |
| **Annual global NFL-related uploads, 2020 versus 2025** | Increase of **more than 400%** | Relative upload-object growth, not view growth or creator count |
| **Within 2025 NFL-related views** | **More than 30%** occurred between the end of the Super Bowl and start of the regular season | A within-year **offseason share**, not year-over-year offseason growth |

The denominator for the offseason figure is *all platform-classified NFL-related views in 2025*. It does not establish exact per-month views, an off-season audience as distinct people, or how much the absence of NFL games causes content engagement.

## Why this is a more informative example than a title-trend snapshot

YouTube's source directly supplies two same-topic **2020–2025 endpoint comparisons** plus a **2025 within-year seasonal partition**. This makes it a genuine dated change-and-seasonality *case*, but only at the reported aggregation level. An independently reproducible time series would still require the missing intermediate annual and monthly values and the platform's classification rules for NFL-related content.

Do **not** synthesize intermediate years from the two endpoint percentages, extrapolate future growth, or divide view totals by number of creators to infer RPM or profit. Upload counts are distinct from view events, and the source does not establish a causal relationship between the two.

YouTube's public playback-start definition changed August 24, 2026, after the periods measured here. Any later comparison with 2026 post-August values needs [metric harmonization](view-definition-break.md).

## Unresolved economic research

Unique fans, active channels producing NFL content, creator labor, sponsorship receipts, net profit, creator population, seasonal lift relative to a comparable control and confidence intervals are **unknown**. The [machine case](nfl-view-seasonality-2026.json) preserves them as missing.

This supports part of Mercata's dated-topic and seasonality study, without completing a general worldwide forecast or assuming that NFL behavior applies to unrelated sports and subcultures.
