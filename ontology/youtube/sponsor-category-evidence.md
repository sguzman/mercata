# Observed YouTube sponsorship activity by content category, 2025–2026

**Research snapshot: October 24, 2026.** [Machine-readable observations and exclusions](sponsor-category-evidence.json). This study measures **detected promotional content**, not audited creator sponsorship earnings or contract value.

## A dated long-form video index

[ThoughtLeaders' October 15 report](https://thoughtleaders.ai/blog/2026-10-15-youtube-sponsorship-market-october-2026/) scanned approximately **2.9 million long-form uploads** dated September 15–October 14, 2026, detecting a sponsor-style read on **176,686 videos (6.1%)**. It reported **5.9%** for the preceding 30 days and **4.8%** one year earlier. The vendor revised its analysis October 17 to exclude the dynamically inserted advertising of the Locked On podcast network. Public scans can flag creator codes, affiliate mentions or other branded reads with no independently verified payment.

The following is the percentage of **scanned long-form videos in each vendor-defined channel category** containing a detected sponsor. A rate of 15% does **not** mean 15% of creators receive payment.

| Channel category | Detected videos, Sep 15–Oct 14, 2026 | Share of scanned category uploads |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Gaming | 37,093 | 8.6% |
| Lifestyle | 30,997 | 4.9% |
| Technology | 21,888 | 12.2% |
| How To & Crafts | 18,724 | 13.8% |
| Sports | 18,440 | 9.1% |
| General Knowledge | 17,005 | 7.6% |
| Entertainment | 10,422 | 2.8% |
| Music | 7,077 | 3.0% |
| News & Politics | 6,458 | 1.9% |
| Food | 5,191 | 5.2% |
| Health & Fitness | 2,018 | 15.0% |

ThoughtLeaders reports that **Shorts were not analyzed for sponsorship detection**, despite their meaningful share of observed uploads. Its tracked channel composition changes across periods and an automated brand match can lag, so even repeated sponsor detections are not a census of contracts or a causal increase in advertising investment. Rates refer only to **long-form** and the vendor's own classification.

## Independent but non-interchangeable index

[SponsorRadar's August 2026 published snapshot](https://sponsorradar.com/state-of-youtube-sponsorships) describes **660,985 detected placements** in the February 2025–January 2026 analysis window and 129,386 tracked channels in its stated corpus. Its indexed category totals include **147,964 gaming placements (22.4%)**, **66,217 education placements (10.0%)**, and **51,743 finance/business placements (7.8%)**.

Those percentages mean *shares of its detected placements*, **not the share of gaming uploads with a sponsor**. SponsorRadar explicitly cautions that detection coverage grew during the window and larger channels are more heavily covered. These numbers cannot be added to ThoughtLeaders' reports or interpreted as a new-channel sponsor success rate.

## Another adjacent demand signal: applications, not YouTube videos

[Upfluence's Q1 2026 analysis](https://www.upfluence.com/research/creator-economy-industry-study-q1-2026/) reports **17,288 creator applications** for **2,605 brand campaigns** dated January 1–March 27, 2026, from its commercial multi-platform network. Fashion/apparel's share of platform applications moved from **6.2% in January to 12.8% in March**; health/wellness fell from **17.0% to 10.7%**.

The unit here is a **cross-platform application**, not an observed YouTube promotion, a successful contract or a disclosed commission. Seasonal brand marketing may influence the mix; it cannot establish a YouTube creator's income or advertiser demand for a specific niche without new evidence.

## A separate creator-niche fee sample: accepted agreements, not detected ads

[Kolect's October 2026 managed-campaign benchmarks](https://www.kolect.ai/reports/creator-marketing-benchmarks-2026) report **agreed creator-side fees** by the *creator's main content niche* among selected campaigns across **TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and other platforms**, not YouTube-only contracts. Six illustrative published rows preserve the observed differences without reproducing the original complete commercial benchmark table:

| Creator's primary niche | Median agreed creator fee (USD per accepted collaboration) | Median fee per 1,000 creator-average views (USD) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Finance | $800 | $36.53 |
| Travel | $775 | $24.35 |
| Gaming | $560 | $25.93 |
| Education | $500 | $24.17 |
| Technology | $450 | $26.41 |
| Health and fitness | $400 | $27.69 |

The cohort covers campaigns initiated August 19, 2025–October 15, 2026, with most collaborations during 2026, and is concentrated among U.S./European creators in technology, home, DIY and lifestyle. Per-niche sample sizes and YouTube-specific splits were **not** published in this table. These amounts describe negotiated contracts **conditional on acceptance**, not paid invoices, unique creators, gross annual earnings, net profits, or the probability a new creator secures a brand deal. The fee per 1,000 creator-average views uses historical account view benchmarks, not realized sponsored-post views.

**Measurement incompatibility:** ThoughtLeaders' category percentage is a share of *scanned YouTube long-form uploads with promotional reads*; Kolect's niche median is a *cross-platform agreed collaboration fee*. Neither measures brand spending by content category or creator-year net profit. Do not divide one by the other, combine them in a market share, or infer niche causation.

## What remains to complete the sponsorship economics

The project still lacks audited contract values, a representative frame of new/unsponsored creators, sponsor budgets, Shorts sponsorship rates, creator acquisition and editing costs, repeat-deal renewal information and before-tax **net cash profit per creator**. Each remains `null`. The study's associated task is not complete until representative paid-deal amounts and true denominators can be estimated or the scope is explicitly narrowed and verified.
