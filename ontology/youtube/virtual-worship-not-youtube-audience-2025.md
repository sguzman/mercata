# Religious-service video use: broad online/TV survey is not YouTube

**Checked:** 2026-10-25. Original evidence: [Pew Research Center's religious attendance study](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/02/26/religious-attendance-and-congregational-involvement/) and the [2023–24 Religious Landscape Study methodology](https://www.pewresearch.org/2025/02/26/religious-landscape-study-methodology/).

Pew conducted the nationally representative Religious Landscape Study in **English and Spanish** among **36,908 U.S. adults** between **July 17, 2023 and March 4, 2024**, primarily using online or mail questionnaires with a smaller phone mode. The national sampling margin of error is **±0.8 percentage points**; this does not apply to all subgroups or to cross-survey comparisons.

On a question about **watching religious services online or on television**, **23%** said at least monthly (16% weekly or more, plus 7% once/twice a month). Another 11% reported a few times a year, and 65% seldom or never. Some categories do not sum exactly because of rounding or nonresponse.

**This question does not mention YouTube.** The measured services may be watched through multiple websites, television, apps, and videos. No platform is individually enumerated, so there is no defensible YouTube-only attendance percentage, unique religious-content audience, or creator earnings estimate.

## Why not join the 2026 YouTube survey?

[Pew's separate 2026 social media use survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) measured whether U.S. adults ever used YouTube; its population, fieldwork and question differ. Without a matched respondent-level joint cross-tab, multiplying broad worship prevalence by YouTube-ever-use prevalence fabricates a platform-specific estimate. Similarly, watching a religious sermon does not identify the viewer's religious faith, the channel's religious affiliation, or engagement by a uniquely counted person.

## Publication and privacy

Only published aggregate statistics are retained. No user, channel subscriber, comment author, or inferred sensitive religious identity is recorded. Platform attendance, breakdown by affiliation, viewer counts and revenue remain **unknown**. The 2014 religious attendance wording and telephone-only fieldwork were different, so these observations cannot be treated as a directly comparable historical YouTube time series.
