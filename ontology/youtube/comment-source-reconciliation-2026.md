# Comment-study source discrepancy and incompatible sentiment samples

**Verified October 25, 2026.** [Machine reconciliation record](comment-source-reconciliation-2026.json) · [original two-study descriptions](comment-norms-evidence.json). This review corrected a reversed statement in Mercata's earlier source summary.

## 2025 political YouTube study: which commenter group is more toxic?

The [Cardiff bibliographic abstract](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/176263/) says more toxicity was found among infrequent commenters. But the [authors' journal-formatted manuscript](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/176263/8/Manuscript.pdf), **page 1 abstract and page 21 body**, and its **Figure 11 on page 22**, all report **higher average classifier-coded toxicity among prolific commenters** in the selected sample. The authors' institutional summary also agrees with the full text. This is a source-variant contradiction, not an independent replication.

Mercata now follows the **full text and supporting plot** for this narrow directional claim, records the contradictory abstract, and does not publish an all-platform or individually predictive toxicity rate.

The authors report about **2,940 selected channels**, about **2.5 million videos**, **over 320 million comments**, and **over 17 million comment authors** in the collection; the code-based scores use a more limited random comment subset. The study reports that approximately **half the sampled comments** came from **just over 2% of observed commenters**. The vast majority of viewers do not comment, so this is not a viewer opinion or voter census.

## 2026 climate attitudes study: a different question

The independent [Frontiers in Climate article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/climate/articles/10.3389/fclim.2026.1803829/full) studied **129 selected videos**, drawn from March 2021 ranked search results, and manually labeled **2,580 comments** (20 per video) to train/validate the coding process. Its reported higher positive attitudes in threaded conversations is an **observational association**, not proof that participation improves the behavior of YouTube commenters generally.

## Comparability and privacy

One study describes political-channel **comment frequency and toxicity**, the other coded **climate message attitudes** within heavily filtered videos. They do not share sampling frames, classifier targets, time windows, viewers, or an identified causal intervention. Do not subtract or pool their percentages, infer individual political affiliation, or create direct-message targeting lists. Mercata records no commenter IDs, personal comments, or raw histories.

**Correction scope:** only the erroneous direction, fuller public bounds and source-conflict documentation changed. No personal data or new marketplace specimen was added.
