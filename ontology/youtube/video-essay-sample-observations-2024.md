# Video essays: platform view events versus a curated criticism sample

**Checked:** 2026-10-25. Sources: YouTube Culture & Trends, [The joy of video essays](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/the-joy-of-video-essays/), published April 16, 2025, and British Film Institute *Sight and Sound*, [Best video essays of 2024](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/polls/best-video-essays-2024), published December 18, 2024.

## Two legitimate—but incompatible—measurement frames

YouTube reports **more than 200 million global views in calendar 2024 of videos related to video essays**. These are views, not unique people, distinct video essays, validly monetized impressions or creator dollars. The platform did not disclose a complete public query or denominator for independently reproducing its genre grouping.

The BFI's expert-curated poll included **47 voters from 17 countries**, **256 nominations of 183 unique works**, and **165 named creators or teams** from 32 countries. This is a selected set of works that experts regarded highly, **not** a random sample of YouTube channels or uploads.

| BFI curated observation | Result | What it means |
|---|---:|---|
| All selected works' average runtime | 30 minutes | Cross-platform selected works, not viewing duration |
| YouTube-published selected works | 70 | Selected uploads, not all video essays on YouTube |
| Selected YouTube works' average runtime | 36 minutes | Duration among BFI choices, not typical YouTube duration |
| Vimeo-published selected works | 56 | Other publication route; cross-posting can occur |
| Selected Vimeo works' average runtime | 11 minutes | Selected works, not platform-wide session depth |
| Selected YouTube works with external supporter links | More than 70% | Presence of Patreon/Ko-fi links, **not** actual subscriber payments |
| Languages in curated poll | At least 14 | Language diversity of nominees, not language-market share |

A video essay typically develops a thesis through interpretation, narration, criticism or montaged footage; a documentary primarily observes, narrates or compiles evidence, while a short explainer targets quick instruction. These are **working research codes**, not mandatory YouTube product categories. A film critique may also be a video essay; one work can cross categories, so raw category totals need a clear deduplication rule.

## How to measure an actual channel cohort

Pre-register a finite publication window, a discovery method that does not select on success, a video-level sample unit, public timestamps, genre and language coding, video duration, publication cadence and fixed-date public view events. Use separate owner-authorized reports for actual watch minutes and repeat viewers. Production time requires creator diaries or work logs; creator earnings require receipts and costs. None are present in these two published sources.

The BFI poll does **not** report a typical essayist's time cost, paid fan conversion, YouTube CPM or income distribution. More than 70% including external supporter links is not proof the links earned money. Similarly 200 million view events cannot be divided by 70 nominated videos to invent mean per-video reach.

## An independent subsequent *curated* year: BFI 2025 poll

The [British Film Institute's December 17, 2025 *Sight and Sound* poll](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/polls/best-video-essays-2025) records **72 invited voters** and **255 video essays mentioned** in the ninth annual edition. It groups participation into three **editorially defined ecosystems**, each with its own discovery and prestige conventions:

| 2025 BFI invited community | Voters | Works mentioned within community |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Academic and film/media criticism | 34 | 94 |
| Festivals, galleries and museums | 17 | 65 |
| Social media and YouTube-oriented contributors | 21 | 96 |

The 255 mentioned works are **poll selections**, not a YouTube video census, genre subscriber base, unique viewing audience or channel publication frequency. Some works are exhibited at festivals or galleries and are not released on open websites; cross-community work identity deduplication is not established here. The art-film category is also not identical to a YouTube documentary or review genre.

**No 2024→2025 platform-growth conclusion follows.** The [2024 BFI poll](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/polls/best-video-essays-2024) cited 47 voters, 256 nominations, and 183 distinct works, while the 2025 poll uses 72 voters and 255 works mentioned. Different voter counts, changed rosters, nomination methods and exhibition/online availability prevent treating the change in nominated works as a growth rate in video-essay uploads, active creators or audience demand.

The YouTube company's >200 million *2024 related view events* remain another measurement family entirely. These curatorial reports cannot establish 2025 YouTube watch minutes, nominated essay revenues, media labor requirements, typical video lengths or creator income. The added machine-readable 2025 poll frame preserves those missing values as `null`.
