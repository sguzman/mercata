# Public video-reference graph for studying cross-channel memes

**Research date: October 25, 2026.** [Machine evidence and privacy protocol](public-meme-reference-graph-2026.json) · [pure graph validator](../../site/public-reference-graph.mjs).

A YouTube video may explicitly link, credit, remix or formally collaborate with another video. Those are public **content relationships**, not measurements of how private viewers, subscribers or buyers move between channels. The official [Collaborations feature](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16554898?hl=en) provides accepted visible collaborator credits without automatically dividing ad revenue or guaranteeing recommendations.

The new validator requires a public source, dated eligible videos and a real public reference; it rejects impossible predecessor chronology for link/remix references, duplicates and unverifiable edges. It reports descriptive directed-edge counts and a linking-video share for a **provided selected set**. The included test graph is entirely synthetic; no actual content, comment handles or user histories have been collected.

The [2025 Culture & Trends examples](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/end-of-year-summary-2025/) and [March 2025 meme editorial](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/trending-now-march-2025/) motivate case definitions but are not a random creator sample or proof of original authorship. A future real study must declare eligible videos and country/language/topic/time sampling, show unlinked and missing videos, honor rights, and avoid private-person inference. The graph cannot identify who first invented a meme, deduplicated human audience transfer, earnings or a causal recommendation effect.
