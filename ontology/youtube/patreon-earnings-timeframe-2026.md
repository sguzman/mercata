# YouTube-linked Patreon earnings: real selected monthly distributions, not YouTube payouts

**Published peer-reviewed 2026 research, reviewed October 24, 2026.** [Machine source/units](patreon-earnings-timeframe-2026.json); [tests](../../tests/patreon-earnings-timeframe.test.mjs).

Strauss, Yang and Mazzucato's [*“Rich-get-richer”? Platform attention and earnings inequality using Patreon earnings data*](https://doi.org/10.1093/icc/dtag044) used Patreon monthly income data purchased from the payment service, for **March 2018, March 2021 and March 2024**, restricted to creators with **more than $10 Patreon monthly earnings** who list **only one external social-platform affiliation** (or Patreon-only). Some nondisclosed Patreon earnings are model-imputed. The same source is already included in Mercata's [creator-income evidence](creator-income-evidence.json): this is a **deeper examination of that one data family**, not another independent dataset.

## YouTube-affiliated Patreon earnings by March cross section

| Month | Observations | Monthly Patreon mean | Median |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| March 2018 | 2,143 | $199 | $44 |
| March 2021 | 6,835 | $280 | $53 |
| March 2024 | 14,457 | $243 | $43 |
| **Pooled across three cross sections** | **23,435** | **$250** | **$47** |

The pooled sample's 25th and 75th percentiles were **$28 and $119** per Patreon month. The much higher mean than median shows a skewed **selected Patreon income** distribution, not a platform-wide YouTube creator distribution. These March cohorts are not the same longitudinal people and therefore do not prove each creator's income declined.

## Same Patreon payment mechanism, different primary external affiliations

The same paper's March 2024 single-affiliation table reports: YouTube median $43 (n=14,457), Twitch $42 (n=870), Instagram $53 (n=20,876), Facebook $44 (n=2,520), Twitter/X $72 (n=23,966), and Patreon-only $55 (n=37,961). These amounts exclude native ad payments, YouTube memberships, merchandise, sponsors, taxes and production costs. Sample composition, category, NSFW share and imputation can differ across affiliations; these are **not** causal platform comparisons, nor income forecasts.

The paper qualitatively finds particularly concentrated Patreon earnings tails in **Animation** and **Podcasts**, while **Music, Comics and Writing** appear less tail-concentrated **when pooling across all social platforms**. It does not furnish a YouTube-specific Patreon median or membership conversion percentage for those communities, and we do not invent one.

**Still unmeasured:** non-Patreon creators, earners under $10, actual native YouTube ad payouts, all-channel membership purchase rates, verified net labor-hour wages, zero-earner prevalence, costs and causal effects of platform recommendations. Patreon creator affiliation is not a census of YouTube's buyers, unique audience, or profitable channels.
