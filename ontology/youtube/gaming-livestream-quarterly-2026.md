The publisher reports **−19.3% Q2 quarterly gaming watched-hour growth**, **−18.9% year on year**, with Q2 gaming channel counts **−17.9% quarter on quarter**. Approximate published totals are rounded, so naive subtraction from two-decimal totals may yield slightly different percentages. A related [2025 annual Stream Hatchet report](https://hatchet.gg/resources/guides/2025-youtube-gaming-live-streaming-trends/) reported 8.8 billion 2025 Gaming watched hours. Do not add that annual period to its Q4 subset.

The Q2 report newly includes **general YouTube Live** in comparisons and reports **12.97 billion watched hours**, up 2.7% quarter-on-quarter, separately from gaming. Categories, inclusion and overlap must be documented before computing combined YouTube Live shares. Gaming watched hours are not viewer counts, streaming-channel IDs are not distinct creators, and hours broadcast are not production labor hours.

## Independent Q3 2026 Streams Charts observation (not an extension of the Stream Hatchet series)

[Streams Charts' October 13, 2026 report](https://streamscharts.com/news/q3-2026-global-livestreaming-landscape) publishes approximately **1.98 billion YouTube Gaming watched hours** in Q3 2026, with gaming accounting for **14.5%** of all *YouTube livestream* watched hours in that vendor's observation. Streams Charts separately reports **11.3%** for the analogous Q2 share. It also reports **13.62 billion** YouTube livestream hours in Q3 and **31.698 billion** hours across the livestreaming services it tracks.

These are a **different commercial provider's classifications and collection system**, not a fourth entry in the Stream Hatchet Q4 2025–Q2 2026 time series above. Do **not** infer a true 1.79B → 1.98B watched-hours change by directly comparing Stream Hatchet Q2 with Streams Charts Q3; their individual stream samples, regional platform coverage and categorization equivalence are not demonstrated. Within Streams Charts, the 11.3% and 14.5% figures describe a *share of the vendor-tracked YouTube livestream hours*, not the probability a viewer watches gaming, a gaming share of all YouTube video time, or an observed paid creator fraction.

Streams Charts attributes some Q3 gaming viewing to major esports tournaments but does not provide a randomized or matched causal game-title effect. Unique gaming people, individual creators, streaming wages, advertiser payouts and genre-level profitability remain unknown.

Actual unique viewers, monetized channels, net profits, country and genre splits, or actual transfers to Twitch/Kick are **unknown**. These figures support a time-specific live gaming study, not a general claim that YouTube usage, gaming uploads or income fell in Q2.
