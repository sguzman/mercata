# YouTube branded-content disclosures: platform rules versus U.S. endorsement guidance

**Research snapshot: October 23, 2026.** This is a public-policy research comparison, not a legal opinion or a completed worldwide jurisdiction audit. [Machine-readable rules and unknowns](brand-disclosure-risk.json).

## Three different compliance layers

| Layer | What the cited source supports | Not established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [YouTube branded-content policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/17596007?hl=en) | Declare covered paid or in-kind brand relationships in Studio. Platform policy covers Shorts, recorded video, live, descriptions, comments and other YouTube features. | That the platform label alone satisfies every applicable national law or product-category restriction |
| [U.S. FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) and [Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) | A material relationship can involve pay, free items, discounts, employment, family or commissions; whether disclosure is needed and sufficient depends on U.S. consumers' understanding and context. | A one-size-fits-all mandatory phrase, a universal monetary threshold, or a safe harbor from an automated badge |
| Non-U.S. advertising law | YouTube itself reminds creators to check applicable local jurisdictions, age and product categories. | Country-wide requirements for Mexico, UK, EU or every target audience, which require separate primary-law review |

**Do not conflate the creator's country with the viewer's country.** FTC guidance says U.S. law can apply to material endorsements posted abroad where effects on U.S. consumers are reasonably foreseeable. That does not mean all non-U.S. posts are U.S.-regulated or that the FTC rules are worldwide law.

## Dated September 2026 YouTube platform change

[TeamYouTube's September 3, 2026 announcement](https://support.google.com/youtube/blog/458476782/giving-you-tools-to-easily-disclose-paid-partnerships-with-new-controls-automated-disclosures) describes three developments: refreshed viewer-facing branded-content labels, manual age/geography controls for branded content, and an automated detection/labeling system being rolled out over subsequent months. The company simultaneously restated the creator's legal disclosure obligations. **The announcement does not establish that every account already has every control or that automatic detection is accurate.**

[YouTube's branded-content policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/17596007?hl=en) says Studio declaration produces a paid-promotion label, but possible automatic labeling by platform detection does not substitute for creator review or underlying disclosure duties. It separately permits some YPP advertising on eligible branded videos, may adjust ads conflicting with sponsor brands, and states that branded videos are removed from the **YouTube Kids app**. Removal from Kids is not deletion from ordinary YouTube.

## Relationships and disclosure surfaces

| Relationship | Research interpretation | Additional questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sponsor pays for a promotion | Declare platform branded content and evaluate conspicuous material-connection disclosure. | Exact deal terms, countries, product rules and audience |
| Free product or other consideration | Payment is not the only material connection; a relevant free product can matter. | Context, expectations and whether the benefit would affect consumer evaluation |
| Affiliate commission | Commission may need a clear explanation near the linked endorsement; the bare phrase "affiliate link" may be insufficient. | Whether the recommendation actually earns a commission; local law and surface |
| Creator promotes their own product | Ownership should be apparent when it would affect U.S. viewers' understanding. | Whether the relationship is already obvious |
| Independent review without any material brand relationship | Do not fabricate a sponsored relationship or disclosure duty. | Actual relationship and evidence; circumstances can change |

FTC staff guidance distinguishes **in-video** disclosures from a description that many viewers never open. For a video endorsement, a disclosure solely in the description is inadequate; an audio-plus-visual presentation is often easier for viewers to notice. For **livestreams**, repeat disclosures periodically for viewers arriving midstream. These are context-sensitive recommendations from the FTC, not an automated legal compliance score.

## Risk and economics

Policy review is required before estimating sponsor gross receipts versus net cash profit: contract negotiation, content rights, review labor, ad-category checks, regional age exclusions and regulatory compliance can affect both reachable viewers and creator costs. At present Mercata has **no measured typical sponsorship amount, disclosure violation rate, compliance expense, contract prevalence, or net sponsor profit**. The structured record keeps them `null`.
