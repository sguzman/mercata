# Creator publishing inactivity is not creator attrition

**Source review: October 25, 2026.** [Machine-readable, window-specific evidence](creator-inactivity-frames-2026.json). This extends the preexisting [indexed channel cohort work](channel-cohort-observations-2026.json) while keeping newcomer and incumbent populations separate.

| Evidence frame | Sample | Activity or inactivity observation | Why it is not an exit rate |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| [Click Analytic 2026 creator report](https://www.clickanalytic.com/creator-economy-report/youtube/) | Approximately **1.9 million** indexed YouTube accounts with 10k+ subscribers, snapshot covering 2025 | **43%** vendor-defined active in last 30 days; **57%** otherwise dormant | No verified permanent exit or closure; publisher activity definition, exact sample count and window date are not fully published |
| [Independent 2026 company-channel census](https://heidarrudyi.com/blog/company-youtube-channel) | **3,375** company channels with videos, selected from **18,284** company sites checked Aug 31 | **46%** no public upload in last 90 days; **27%** no upload in a year; **18.6%** in two years; **14.3%** in three | Company-owned, website-discoverable channels are not individuals or necessarily failed creator businesses |
| [vidIQ's 360-day snapshot](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-uploads-1000-subscribers/) | **32,926,088** tracked channels with under 1k baseline subscribers and at least one earlier public video, both endpoints visible | **27,360,211** had *zero net change* in public video count | Net count can stay zero despite real uploads and removals; selection excludes negative changes and absent histories |

## Specific boundaries

The Click Analytic publication describes its 2025 snapshot and approximate 1.9 million-account sample; it does not provide enough public detail to identify first-time creators, channel owners or exact attrition mechanisms. Its **57% dormant** is publisher-defined *recent nonactivity*, not verified account deletion.

The independent company study started from company websites (rather than recommended YouTube channels), but its sample conditions on reachable webpages, accessible linked channels and at least one public video. The **46% without a recent upload** is a **90-day** statement for this selected company cohort. The **27% without a yearly upload** is a **one-year** statement for the same cohort; do not sum the nested inactivity windows. The report's speculative claims about advertisement distribution are not used as proven facts in this research.

The vidIQ dataset tracks publicly visible video-count differences between two endpoints **360 days apart**, and restricts its selected sample to nonnegative changes. The **27.36 million** no-change count is **not** the number of channels that did not publish. Existing or disappeared channels with missing histories may be excluded.

## Genuine creator turnover acceptance gate

To measure exit risk, an independently defined entrant cohort needs actual first publication dates, consecutive upload records, censoring rules for inaccessible or deleted channels, reason for stopping where legitimately known, and follow-up intervals. It also needs receipts and labor records to distinguish a channel continuing unpaid work from an income-generating business. None of these datasets supplies a representative exit probability or a creator profit trend.

Unknown: all-platform closure share, first-year entrant exit probability, content niche-specific survival, never-posted channels, wages, and causes of inactivity. The samples cannot be pooled or turned into a causal strategy recommendation.
