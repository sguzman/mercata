# YouTube television versus smartphone viewing — a bounded UK comparison

**Study source:** [Barb's July 31, 2025 public release](https://www.barb.co.uk/news/barb-starts-reporting-tv-set-viewing-to-youtube-channels-in-world-first-initiative/), with an independent [Ofcom Media Nations 2026 release](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-adults/nations-media-habits-revealed-in-annual-ofcom-report) for longer-run context. [Machine-readable observations](screen-viewing-uk-2025.json).

## Observed device split, not unique people

Barb measured YouTube viewing **at home through home Wi-Fi** in the United Kingdom in **Q2 2025**. Among people **aged 16+**, **43% of measured YouTube viewing** was on television sets, compared with **32% on smartphones**. Among **children 4–15**, television sets accounted for **53% of measured YouTube viewing**. Barb does not provide the comparable smartphone percentage for the child subgroup in this cited release.

| UK audience group | TV-set share of in-home Wi-Fi YouTube viewing | Smartphone share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Age 16+ | 43% | 32% |
| Age 4–15 | 53% | Unknown in cited release |

The percentages measure **viewing on devices within the reported sample**, **not** fractions of distinct people, accounts or households. They do not cover out-of-home use, YouTube watched via cellular networks away from home Wi-Fi, or a worldwide population. Children remain part of a published aggregate measurement; nothing here identifies or profiles any child.

## Distinguish two Barb methods

The same July 2025 news release also describes a separate **200 selected YouTube channels** TV-set audience reporting initiative. Barb uses audio matching/automatic content recognition (ACR) on TV sets to identify viewing of those selected channels. The **channel-specific ACR sample excludes** Shorts, live streams, music/karaoke and some clip-only channels, among other conditions.

**Those exclusions are for the selected-channel identification program, not evidence that all-service smartphone/TV shares exclude those video formats.** Do not assign a TV-viewing percentage to Shorts versus livestreams from this release.

## Comparable longitudinal context: TV grows within in-home viewing

Ofcom's [July 29, 2026 release](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-adults/nations-media-habits-revealed-in-annual-ofcom-report), drawing on Barb in-home measures, reports average daily YouTube viewing on TV sets increased from **9 minutes per person in 2022** to **19 minutes in 2025**; all connected home devices rose from **33 to 41 minutes per person per day** across those years.

These are same-source in-home time series but their **all-device totals include television**. Adding the 19 TV minutes to 41 all-device minutes would double-count. The separately reported [Online Nation 2025 non-TV 51-minute statistic](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/online-habits/from-apps-to-ai-search-how-the-uk-goes-online-in-2025) comes from a different measurement framework and must not be added mechanically either.

## Still unknown

Neither source measures television versus smartphone **creator RPM**, conversion to subscriptions, first-year channel success rates, individual niche preference by device, production costs, or screen-specific monetized profit. The machine file records those as `null`.

This bounded comparison is useful for identifying a **device research dimension**, not choosing a channel strategy based only on viewer screen. Its population, time window, Wi-Fi restriction and service/channel methodological distinction must accompany every quotation.
