# YouTube ontology — audiences, content and earning

The independent [machine model](model.json) defines **64 concepts** and **23 relationships**. [Policy ledger](policies.json) captures cited eligibility, platform-fee and child-directed restrictions. [Measurement dictionary](measurements.json) explains which statistics exist, what they measure and whether the public can access them. The [topic taxonomy](taxonomy.json) describes **28 candidate overlapping subcultures**, without pretending their populations have been measured.

**Source snapshot: 2026-10-23.** Contemporary policy assertions refer to the verified official links in [YouTube's dossier](../../specimens/youtube.json). These documents distinguish official facts from research definitions, models and unknown economic estimates.

## Participants, accounts and audiences

A viewer, registered account, channel, subscriber, publisher, monetized creator, view and advertising impression are not interchangeable units. One person can operate channels; subscribers may never watch; viewers can be unregistered. The rolling 28-day unique audience of a channel must not be called YouTube's monthly global user base.

Platform demographics (age, sex, gender, race or ethnicity, political affiliation, country and language) require survey or platform-wide denominators. A creator's authorized YouTube Analytics may give age, gender or country for that channel but does not make a population census. Demographic reporting can be suppressed.

## Community ontology

The 28 topic definitions include beauty, feminine lifestyle, youth culture, child-directed animation, general animation, gaming, memes, civic/political commentary and other cultural interests. They are open, overlapping coding categories. Labels do not establish that any person belongs to a group, and a topic's video count is not a topic's number of unique viewers. Until an appropriate sampled or aggregate source is found, *every* audience and creator size estimate remains unknown.

Children's animation requires separating broadly suitable animation from material deliberately directed at children. That classification affects advertisement and social features under YouTube's rules, and individual children must not be profiled.

## Earning model

Use independent contracts and earning paths for ordinary Watch Page ads, pooled Shorts Feed advertising, viewer-funded features, subscriptions, and external products or sponsors. The [policy ledger](policies.json) defines distinct application thresholds, share bases and content restrictions. Neither 55%, 45% nor 70% is a guaranteed percentage of total viewer spending or total video views. Creator earnings depend on monetized playbacks, audience, geography, eligibility and other product-specific circumstances. Costs, time, taxes and revenue volatility must be analyzed separately.

## Valid evidence

All claim records require official or independently credible sources, observations dates and limitations. Public API statistics are not the same as authorized creator metrics. Statistical inference from samples requires a recorded sampling method, sample size, selection mechanism, exclusions and appropriate uncertainty statement. No individual's political affiliation, sex or ethnicity should be guessed.

The [399-task YouTube research inventory](../../plans/youtube.md) remains the work ledger. This ontology is the vocabulary for research, not a substitute for doing it.

## How discovery works

See the [officially documented search and recommendation surfaces](discovery.md). Search ranking, personalized Home, Up Next and Shorts are different ways audiences find content. Creator RPM and advertiser CPM use different denominators; no per-view revenue rate is assumed.

## Researching communities without inventing their sizes

The [public YouTube sampling protocol](public-sampling.md) defines topic inclusion rules, legal data access, stratified sampling, observed channel counts, demographic survey denominators, missingness, and the August 2026 break in public video view-count definitions. It must be followed before treating any topic sample as representative of people or earnings.

## Measured content activity

The [topic evidence register](topic-observations.md) records 14 observations from YouTube publications, including global gaming views, topic-title-filtered beauty or format views, news watch hours, and animation fan-survey results. The [machine version](topic-observations.json) includes each source, geography, period, unit and limitation. These measures reveal **content activity**, not unique community population. All 28 topic population estimates remain unknown.

## What creators earn

The first [creator-income evidence review](creator-income-evidence.md) examines a peer-reviewed study of **Patreon earnings for YouTube-affiliated Patreon creators**, rather than YouTube advertising or total creator income. The selected sample's $47 monthly median and $250 mean show why definitions and sample exclusions matter. The [machine evidence](creator-income-evidence.json) preserves period, currency, quartiles, coverage, omitted non-earners, imputation and other limits. Typical total YouTube creator income is **still unknown**.

## Newly monetized small channels

AIR Media-Tech's [297-channel studio-data report](small-channel-income.md) contains six monthly earnings observations and genre and top-viewer-country breakdowns. Its [machine dataset](small-channel-income.json) separates the sample of already monetized channels from the broader platform. It reports a USD 88 median in the first full month and USD 32 in month six, but neither figure is a representative expectation for creators who have not reached monetization. The commercial source's recruitment criteria and sample limitations remain visible alongside every breakdown.
