# YouTube ontology — audiences, content and earning

The independent [machine model](model.json) defines **184 concepts** and **175 relationships**. [Policy ledger](policies.json) captures cited eligibility, platform-fee and child-directed restrictions. [Measurement dictionary](measurements.json) explains which statistics exist, what they measure and whether the public can access them. The [topic taxonomy](taxonomy.json) describes **32 candidate overlapping subcultures**, without pretending their populations have been measured.

**Source snapshot: 2026-10-23.** Contemporary policy assertions refer to the verified official links in [YouTube's dossier](../../specimens/youtube.json). These documents distinguish official facts from research definitions, models and unknown economic estimates.

## Participants, accounts and audiences

A viewer, registered account, channel, subscriber, publisher, monetized creator, view and advertising impression are not interchangeable units. One person can operate channels; subscribers may never watch; viewers can be unregistered. The rolling 28-day unique audience of a channel must not be called YouTube's monthly global user base.

Platform demographics (age, sex, gender, race or ethnicity, political affiliation, country and language) require survey or platform-wide denominators. A creator's authorized YouTube Analytics may give age, gender or country for that channel but does not make a population census. Demographic reporting can be suppressed.

## Community ontology

The 32 topic definitions include beauty, feminine lifestyle, youth culture, child-directed animation, general animation, gaming, memes, civic/political commentary and other cultural interests. They are open, overlapping coding categories. Labels do not establish that any person belongs to a group, and a topic's video count is not a topic's number of unique viewers. Until an appropriate sampled or aggregate source is found, *every* audience and creator size estimate remains unknown.

Children's animation requires separating broadly suitable animation from material deliberately directed at children. That classification affects advertisement and social features under YouTube's rules, and individual children must not be profiled.

## Earning model

Use independent contracts and earning paths for ordinary Watch Page ads, pooled Shorts Feed advertising, viewer-funded features, subscriptions, and external products or sponsors. The [policy ledger](policies.json) defines distinct application thresholds, share bases and content restrictions. Neither 55%, 45% nor 70% is a guaranteed percentage of total viewer spending or total video views. Creator earnings depend on monetized playbacks, audience, geography, eligibility and other product-specific circumstances. Costs, time, taxes and revenue volatility must be analyzed separately.

## Valid evidence

All claim records require official or independently credible sources, observations dates and limitations. Public API statistics are not the same as authorized creator metrics. Statistical inference from samples requires a recorded sampling method, sample size, selection mechanism, exclusions and appropriate uncertainty statement. No individual's political affiliation, sex or ethnicity should be guessed.

The [399-task YouTube research inventory](../../plans/youtube.md) remains the work ledger. This ontology is the vocabulary for research, not a substitute for doing it.

## The August 2026 playback-start measurement break

The [dated first-frame view-count study](view-definition-break.md) and [metric bridge](view-definition-break.json) document YouTube's **August 24, 2026** switch to public views counted from first-frame playback starts. The change affects Shorts, ordinary videos, live and podcasts, but does not make public starts synonymous with engaged viewing, qualifying Partner Program views, monetized playbacks, thumbnail impressions or unique people. Pre/post public-view series are structurally incomparable without further adjustment; no conversion factor, income gain or artificial audience-growth estimate is invented.

## Announced February 2027 YouTube Partner Program changes

The [time-bounded 2027 YPP policy study](ypp-transition-2027.md) and [machine transition model](ypp-transition-2027.json) separate **October 2026 entry rules** from those taking effect **February 1, 2027**. New ads/Premium applicants will face announced thresholds of **8,000 qualifying public watch hours** (365 days) or **20 million qualifying Shorts views** (90 days), while preexisting YPP members are not subject to the higher entry barrier. A separate **rolling 10-million-qualified-Shorts-view gate** will control continued Shorts revenue sharing, without terminating long-form earning for channels that fall below it. Premium and Premium Lite have separate announced net subscription allocations, not fixed payouts. The 2027 new-applicant subscriber floor was not restated in the announcement and remains unknown. Current 2026 thresholds have not been silently overwritten.
