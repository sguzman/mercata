# Comment norms and participation: three bounded YouTube research cases

**Reviewed October 26, 2026.** [Machine findings and source restrictions](comment-norms-evidence.json). These are peer-reviewed **descriptions of selected communities**, not a study of every YouTube comment or a profiler of individual commenters.

## Case A: English-language political channels, published 2025

[Pressing play on politics: Quantitative description of YouTube](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/176263/), in the *Journal of Quantitative Description: Digital Media*, studies selected **anglophone politics channels**, their videos and their comments. The associated abstract reports that **approximately half of observed comments were written by just over 2% of commenters** in the sample. The linked author's full manuscript (pp. 1, 21–22) reports **higher classifier-coded toxicity among prolific commenters**, not among the infrequent group. Cardiff's bibliographic abstract states the reverse; the [full-text reconciliation](comment-source-reconciliation-2026.md) documents this conflict and the evidence supporting the corrected direction.

Those statements concern **activity concentration among observed commenters**, not the political distribution of the population. The full manuscript reports more than 17 million unique comment authors, over 320 million comments, about 2.5 million videos and 2,940 selected channels in its collected sample. The number represented by 'just over 2%' is not given as an exact percentage. An all-platform population average remains unknown. A commenter may post repeatedly and may watch other subjects; posting volume is not a vote, persuasion susceptibility, ideology or an honest representation of total audience opinion.

## Case B: selected climate-change videos, published April 2026

[Audience engagement with climate change content on YouTube](https://acml.teddylazebnik.com/publications/audience-engagement-climate-change-content-youtube-analysis/) uses **129 English-language YouTube videos**. The candidate set came from keyword search results collected in **March 2021** and required at least 200 comments, a high proportion of English-language comments, and alignment with the scientific consensus. These filters exclude many channels and viewpoints; they are part of the measured population, not a neutral platform-wide census.

The researchers categorized presenter types and video framing, then used a mix of manual coding and supervised text methods for attitudes. Within that selected set, **reply-thread discussions had a larger share of coded positive attitudes than independent top-level comments**. The study also reported differences by presenter and framing.

These are **observational associations**, not proof that inducing replies makes commenters kinder, or an endorsement of any particular persuasive message. The small topic-specific sample and automated classification conditions must accompany every citation.

## Case C: prerecorded Study With Me videos, published February 2026

The existing [Liu, Deng and Zhou study evidence](creator-collaboration-fandom-2026.md) examines **393 prerecorded YouTube videos** selected through a September 2024 title query for *study with me*, together with **164,611 public comment events**. The sampled videos had more than **400 million historical video views**, but views are not people, students or completed study sessions.

The authors combined comment topic modeling with automated sentiment classification. Their three broad topics concerned **video design and ambience**, **self-reported self-regulation**, and **social interaction and support**; they characterized **roughly half** of the selected visible comment messages as positive, without an independently validated exact positivity share. Mercata does **not** assign a numerical positive percentage or infer an individual user's emotion, academic progress or satisfaction from a machine-coded message.

This is a deliberately selected educational content/community sample, not the population of all YouTube students. Replies, silent viewers, moderators, repeated commenters, topic/language inclusion, older videos and users who leave no public comments are not reconciled to unique individuals. No student outcomes, creator revenue, causality or normal viewer participation rate was measured.

## Why the three studies must not be pooled

| Dimension | Anglophone political channels | Selected climate-change videos |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Population frame | English-language political channel sample | 2021 search-selected English-dominant consensus-aligned climate videos |
| Primary analytic contrast | Share of comments produced by more versus less prolific commenters | Coded attitudes in reply threads versus independent comments |
| Primary units | Commenters and comments (also channels/videos) | Video-level and comment-level coded attitudes |
| Reported numeric anchor | ~50% of comments from just over 2% of commenters | 129 selected videos; no pooled attitude percentage reproduced |
| Inference not supported | General political opinions or all YouTube commenters | General YouTube climate attitudes or causal effects of replies |

The Study With Me case differs again: its denominator is title-selected prerecorded education videos and their visible comments, not the political authorship concentration or the climate response-thread attitude measure. A numerical all-topic sentiment average cannot be constructed. Visible comments are also selected by moderation, content removals, creator policies, language, platform rankings and the propensity to speak. A large comment corpus is not a random sample of viewers. A repeated commenter can shape visible conversation but cannot be equated with multiple distinct people.

## Privacy and neutral descriptive work

Mercata stores **no commenter names, raw text, IDs, inferred sensitive identities, or minors' data**. It does not form ideological targeting segments or persuasive tactics. Future direct measurement would require lawfully acquired aggregate data, documented quota/method permissions, consistent sampling periods and matched denominator definitions, with published uncertainty and audit trails.

There is **no verified platform-wide toxicity rate, topic-by-topic norm ranking, population-wide comment frequency distribution, personal political-affiliation estimate, or financial payoff from community moderation**. Those fields stay `null`.

See the [creator safety study](creator-safety-continuity.md) for official moderation controls and [community overlap rules](audience-overlap.md) for distinct people versus comments and channels.
