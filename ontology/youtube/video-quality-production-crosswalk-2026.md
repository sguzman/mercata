# Observable quality signals and production-workload comparisons

**Study date: October 25, 2026.** [Machine evidence crosswalk](video-quality-production-crosswalk-2026.json). This extends the existing [production effort comparison](production-effort-comparison.md) and [three educational-video samples](educational-video-format-cohorts-2026.md); it does not introduce a new study population.

Video polish, documented labor, viewer attention, public engagement and actual educational effectiveness have different units. A cross-video quality score cannot be computed from these sources without defining a measured outcome and a comparable sample.

| Sample | Population and method | Observed signals | Not observed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Photography creator work diary](https://zenagainst.com/2025/09/15/how-long-to-make-a-youtube-video/) | **1 creator**, **61 completed videos**, self-recorded work hours | Median **7.37 hours**, mean **9.74 hours** per finished video; stage means by task | Non-published failures, cash income, standardized quality ratings |
| [Spanish-language STEM video sample](https://doi.org/10.1080/21548455.2026.2616740) | **124 videos** within one educational channel | Owner-side retention and average view duration by video type | Viewers' measured learning, randomized duration effects, work hours and profit |
| [Indonesian instructional and practice-test sample](https://journal.uny.ac.id/index.php/jitp/article/view/77488) | **139 of 218 videos** in two categories, one channel | View/share/like, watch-time and duration differences between categories | Representative learner quality, cross-channel causal differences and earnings |
| [Highly viewed Turkish mathematics sample](https://ijci.net/index.php/IJCI/article/view/1633) | **126 preselected highly viewed videos** | Public views, likes and comments; no significant reported duration association | Owner-only retention, labor, unsurfaced zero-view videos and actual cash receipts |

## Quantitative guardrails

The photography diary's rounded mean stage hours are **1.33** preproduction, **2.95** production, **4.87** postproduction, **0.43** publishing, **0.12** marketing and **0.03** other. These sum to **9.73**, while the author's independently rounded overall mean is **9.74**. The 0.01-hour gap is rounding, not new work to be imputed. The incomplete/unpublished-project denominator is unknown, so 9.74 hours is **not** a representative all-attempts or all-creator average.

The STEM and Indonesian samples have creator-access analytics; the Turkish study uses selected public counters. The results may differ without contradiction because measurement frames, video lengths, audiences and sampling methods are not interchangeable. None is a randomized test, general video-quality rating, representative earnings cohort or recommended publishing schedule.

## Matching protocol for research into differentiation

A future *observed* comparison must define its task-specific format, production stages, audio and caption accessibility, license/rightsholder checks, channel age, content language and topic, new versus established channel, and matched observation dates. Record production hours and cash outlays for **both completed and abandoned** projects. Keep direct learner assessments separate from traffic metrics, and expenses separate from earned or cleared payouts.

YouTube's [Content ID guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370?hl=en) makes media clearance an independently important production constraint; it does not measure production quality or audience value. Profit, universal ideal video length, a cross-sample quality index and causal improvements are **unknown**.
