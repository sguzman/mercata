# How to compare opportunities on YouTube

Mercata needs to answer a harder question than "which category gets the most views?": **What can an independent creator make, and what would it cost in cash, time, legal exposure and lost alternatives?**

This is a [machine-readable research protocol](opportunity-framework.json), not a leaderboard. All 33 topics are currently unranked because their distinct viewers, active competing channels, median economic returns and cost distributions have not been measured on comparable bases.

## Ten dimensions of an opportunity

**Audience demand.** Separate distinct viewers, engaged views, watch hours, search interest and country; topic's published activity is not its population.

**Creator supply.** Counts of active channels, upload volume and concentration, with sample selection documented.

**New-channel discoverability.** Impressions, clicks and discovery distribution for comparable newly publishing channels, not only incumbents.

**Revenue options.** Ads, Premium, Shopping, sponsorship, support, product sales and services must be broken out.

**Creator income distribution.** Mean, median, dispersion, non-earners, cohort composition and time to first payout, separated by revenue channel.

**Cash cost.** Include upfront charges, ongoing tools, rights, equipment, returns and promotion as applicable.

**Labor and difficulty.** Hours for idea research, writing, art, filming, editing, uploads, outreach and support.

**Content-format fit.** Separate Shorts, long-form, live, serial releases and localization; each has distinct demand and earnings mechanics.

**Policy and rights.** Child-directed classification, ad suitability, copyright, seller disclosures, age rules and regional access.

**Durability and portability.** Evergreen demand versus short-lived interest, audience reach outside YouTube and ability to own resulting products.

## A valid comparison

1. **Describe an audience need.** Pick specific topic and content format, geography, language, target buyer/interest and timescale before collecting numbers.
2. **Measure demand and competing supply.** Compare at least two independent measures when possible and explain selection bias, duplicated content and cohort limitations.
3. **Build a channel-specific cost and cash-flow scenario.** Enter observed or explicitly assumed ad-only earnings, other net receipts, labor and expense assumptions, with uncertainty intervals.
4. **Write a small, reversible content experiment.** Specify outputs, labor ceiling, observation window, publishing cadence, metric thresholds and a decision date.
5. **Assess competing opportunities.** Choose a plan only after displaying uncertain profit-per-hour ranges and the main unobserved risks. Otherwise publish 'insufficient evidence'.

A candidate must retain its scope: for example, a 90-day sample of Spanish-language long-form software tutorials has a different demand, production cost and revenue audience from a global Shorts comedy trend. Comparing raw view totals across them provides no reliable expected profit.

For any creator-level niche scenario, inventory [cash expenses, existing equipment and work hours separately](production-costs.md) before aggregating them. Costs of abandoned drafts and all promotion work belong in a defined entry cohort; successful videos alone are a selected sample. Cash-profit-per-hour arithmetic does not itself price unpaid labor as a cash expense.

## Economic arithmetic

Ad-only creator revenue may be *modeled* as matched paid views multiplied by a similarly scoped ad-only yield divided by 1,000. Other net receipts are then included **only when not already counted**, and attributable cash costs deducted. Dividing by all work hours gives a hypothetical cash return per hour. A time value may be added as a separate opportunity cost; this must be labeled to prevent double deduction.

No default CPM, conversion rate, creator income or hours per video is permitted. Zero and "unknown" are different states. A cash-profit scenario based on assumptions is not a prediction or observed distribution.

A new [audio rights and claims register](music-rights-economics.md) provides a concrete accounting stress test: track-specific music revenue sharing affects net receipts, while a Content ID dispute can defer cash without causing a final loss. The August 2026 Creator Music paid-license sunset means prior cost schedules must not be silently reused for a current creator scenario. Until scoped cohorts provide licensing invoices, channel reports and labor logs, rights-related cost medians remain unknown.

## Source integrity and independent publishing

The [14-plus official topic activity observations](topic-observations.md) show genuine viewing activity for selected topic-title sets, games and news, but no number of distinct people in any community. The [297-channel monetization sample](small-channel-income.md) is restricted to already monetized channels, not all people who attempt to build a channel.

A proposed content experiment must specify an audience problem, topic, format, cash/time ceiling and acceptance metric before it begins. **Writing such a plan does not authorize spending money or publishing videos.** Distribution and monetization decisions require new evidence, especially around child-directed rules, copyrighted content, and viewer-country restrictions.

## How scoring works

No hidden weighted total. Each evidence dimension stores its observation, population and confidence, plus missing inputs. All scores and weights begin `null`. A ranking or recommendation cannot be produced until its inputs are defensible and uncertainties visible. Cross-category comparisons should acknowledge that many opportunities cannot be ranked from public data alone.
