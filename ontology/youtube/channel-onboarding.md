# YouTube creator onboarding and feature access

**Document snapshot: October 23, 2026.** [Machine-readable pathway](channel-onboarding.json). This study separates the objects needed to watch, publish, unlock advanced tools and earn through YouTube. These are **different permissions**, not a single sign-up milestone.

## Six different steps

| Stage | What is possible | Access requirement or barrier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Google Account | Sign in, watch, like and subscribe | Does **not** automatically create a publishing channel |
| 2. YouTube channel | Establish a personal or Brand Account channel | Supervised account limitations; Brand Account can have multiple managers |
| 3. Standard features | Initial video and Shorts uploads, within daily limits | Active Community Guidelines strikes can restrict access |
| 4. Intermediate features | Longer videos, thumbnails and certain live tools | Phone number verification by SMS or voice; other product rules still apply |
| 5. Advanced features | Pinned comments and higher limits, among other features | Phone verification **and** sufficient channel history, or ID/video verification where available |
| 6. Native creator earnings | Potential approval for YPP and specific monetization modules | **Separate** subscribers, watch/Shorts metrics, country, review, acceptance and payment requirements |

Sources: [create a channel](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1646861?hl=en), [feature access table](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9890437?hl=en), [advanced verification](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9891124?hl=en), [Partner Program eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en).

## Roles, ownership and verification

A Google Account can use YouTube without a publishing channel. YouTube's channel creation instructions distinguish a personal publishing identity from a Brand Account with multiple managers or owners; **only the primary owner** is eligible for advanced ID or video verification. General access to a Brand Account as a manager does not prove ownership or advanced-feature authorization. <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1646861?hl=en>

Intermediate feature access normally follows phone verification. The [official verification instructions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9891124?hl=en) describe a code sent by SMS or voice. Phone verification alone does not grant the advanced tier. Advanced access additionally needs sufficient channel history or eligible ID/video verification. The latter is not available to every creator. A person can build channel history instead of providing ID or video verification; there is no universal fixed period guaranteeing it.

Access can later be restricted for guideline violations or inadequate channel history. The [feature table](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9890437?hl=en) describes separate daily limits and product-specific requirements, which should never be merged with Partner Program earning qualifications. Computer live streaming and mobile live streaming also have different restrictions.

## Upload is not publish

The [Studio upload guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/57407?hl=en) explains file upload, metadata and visibility. An uploaded private/unlisted video is not a universally public video. Scheduling has separate conditions. The video file, the published post, counted viewing events and monetized playbacks must remain distinct statistical units.

## A conditional zero-cash-outlay path

Using an existing eligible account, owned hardware, connectivity and lawful audio/video assets, someone may be able to publish an initial video without buying new tools. That is **not evidence** that entering YouTube costs nothing. Data connectivity, owned equipment, labor hours, storage, copyright clearance, software and opportunity costs all exist; their amounts may be zero, paid earlier, or unknown depending on the creator. The machine model therefore records an illustrative existing-equipment pathway without default expense or work-hour values.

The program does not authorize account creation, publication, paid experiments, identity verification or collection of private audience data. **Time to first payout, success probability, representative production hours and cash cost distributions remain unknown.**

## Research measurements still needed

An auditable creator-entry cohort requires documented country, language, starting channel condition, cohort selection, first upload and publishing dates, verified feature tiers, eligibility dates, earned product payments, total cash costs, hours, and nonresponse. Do not infer demographics from a channel's appearance. Record the difference between an account that never uploads, an uploading channel, and an already monetized creator.

For independent evaluation, compare participation cohorts on a matched date basis and retain zero earners. The existing [YouTube application lifecycle](application-lifecycle.md) begins after some of these steps and should not be mistaken for the time needed to reach Partner Program view/subscriber thresholds.
