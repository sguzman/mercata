# Niche pilot: cost, labor and abandonment experiment protocol

**Evidence review:** 2026-10-25. This is an independent research and measurement method built on registered official sources: [YouTube Shorts creation guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12921536?hl=en), [responsive video formats](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6375112?hl=en), [livestream encoder preparation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2907883?hl=en) and [YouTube Analytics metric definitions](https://developers.google.com/youtube/analytics/metrics). It also uses Mercata's existing [production cost inventory](video-production-cost-register.md) and [monthly cash settlement ledger](monthly-creator-cash-flow-protocol.md).

## Prespecification before a channel test

A research pilot must name its specific **topic, production format, release window, proposed episode count, capped new cash, capped labor hours, one primary attention metric and the minimum eligible exposure needed for interpretation**. This must occur before any claimed performance result. Only records obtained from an authorized account can populate a measured exposure field. A sample with no access to owner analytics must carry **unknown outcomes**, not substitute public views or invented conversions.

Each episode attempted, including one abandoned before publication, has a separate ledger of seven **new cash** categories (equipment actually purchased, third-party assets, contractors, software, connectivity/power, marketing and other expenses), one separate **allocated already-owned equipment-use** category, and nine time categories (research, writing, recording, editing, artwork, publication, moderation, marketing and administration). Published video count and attempted video count are different. Allocation of a previously owned camera or computer is **not itself a new cash payment**.

## Predeclared stopping and comparison gates

The engine stops further authorizations whenever observed cash spending **reaches** the stated cap, or tracked hours **reach** the labor cap. It also records **overruns**, because a real pilot may overspend before stopping; a cap is a decision boundary, not proof spending stayed under it. After the planned end date and/or all scheduled attempts, the experiment can finish operationally but is not an *audience success* without a compatible owner-observed exposure count. It cannot be called a proven commercial failure without a justified business outcome definition and suitable observations.

Published video's public lifetime views, eligible impressions, watch-minute events, click events, paid customer sales and net earnings have distinct denominators. The pilot decision **never** calculates causal incremental revenue, typical profit per hour, or odds that the next content niche will work. Changing topic, format or eligibility mid-pilot requires a new comparison frame, not retroactive cherry-picking.

### Interpretation example (fixture only)

Synthetic test fixtures validate that a two-video plan with a $50 new-cash cap and a 12-hour limit ceases permission for further work when either limit is reached, even when no authorized exposure data exist. The numeric illustration is not a reported channel budget or evidence of average creator costs.
