# U.S. YouTube news audiences: bounded survey evidence

**Sources:** Pew Research Center's [2026 Social Media and News Fact Sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/fact-sheet/social-media-and-news-fact-sheet/) and [2025 appendix on regular social-media news consumers](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2025/09/25/appendix-demographic-profiles-of-regular-social-media-news-consumers-in-the-united-states/).

This is the first narrower YouTube content-audience study in Mercata with a **directly measured, defined population**: U.S. adults who report that they regularly obtain news from YouTube. It does **not** measure the number of people watching only political commentary, a universal world-wide community, or the audience size of any single channel.

## News use across U.S. adults, 2020–2026

| Year | Share of all U.S. adults regularly getting news on YouTube | Share of U.S. YouTube users regularly getting news there |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2020 | 23% | 32% |
| 2021 | 22% | 30% |
| 2022 | 25% | 30% |
| 2023 | 26% | 32% |
| 2024 | 32% | 37% |
| 2025 | 35% | 41% |
| 2026 | 34% | 41% |

The 2026 figures are **34% of U.S. adults** and **41% of U.S. YouTube users**. Different denominators mean those percentages are not interchangeable. The 2026 sample included 5,166 U.S. adults surveyed August 24–30, 2026. Questions are self-reported; historical methods and respondent definitions may vary.

## Who were regular U.S. YouTube news consumers in 2025?

Each percentage below is the **composition of people who regularly get news from YouTube**, not the likelihood that a particular demographic group gets news.

| Subgroup | Share of regular U.S. YouTube news consumers |

Percentages need not total 100 because of rounding, excluded groups and nonresponse. Race and ethnicity use Pew's self-reported classification; the Asian subgroup represents English-speaking adults. Party identity is a self-reported political survey answer, **not inferred from video selections**.

## What this does and doesn't establish

The 2026 all-adults measure is valuable for identifying the scale of **self-reported news use** in one country, but has no count of unique individuals or estimate of watch hours. It doesn't establish which news categories are saturated, what topics generate sales, how many active news creators compete, or which political content earns more from advertisers.

It should not be used to profile individuals or target political persuasion. For economic comparison, the [opportunity framework](opportunity-framework.md) still requires independent measures of participating channels, production effort, native and outside earnings, and policy limitations.
