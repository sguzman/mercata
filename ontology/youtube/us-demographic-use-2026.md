# U.S. adult YouTube use by income, education and community (2026)

**Published October 19, 2026.** Pew Research Center's [Americans' Use of Social Media in 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) draws from 5,511 **U.S. adults aged 18+**, fielded January 30–June 18, 2026 through web, mail and telephone. Its reported **survey-wide** sampling margin of error is ±1.8 percentage points. [Machine-readable observations](us-demographic-use-2026.json).

**Interpret every percentage below as the share of the named demographic subgroup reporting that they ever use YouTube**. This is not the demographic composition of the YouTube audience, a monthly-user count, watch time, or a sample of YouTube creators.

## Household-income subgroups

| Annual household income | Ever use YouTube |
| --- | ---: |
| Under USD 30,000 | 77% |
| USD 30,000–69,999 | 84% |
| USD 70,000–99,999 | 86% |
| USD 100,000 or more | 90% |

These rates condition on household-income groups among U.S. adults, **not** on YouTube users or earned channel income.

## Education subgroups

| Education | Ever use YouTube |
| --- | ---: |
| High school or less | 77% |
| Some college | 87% |
| College graduate | 90% |

These do not reveal the education distribution of YouTube viewers.

## Community subgroups

| Residence description | Ever use YouTube |
| --- | ---: |
| Urban | 87% |
| Suburban | 86% |
| Rural | 78% |

Pew's urban/suburban/rural descriptions concern residential communities, not a causal penalty from rural internet, active audience counts, or content preferences.

## Statistical and economic boundaries

Each subgroup sample size and margin of error must be assessed independently. The **5,511 total respondents and ±1.8-point overall sampling error** cannot be copied into every cell; this overview does not establish cell-level denominators or uncertainties, which remain `null`.

The responses concern **ever use**, not unique people viewing in a defined 28-day window, verified platform usage, monthly watching, advertising reach, or creator earnings. Collection methods and survey frames may differ across previous years; an apparent trend should not be declared before a separate method review.

No income/education/residence **composition of actual YouTube users**, absolute number of users per group, joint intersections, country-generalized prevalence, or causal effect has been established. The [measurement register](viewer-person-metrics.md), [geographic study scope](geographic-scope.md) and [audience-overlap rules](audience-overlap.md) prevent misuse of these conditional rates.


## Second income frame: parents reporting children's daily YouTube use (2025)

[Pew's October 2025 child technology report](https://www.pewresearch.org/2025/10/08/how-parents-describe-their-kids-tech-use/) presents a distinct **May 13–26, 2025 U.S. survey** of **3,054 parents/guardians**, asked about one selected child 12 or younger. Parents' reported **daily YouTube viewing by the selected child** varies across **regional-cost- and household-size-adjusted family income**:

| Family adjusted income (2023 USD, scaled to a family of three) | Parent-reported child daily YouTube use |
| --- | ---: |
| Lower: below $49,400 | 55% |
| Middle: $49,400–148,200 | 51% |
| Upper: above $148,200 | 40% |

[Pew's methodology](https://www.pewresearch.org/2025/10/08/parents-kids-screens-methodology/) adjusts 2023-dollar income for family size and local BEA Regional Price Parities; the middle tier spans roughly two-thirds to twice the panel's adjusted median income. These are *not* nominal household income bands, global income cutoffs or a measure of the economic characteristics of all viewers.

The **2025 parent survey measures reported child daily watching**; the **2026 adult survey above measures adult lifetime ever-use**. Their populations, income groups, age ranges, time periods and methods differ. It is incorrect to subtract or pool the 55/51/40 percentages with the adult 77/84/86/90 values, or to infer an income distribution of unique YouTube viewers.

The **3,054 and ±2.2-point survey-wide margin** cannot be assigned to individual income cells: the public account does not report those subgroup sample sizes or margins. Exact age-by-income YouTube viewing, watch-time volume, YouTube Kids versus main platform, causal household-income effects, creator profit and unique child-user counts remain `null`.
