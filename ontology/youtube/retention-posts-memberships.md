# Creator Posts, returning viewers and membership renewals: keeping units separate

**Study snapshot:** October 23, 2026. This documents official YouTube capabilities and a future test design, not measured effects of posting on retention. [Machine record](retention-posts-memberships.json).

## Posts are not the same as Communities

[YouTube's Posts help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9409631?hl=en) describes **creator-made** text, GIF, image, video, poll and quiz Posts, displayed on a creator's channel and potentially across the Home, Subscriptions or Shorts surfaces. Posts are unavailable for supervised accounts and channels designated Made for Kids.

[YouTube Communities](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15739409?hl=en) instead allow **subscribers to initiate discussions** within a participating channel, subject to a minimum subscription tenure set by its creator and moderator controls. Members-only posts are a third experience requiring an eligible paid channel membership, not a guaranteed public impression.

Official [creation guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7124474?hl=en) supports creator scheduling and different post formats. None of those features proves reach, returning viewers or income, and the company does not publish a general incremental return-rate effect from Posts.

## A source-reported Posts scale estimate, not creator retention

[YouTube's dated September 23, 2026 TV and Shorts announcement](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/made-on-youtube-creators-shorts-series-tv-features/) states that on average **more than 300 million users engage with Posts daily**. The platform did not specify its underlying account/person deduplication, country, age eligibility, reporting window, methodology or engagement-action threshold. Mercata records this as the **platform's claim about daily engaging users** — not an audited global unique-person count, a per-creator Posts reach, an additive public video audience, or a causal Posts-to-loyalty result.

That announcement also describes plans to recommend more Posts in Shorts feeds and a future TV Posts experience. A future-rollout claim is not evidence that all channel owners have access now or that posting guarantees a larger viewer cohort.

## Owner Analytics metrics cannot be pooled into a conversion rate

[YouTube's Posts Analytics help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340301?hl=en) defines **post impressions**, likes and votes under the owner-only Content → Posts view. Impressions reflect displayed events, not unique viewers; a vote is an engagement, not a subscriber renewal.

A channel's [rolling monthly audience](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416?hl=en) is an estimated unique audience from the preceding **28 days**. In its [loyalty segments](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10246996/understand-returning-amp-new-viewers-data), **casual** viewers watched the channel in **1–5 months** of the past year, while **regular** viewers watched it monthly for **more than six months**. New viewers can include people returning after long absence, private browsing or cleared histories. The regular-viewer classification therefore cannot prove that a Post caused the person to return.

A credible post-retention study must specify a channel, post exposure/release date, content type, baseline upload cadence, matched outcome windows and confounders, and use independently authorized owner analytics. Matched non-post periods or creator channels could help assess descriptive associations. **Without recipient-to-returning-viewer linkage and controls, do not call any difference causal.**

## Paid members, renewals and payments use still other clocks

The [official Memberships Analytics guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7491256?hl=en) distinguishes:

| Owner-visible metric | Unit and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Total members | Members who currently retain perk access, **including some who canceled** |
| Active members | Active subscriptions, excluding canceled-with-access members |
| Members gained | Sign-up events in the selected reporting period |
| Members canceled | Cancellation events, potentially while perks remain accessible |
| Members lost | Canceled members whose current perk access ended |
| Transactions | New sign-up **or recurring payment** events |
| Membership revenue | Creator revenue for the displayed period, not unique buyers |

Billing periods can be **28, 30 or 31 days**. Neither monthly transactions divided by total members, nor gained minus canceled divided by subscriptions, defines a defensible *cohort renewal rate* without tracking the original eligible cohort and actual renewals on each cycle.

An account can cancel yet retain perks; a renewed transaction does not establish a new unique buyer. A true repeat-purchase study requires authorized owner data and appropriate privacy protection. Do not export or store individual member identifiers or billing history in Mercata's public sources.

## Missing economic outcomes

Mercata has **no representative channel cohort** measuring Post-induced return, no owner-authorized user-level joining across surfaces, no typical Posts reach, no actual member cohort retention, no distribution of net membership profit, and no measured production or moderation hours for these activities. All estimates remain `null` in the machine record.

The documented features define research eligibility and observable counters only. The [existing channel growth measurement guide](creator-growth.md), [viewer versus account definitions](viewer-person-metrics.md), and [newcomer income cohort protocol](newcomer-income-cohort.md) set the denominator and missingness constraints for future results.
