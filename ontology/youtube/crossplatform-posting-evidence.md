# Cross-platform posting is not audience migration

**Reviewed October 24, 2026.** [Source data and explicitly unknown results](crossplatform-posting-evidence.json); [constraint validator](../../site/crossplatform-posting-audit.mjs).

## Direct vendor observation: where published copies went

[ShortSync's October 16, 2026 snapshot](https://www.shortsync.app/data/short-form-posting-report) records **6,175 service-using creators**, **66,318 uploads** and **199,276 successful published posts** from November 4, 2025. An uploaded video may generate several published post events, so those counts have distinct units. **76% of uploads** went to multiple platforms, **58%** to three or more, and YouTube was **18% of post events**. The service warns that its users are disproportionately creators interested in cross-posting; these rates cannot be projected onto all YouTube channels.

Among **50,175 multi-platform uploads**, the reported pair shares included **56% Instagram + YouTube** and **55% TikTok + YouTube**. These sets overlap; the same upload may appear in both pairs. Percentages are **not** disjoint and cannot be added. Failed jobs, drafts and imported historical posts were excluded. The report's live page is recomputed daily; **these numbers refer specifically to its dated October 16 snapshot**, not a guaranteed latest all-time platform series. ShortSync releases the published aggregates under CC BY 4.0.

## Competitive response in an independent research setting

Niloofar Abolfathi's [open-access peer-reviewed Strategic Management Journal study](https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.70016), first published September 11, 2025, compares YouTube/Twitch single-platform and dual-platform creators around **September 2016 Twitch Prime exposure growth**. In that historical setting, multihomers reduced effort and underperformed relative to single-homers after intensified rivalry. This tests competitive incentives in a particular historical community: it does **not** prove a 2026 Short should never cross-post, or that the same people migrated between platforms. Study selection, event identification and creator types differ completely from the ShortSync commercial software logs.

## What a cross-platform funnel would need

The transition from **same uploaded video on multiple websites** to **the same person viewing multiple websites** is unobserved. True movement would require consented or legitimately aggregated, attributable origin/destination visitor events, time windows, deduplication and privacy protections. Financial benefits need incremental *net* receipts and separately counted work, tool costs, licenses and taxes.
