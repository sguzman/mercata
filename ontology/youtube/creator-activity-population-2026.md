# Active creator population: channel counts are not unique people

**Research date: October 25, 2026.** [Machine source-frame register](creator-activity-population-2026.json). These observations extend [vidIQ's channel cohort study](channel-cohort-observations-2026.md) and [CreatorDB's independent June snapshot](creator-index-comparison-2026.md) without pooling incompatible provider indexes.

## Why none is the answer to 'How many active creators?'

A channel may have multiple human contributors and a person or company may control multiple channels. A public subscriber count cannot prove that a channel has published recently, is in good standing with YPP, earned any income, or represents a person rather than an organization.

The vidIQ 90-day sample is based on **net public video count change**, not a complete event log of uploads, livestreams and deletions. A channel could publish new videos and remove an equal number in the same interval without increasing the public count. The 360-day cohort is not a cohort of first-time entrants, and its missing, negative or unpublished observations cannot silently be counted as zeros.

CreatorDB and vidIQ counts use different inclusion and coverage rules, so their difference is not birth, attrition, platform growth or a population estimate. Their channels cannot be summed without deduplication.

## Acceptance test for an actual active creator count

The study would need a defined region and calendar period, consistent recorded upload/live events and whether an editor, owner or legal entity counts as the creator; account deduplication; inclusion of zero-subscriber/nonindexed or deleted channels if relevant; privacy and legal permission; and measurement of payment or labor if the word *professional* is used.

These four original vendor observations are useful *bounded index measurements*, not a verified global population of uploaders, earning creators or newcomers. Those remain unmeasured.
