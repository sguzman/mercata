# Creator campaign payments: concentration among payees, not YouTube salaries

**Reviewed October 24, 2026.** [Machine denominator crosswalk](brand-campaign-payment-concentration-2026.json), [source-integrity tests](../../tests/brand-campaign-payment-concentration.test.mjs).

CreatorIQ's [January 21, 2026 State of Creator Compensation release](https://www.creatoriq.com/press/releases/state-of-creator-compensation-) summarizes **2025 payments directly administered through its creator marketing system**. The analysis refers to approximately **14,400 paid creators** in vendor records, not every creator, every sponsorship arrangement, non-earning entrants, or YouTube Partner Program transactions. This is the perspective of one commercial vendor and its participating brands.

## Concentration of payment volume

| Vendor payee share | Portion of vendor-direct payments, 2025 |
| --- | ---: |
| Highest-paid 1% | 21% |
| Next 9% | 41% |
| Remaining 90% | 38% |
| Total | 100% |

The source also reports top-10% shares of **53% (2023), 60% (2024), 62% (2025)** and top-1% shares of **15%, 18%, 21%** respectively. These are year-specific vendor campaign payee groups; the same people, brands or payment inclusion rules cannot be assumed constant between years. They do not show the fraction of YouTube channels earning nothing or whether a small creator can enter the sponsorship market.

## Two denominators that must not be compared as a mean/median pair

The press release juxtaposes **$11,400 average dollars per campaign** and **$3,000 median dollars per creator**. A *campaign* and a *creator/payee* are different observational units. Their difference **does not** mathematically measure creator earnings skew. The direct-payment concentration segments above are the valid same-denominator comparison.

Mercata already registers CreatorIQ's distinct **2026 State of Creators** survey undertaken with Influencers.club (reported **5,095 respondents** and **67% reporting annual creator income under $10,000**). That voluntary respondent survey is not a second set of CreatorIQ vendor-direct payee records. Do not add the two sample counts, pool outcomes or treat them as an identical demographic cohort.

**Unknown:** Native YouTube sponsorship incomes, regional and subscriber-tier payee statistics, share of new YouTube creators receiving no sponsor payments, contract cash expenses/taxes, net profit, and causal effects of recommendations, niches or posting frequency. This is bounded commercial *payment concentration* evidence, not a probability census of an entire creator economy.

## Selected YouTube fee tiers for accepted brand collaborations (October 2026)

[Kolect's October 17, 2026 report, updated October 21](https://www.kolect.ai/reports/creator-marketing-benchmarks-2026), presents public median **creator-side fees for agreed, accepted brand collaborations** within its own managed campaigns. It covers **more than 120,000 accepted collaborations across multiple platforms**, created August 19, 2025–October 15, 2026. **The YouTube-only sample size in each tier is not published.** These are conditional on having an accepted agreement, not a representative sample of all content creators or a probability of obtaining sponsorship.

| YouTube account follower tier | Selected median agreed fee (USD / collaboration) | P25–P75 per agreement |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Nano: below 10,000 | $300 | $150–$500 |
| Micro: 10,000–99,999 | $500 | $300–$800 |
| Mid: 100,000–499,999 | $800 | $488–$1,430 |
| Macro: 500,000–999,999 | $1,100 | $633–$1,994 |
| Mega: 1 million and above | $1,800 | $1,175–$4,000 |

The separately reported all-tier median for selected YouTube agreements is **$600**, **not** the median annual pay for YouTubers. Kolect uses creator follower snapshots from September–October 2026 and USD fees converted at October 15 reference FX rates. The report says only 32% of **all-platform** accepted collaborations reached its stricter **brand-approved closed** state; that is not a verified YouTube payment settlement ratio.

**Missingness and limits:** Its managed campaigns skew toward U.S./European consumer tech, home, DIY and lifestyle projects. No actual YouTube revenue per creator-year, fee frequency, share of never-selected small accounts, production costs, creator take-home profit or YouTube tier-specific numerator is published. The five bands are disjoint only as *follower size tiers*; they do not partition all potential creators into sponsored and unsponsored outcomes. Fees are accepted agreements, **not necessarily paid invoices**. This vendor cohort cannot be combined with CreatorIQ's direct payment recipients, voluntary annual-income survey or the separate Forbes elite ranking to form a typical channel income distribution.

## Independent paid-deliverable comparison: OpenSponsorship (July 2026)

[OpenSponsorship's July 15, 2026 original agency study](https://opensponsorship.com/blog/nano-micro-mega-influencer-pricing-data) reviews **1,527 selected completed and paid creator deliverables** booked January 2025–June 2026, of which **1,502 have current follower counts**. Records had to carry at least **$50 cash** and **more than 100 tracked views**; gifted-product-only transactions and unsuccessful or never-booked creators are not in the population. The agency's clients skew toward sports, wellness, food and lifestyle, and its data cover **multiple platforms rather than YouTube alone**.

| Current follower tier in this vendor sample | Completed paid deliverables (not creators) | Median cash paid per deliverable |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Below 10k | 146 | $134 |
| 10k–49,999 | 349 | $150 |
| 50k–249,999 | 513 | $417 |
| 250k–999,999 | 441 | $517 |
| 1m or more | 53 | $2,727 |
| **Total with tier** | **1,502** | **Not a pooled-median statistic** |

The source reports 25 records missing follower tiers out of the 1,527 complete pricing set. It estimates **per-deliverable payments**, not per-person annual earnings or net profit. The 53-deliverable mega sample is relatively small, and one creator may appear multiple times.

**Never pool it with the Kolect table:** Kolect is *YouTube-specific* for its follower-tier **accepted/agreed** fee estimates but does not report YouTube tier n, and its mid/macro cutoffs are **100k and 500k**. OpenSponsorship is *multiplatform* and **completed-paid**, with cutoffs **50k and 250k**. Their periods, campaign buyers, geography, economics and participation selection differ. A numerical contrast between their reported tier medians does **not** establish platform price gaps, annual income, a sponsorship success rate or a causal payoff from more followers. Both missing all-creator denominators and typical YouTube after-cost profits remain `null`.
