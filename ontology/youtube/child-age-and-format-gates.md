# YouTube children, animation and age-restricted audiences are different populations

**Source review: October 24, 2026.** [Structured classification and evidence record](child-age-and-format-gates.json). The purpose is to prevent false claims that children are absent merely because adult surveys exclude them, and to keep YouTube's distinct age/content policies separate.

## Content classification is not animation genre

YouTube's [made-for-kids determination guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en) distinguishes content deliberately directed toward children (including the legal meaning of *mixed audience*) from content merely safe for a family or broadly appealing animated videos. Cartoons, characters, toys or family-appropriate language alone **do not prove** a video is made for kids. A creator must consider actual subject matter, production and intended audience, while age definitions can differ among countries. Under U.S. COPPA examples, children means those **under 13**, which should not be applied universally to other jurisdictions.

| Content category | Distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Child-directed | Children are the primary or deliberately included target; made-for-kids classification and related platform restrictions apply |
| Mixed-audience, still child-directed | Children are a target even if they are not the dominant group; not equivalent to merely family-safe |
| General/family-friendly animation | May appeal across ages without targeting children; not automatically made for kids |
| Teen-or-older general content | May be suitable for teens but not directed to young children; usually not made for kids if other factors support that classification |
| 18+ age-restricted content | Separate policy gate requiring signed-in adult viewers, not a made-for-kids setting |

The first two classifications affect the creator's YouTube audience setting. The final classification is a separate [18+ age-restriction policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802167?hl=en). Age-restricted content is not viewable by signed-out or under-18 viewers, and some advertising can remain available while being limited; this does not indicate child-directed status.

## Specific platform-feature consequences, not generic income forecasts

According to [YouTube's made-for-kids setting guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en), affected videos lose **personalized advertising**, comments, live chat and Super Chat/Stickers, certain channel-membership and merchandising features, notifications and other personalization/engagement options. On a wholly child-directed channel, Posts and some other channel features are unavailable.

**Not serving personalized ads does not mean that all ads are banned or creator revenue is mathematically zero.** Public documentation provides no representative RPM reduction, creator profit median, typical watch-time loss or count of child-directed channels. These stay `null` in the machine record.

## Two separately measured, noncomparable UK child samples

[Ofcom Online Nation 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/online-habits/from-apps-to-ai-search-how-the-uk-goes-online-in-2025) reports **96% usage of YouTube among UK children aged 8–14** within its measured population. Separately, [Barb's July 2025 publication](https://www.barb.co.uk/news/barb-starts-reporting-tv-set-viewing-to-youtube-channels-in-world-first-initiative/) reports **53% of in-home Wi-Fi YouTube viewing for ages 4–15 occurred on TV sets in Q2 2025**.

| UK reported measure | Age range | Denominator | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Ofcom 2025 YouTube platform usage | 8–14 | People in a named childhood age sample | 96% |
| Barb Q2 2025 YouTube TV-set viewing | 4–15 | Measured YouTube home Wi-Fi viewing time across devices | 53% |

These percentages are **not two estimates of the same quantity**; one is platform usage within an age group, the other is the TV-device portion of measured viewing. Neither is a count of unique children or permission to identify individuals. They cannot be added or divided to estimate creator addressable customers.

## Adult-only sample exclusion is not child non-use

A survey of U.S. adults aged 18+ or any survey with a minimum respondent age of 16 does **not** show that younger populations have zero platform use. Adult respondents and children cannot be combined without reconciling country, age, population, device and survey method.

The project avoids individual child profiles, individual-viewing records, inferred demographic attributes, and personalized audience targeting. The remaining unknowns include unique global child viewers, child-directed viewing share and earnings consequences.
