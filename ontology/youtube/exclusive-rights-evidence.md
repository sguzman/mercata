# Ownership, non-exclusive uploads and exclusive Content ID rights

**Reviewed October 24, 2026.** [Machine legal/policy source matrix](exclusive-rights-evidence.json).

[YouTube's general terms](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms) say a creator **retains ownership** of content they own while granting a **non-exclusive**, worldwide, sublicensable, transferable and royalty-free service license. That is not a copyright sale to YouTube, a claim of exclusive ownership by YouTube, or a guarantee of any creator payment. License duration continues until removal subject to service and legal exceptions, and separate third-party footage and audio rights remain relevant.

[Content ID reference policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2605065?hl=en) is **stricter**: a claimant must have **exclusive rights in the territories claimed**. Nonexclusive footage/music licenses, public domain content and CC BY material cannot be used as exclusive automated match references. [Content ID eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311402?hl=en) is not automatic just because an upload is original or lawful. The ordinary permission to publish an authorized nonexclusive video and the exclusive rights to claim other uploaders' videos are separate.

[YouTube CC BY settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797468?hl=en) allow attribution-conditioned reuse of eligible videos, not exclusive capture of their copyright. An upload with an active Content ID claim cannot adopt the CC BY option. [Claim policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276?hl=en-GB) may monetize, track or block use by specific region. A claim is not necessarily a copyright strike and does not establish which creator received money.

To resolve actual costs or ownership, the research would need original contract terms, covered audio/video rights, territory, duration, termination, exclusivity and payout splits. These are **unmeasured**; the policy matrix is not a legal ruling on any creator.
