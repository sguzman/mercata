# Verified Studio metrics from selected AIR-affiliated channels (2025–2026)

**Research review: October 23, 2026.** [Machine data](air-studio-benchmarks-2026.json). Two analyses from AIR Media-Tech offer real Studio observations for **selected monetized client channels**. They are **not a probability sample of new YouTube channel owners**, and their income figures must not be extrapolated to zero-earning, rejected or abandoned entrants.

## Study A — 300 monetized channels and 3,595 channel-months

AIR's [2026 RPM report](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/how-much-does-youtube-really-pay-in-2026-real-rpm-data-from-300-channels) (last updated **July 1, 2026**) examined a selected network of **300 channels**, ranging from **10,000 to 50 million subscribers**, across 13 niches. It covered **3,595 monetized channel-months** from May 2025 to May 2026.

AIR reports an overall **median RPM of USD 2.30 per 1,000 views**, calculated from its YouTube Studio revenue observations rather than advertiser-facing CPM estimates. RPM already reflects the creator's reported share of platform earnings, **before production expenses, taxes and valuation of unpaid work**.

The article supplies these selected niche examples:

| Publisher niche | Median USD RPM | Publisher-reported P25–P75 range | Precise niche sample size |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Education & Science | 10.22 | 2.31–19.50 | Unknown |
| Gaming | 2.05 | 0.70–3.62 | Unknown |
| Kids & Teens | 0.33 | 0.16–0.88 | Unknown |
| Business & Finance | 2.01 | 0.73–5.17 | Fewer than 10 channels; exact n unknown |

These are selected examples, **not a defensible platform-wide ranking of niche profitability**. Values describe revenue per 1,000 views of monetized channels, not expected take-home money for a first-year creator. The article flags several niche groups with fewer than ten channels; the exact channel count for most rows is not supplied in the page's table.

## Study B — 404 channels; 127 monetize livestreaming

AIR's [July 10, 2026 livestream report](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/are-youtube-livestreams-worth-it-real-data-on-revenue-and-growth-from-404-channels) covers **404 client channels** across 13 niches and **3,893 channel-months** of general video-performance data. But the streaming revenue/subscriber subgroup consists of **127 channels** and **1,287 monetized streaming channel-months**.

The selected streaming sample's reported median was **45 views per livestream** for the measured 2025 streaming-month data. These are *views per stream*, not concurrent viewers, unique people, revenue, profit or hourly compensation. The observation precedes YouTube's August 24, 2026 change to the meaning of a public video view.

The vendor also compares some of the same channels during streaming versus nonstreaming months, but month-to-month schedules, new uploads and seasonality may vary. A comparison is not automatically a causal increase or decrease in revenue from streaming.

## Denominator and independence controls

Both studies are published by **AIR Media-Tech**, and their participant-channel overlap is **unknown**. **Never add 300 + 404 as if they were 704 independent channels**. Likewise, 3,595 and 1,287 are repeated **channel-months**, not distinct YouTube creators.

Neither selected study samples all new channels, all unmonetized creators, failed publishing attempts, people who stopped posting, or channels with zero program income. AIR's affiliated sample has substantial monetization and client selection. The reports do not jointly measure production labor, software/equipment spend, off-platform sales or net cash profit.

The [new-creator cohort protocol](newcomer-income-cohort.md) requires entry-based sampling including zero earners and attrition. The [current-year creator survey comparison](cross-platform-income-surveys.md) covers different respondent recruitment methods, and the [source-family audit](source-provenance.md) guards against treating syndicated or overlapping research samples as independent replication.

These are **verified selected-channel Studio measures**, not proof that a beginning YouTube creator can expect to earn a particular amount per 1,000 public views.
