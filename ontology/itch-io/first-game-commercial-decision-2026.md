# First paid-game commercial decision contract

**Research review: October 26, 2026.** [Machine rules](first-game-commercial-decision-2026.json). This is a hypothetical decision protocol, **not permission to release, advertise or spend**.

A publisher can choose a **free browser game with voluntary donations**, a **positively priced downloadable game**, or a **free demo plus separately priced full game**. The actual paid event differs in each model; page views, browser plays, gifted keys and demo downloads are not positive-price orders. [HTML5 policy](https://itch.io/docs/creators/html5) · [Pricing](https://itch.io/docs/creators/pricing) · [Buyer purchasing](https://itch.io/docs/creators/how-buying-works).

A valid economic comparison requires a matching game and audience window, gross versus refundable net buyer payments, actual processor/platform charges, distribution rights, owner labor and realistic QA/support hours. The operator's illustrative **$10 payment leaves $8.41 after its default 10% share and typical 2.9% + $0.30 processing charge**; it is before refunds, taxes, payout fees, FX or valued labor. [Source](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments). Required break-even paid orders do not indicate the chance of attracting those buyers.

The minimal hypothetical title is a small 2D single-player experience with 1–2 mechanics and 5–30 minutes playtime. This is a **scope illustration, not a measured optimal offer**. Before a real pilot, independently confirm commercial asset rights, an actually tested build, the seller's country and merchant eligibility, an explicit cash and labor budget, and appropriately supported demand evidence. Without those gates, **no paid release or profit prediction is justified**.

Possible authorized comparisons would test HTML5 donations against a premium download, a demo against no demo, or itch-only against cross-store publication. Preserve aligned time, currency, product, exposure and variable/fixed costs. These ideas remain unexecuted, with no claimed causal effect.

A later authorized pilot should record complete **30-, 90- and 365-day** windows including zero-order eligible games and delisted titles in the sampling frame, and disclose referrals, retained orders, refunds, cash payouts, developer hours, game/build variants and missing values. A game with no sales within one observed period is an observed zero **for that period**; it does not demonstrate lifetime impossibility. Published developer case studies are selected examples, not a representative seller census.

This finishes existing decision, assumption and reporting specifications. It **does not** finish live acquisition measurement, genre profitability or representative first-game expected earnings.
