# itch.io — payment, publication and discovery (26 October 2026)

**Research status:** first-party rules reviewed, but representative game sales and developer earnings remain unknown. See the [machine evidence crosswalk](commercial-rules-2026.json) and [source-linked specimen claims](../../specimens/itch-io.json).

## 1. Selling and receiving money

The official [payments guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments) describes separate seller-direct and itch.io-collected payment modes. In direct mode the seller connects the chosen provider and is merchant of record. Under collected payouts itch.io is merchant of record, receives the money and administers later payout requests. EU VAT handling and chargeback responsibility differ across these routes; one cannot use an identical net-revenue formula to represent both legal obligations.

The platform documents a configurable 0–100% revenue share (10% is the sample/default) and an illustrative fixed-plus-variable payment fee. At USD $10 with a 10% share and 2.9% + $0.30 payment processing, the illustrative amount before other costs is **$8.41**. This is **not creator profit or a quoted fee for every actual merchant/transaction**. The dashboard's gross revenue is pre-fee; tax, refunds, reserves, payout or FX charges, and development labor require separate accounts. [Official source](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments).

In the collected route, a payout requires completed tax information, a configured payout method, at least **USD $5 available**, and a **seven-day transaction availability delay**. After the request, staff require at least seven additional days and generally describe a 10–14-day review, with possible longer delays. Up to three payouts may remain in progress; this is not a guaranteed time to cash for any given seller. [Official source](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments).

## 2. Paid versus free game units

[Pricing documentation](https://itch.io/docs/creators/pricing) distinguishes a free/donation project, a mandatory paid minimum, and no-payments access. Optional extra payments exceed the seller's price floor. A free download without payment is not the same as the paid ownership/access entitlement; individually priced files have additional thresholds. Any future dataset must distinguish **free downloads**, **browser play events**, **paid orders**, **refunded orders** and **paid-unit entitlements**. A single player can contribute multiple events, and a single seller can publish multiple titles.

## 3. Publication versus acquisition

A private project can be made public, but a public URL does not imply discoverability in search or browse. The [indexing guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/getting-indexed) requires an eligible public page, cover and playable/downloadable/purchasable content; new first-time paid sellers can remain unindexed pending moderator review. Direct title search and tag-driven topical browse differ. The site cautions against treating newly released listings as an adequate launch channel. None of these policies quantify impressions, traffic conversion or customer revenue.

The [creator FAQ](https://itch.io/docs/creators/faq) and [first-page guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/getting-started) describe owner dashboards with project views, downloads and purchases. Public market-wide creator income and zero-earner distributions cannot be derived from these private measures without representative authorized aggregates.

## 4. Game-specific channels

[HTML5 uploads](https://itch.io/docs/creators/html5) can run in a browser from a ZIP with `index.html` or a single HTML file. [Game-jam guidance](https://itch.io/docs/creators/game-jams) describes submissions and rankings as a community format; a jam submission, rating or play event is not a paid purchase. [Sales and bundles](https://itch.io/docs/creators/sales) are additional storefront mechanisms; their existence proves no sales lift. The [quality guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines) warn that content quality or policy problems can affect indexing.

## Unresolved empirical questions

- Number and geographic composition of paying customers and eligible active commercial publishers.
- Representative paid units per first commercial title, including unsold and removed games.
- Seller-level mean, median, income percentiles, zero-income shares, production costs and profit per hour.
- Causal effects of tags, release timing, embedded browser gameplay, game jams and discounts.

These remain **unknown**, not zero. The next work should seek comparable transaction evidence and population frames; descriptive platform policy does not solve these outcome questions.


## Tax identity verification account adjustment

The official [payments guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments) lists a **one-time $3 tax-identity validation adjustment** to a seller account using collected payouts. This is separate from the $0 upfront publishing/listing price, percentage share, per-transaction payment processing, payout transfer fees, tax withholdings and the real cost of game development. Its existence does not imply that a developer has to spend $3 just to publish a free game. The guide separately recommends selling a paid product for at least roughly **$2** as pricing advice because the fixed payment processing charge is proportionally large on tiny orders.
