## Three economic traps

**1. Top Seller ≠ proven transaction.** An item appearing on a ranked browse page does not disclose how many units sold, whether receipts came from standalone games or bundles, or what the creator retained. It is **invalid to divide 19,508 by the separately observed 49,588 paid-game filter** and call the quotient a commercial success rate. The browsing and ranking criteria and observation dates differ.

**2. HTML5 ∩ Paid ≠ mandatory-pay-to-play web game.** The operator's [HTML5 guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/html5) explains browser gameplay. A page may also have paid downloadable files or optional donations. The 539-result intersection is metadata, not proof that an embedded web game requires payment or ever sold.

**3. Localization language ≠ regional demand.** 174 pages marked Latin American Spanish are **content availability**, not a count of Mexican purchasers, commercial Spanish-language demand or uplift from translation. Likewise, the 30-day and 2022 browse shortcuts are not verified first-game commercial release cohorts.

All query intersections are overlapping: games can be classified as Puzzle and Adventure, support Windows and HTML5, and be featured. These counts cannot be summed. Paid buyer geography, active seller identities, real sales and labor-adjusted profit remain unknown.
