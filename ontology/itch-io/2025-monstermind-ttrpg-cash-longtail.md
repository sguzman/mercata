# 2025 tabletop publisher: mixed income and long-tail titles

**Reviewed October 27, 2026.** Source: [Peter Eijk's January 20, 2026 first-party retrospective](https://monstermind.substack.com/p/2025-a-year-in-review). [Structured record](2025-monstermind-ttrpg-cash-longtail.json).

The one selected publisher reported **approximately $2,400 gross** from multiple platforms and physical/royalty channels in 2025, **approximately $2,300 after PayPal/platform fees**, and **approximately $2,200 after other stated cash expenses**. These are approximations across EUR, USD and GBP. No complete developer labor or verified bank settlement was disclosed.

The author described **43.8% of mixed-channel income via itch.io**, **22%** through a physical-zine distributor, **21.7%** as an advance on translation royalties and **9.7%** through direct physical-shop sales. These shares are not independently verified game-unit totals, and the royalty advance is not a completed customer sale. Multiplying the 43.8% by approximate total earnings would falsely imply an exact itch-only after-fee amount.

The creator reported older games **A Visit to San Sibilia** (2021) and **Hiria: the Eternal City** (2023) as important 2025 earners, with renewed second-half sales of the 2021 title. That is bounded, selected evidence for continued sales years after first publication. It is not a measured 7/30/90/365-day title cohort, age-matched decay curve, or representative success probability.

The author also attributed **about 20% of sales to bundles** but did not provide a verified units-versus-revenue denominator or buyers who would otherwise have purchased separate games. Both new projects in 2025 were pay-what-you-want tabletop titles, not two mandatory-priced digital videogame launches.

**Disposition:** Existing task `itch-io/sales/measure-long-tail-income` advances from **97% to 98%** on an additional independent first-party older-title persistence report, but remains incomplete. The merchant median, mean, zero-sale fraction, actual paid-game conversion, after-cost profit, and population long-tail distributions remain unknown.
