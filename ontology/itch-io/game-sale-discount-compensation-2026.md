# Discounted-game order thresholds: arithmetic, not demand

Reviewed October 26, 2026. [Machine model](game-sale-discount-compensation-2026.json) · [tested calculator](../../site/itch-discount-order-threshold.mjs).

A seller's [itch.io sale](https://itch.io/docs/creators/sales) changes the buyer-facing price. Under the operator's illustrative [10% revenue share and 2.9% + $0.30 processing rule](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments), a **$10** game contributes **$8.41** per retained purchase before other costs. A **20% discount** makes its checkout price **$8**, with **$6.67** contribution per retained order.

A hypothetical pre-discount cohort of **100 paid orders** would contribute **$841**. The discounted title would need **127 retained paid orders**, or **27 additional orders**, merely to match that original contribution. These are supplied synthetic quantities and threshold arithmetic. There is **no observed increase in sales**, no costed advertising campaign, and no inferred price elasticity.

An additional campaign cost can be supplied explicitly. A 100%-off offer becomes a **free checkout**, not a positive-price order that incurs assumed card fees. Likewise, the [2025 Creator Day](https://itch.io/updates/what-is-creator-day.amp) waived the operator's share, a distinct mechanism from reducing the customer's price.

Commercial comparisons require the same game, buyers or valid matched exposures, price/fee regime, time window, refunds and developer work. Without a valid experiment, a discount cannot be claimed to increase revenue or profit. Zero publisher-specific purchase data were obtained.
