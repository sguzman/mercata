# itch.io — first paid-game discoverability and account security

**Reviewed October 26, 2026.** Machine record: [account-launch-indexing-2026.json](account-launch-indexing-2026.json). This is public official guidance and does not involve operating any account.

The [Search & Browse guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/getting-indexed) distinguishes **publishing a public game page** from **being indexed for discovery**. A first-time seller's paid listing can remain pending human review while the direct page link works. Index eligibility requires public access, a cover, and a purchasable, downloadable or playable product. Incorrect tags, misleading metadata or quality violations can result in deindexing. The guide warns that recreating a game page to refresh its Most Recent date can be interpreted as circumvention. Once published, the original publication date is not changed merely because indexing occurs later.

The guide distinguishes **direct-name search** (a buyer already knows the game) from **topic/tag browse** (the buyer explores a genre). The two discovery surfaces must not be pooled in a future causal analysis; the guide does not publish paid sales associated with either.

The [account security guide](https://itch.io/docs/advanced/two-factor-authentication) documents a TOTP-based second factor and backup codes, rather than SMS 2FA. A commercial payout account should have strong account security and recovery procedures, but no seller account was accessed and no adoption, incident or hack rate was measured. Never save or publish actual OTP keys or backup codes in Mercata.

Data needed to determine effectiveness remains missing: newly released paid-game seller population, observed time to index, traffic by acquisition surface, true paid orders, and comparisons controlling for outside audience. Our current first-release indexing probability and security incidents are **unknown**, not zero.
