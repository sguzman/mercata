## Accounting and price-model boundaries

The 2016 game had **no mandatory purchase price**: 11 positive-pay downloads cannot enter a denominator of paid-premium titles, and 156 download events are not 156 exposed visitors. The author's cash outlay totaled **€330**, composed of **€240 music** and **€90 Steam Greenlight entry**, across platforms. The author's approximately **€55** revenue translation is not an independently verified exchange rate or net-after-labor profit. The dollar receipts and euro costs are preserved separately.

For the 2026 title, the author's pre-Steam **5,000 sold copies** is a genuine published milestone from a page selling the game; it precedes Steam's stated **June 26** release. However, *all* 5,000 receipts are not independently certified as itch-only, and there is no reported price history, refund status or net payout. The later **50,000 Steam copies** are a different store and different observation window. They must not be divided by 5,000 to assert a causal marketplace uplift, added as unique customers, or misidentified as itch sales.

The publicly visible **$1.99** itch purchase minimum is a current listing condition, not a verified June transaction price. Multiplying it by the 5,000-copy statement would fabricate gross historical revenue.

## Why this changes the research

The newly registered 2026 pre-Steam milestone expands the range of source-supported commercial-game outcomes; the 2016 case provides a contrasting zero-minimum first-game payment/cost disclosure. Neither measures median income, the proportion of paid games making zero sales, an average viable commercial-game genre, paid-buyer population or after-cost seller returns.

Two *existing* fixed tasks receive conservative evidence advances: base **market/volume 94→95** and detailed **itch-io/market/estimate-paid-purchase-demand 69→70**. No task is marked complete; all 244 original tasks remain the fixed denominator.
