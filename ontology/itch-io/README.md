# itch.io study ontology

This vocabulary defines *measurement concepts* for future game-market economics research, not factual findings. See [machine vocabulary](vocabulary.json), [unresearched plan](../../plans/itch-io.md), and [original empty dossier](../../specimens/itch-io.json).

## Concepts

- **game listing:** Public game page is not necessarily a paid commercial title.
- **publisher account:** An account may represent a team or person; not automatically an individual developer.
- **commercial game:** A game offered for positive payment under clearly defined conditions.
- **free game:** A game without mandatory payment, possibly receiving voluntary donations.
- **game build:** Specific downloadable or browser-playable release artifact.
- **browser play:** Play event, not necessarily a unique player or a purchase.
- **download:** File acquisition, not necessarily a customer or paid unit.
- **paid unit:** Unit associated with an actual payment, subject to refund and bundle definitions.
- **seller:** Eligible seller entity; not necessarily the same as individual game developer.
- **buyer:** Paying entity in defined period, requiring validated counting method.
- **gross revenue:** Payment before explicitly defined deductions.
- **net proceeds:** Settled seller amount after disclosed marketplace and processing deductions.
- **net profit:** Seller proceeds minus applicable operating costs, with labor treatment declared.
- **effective hourly income:** Defined economic income divided by relevant labor hours.
- **zero earner:** Eligible publisher cohort member with zero proceeds over an observed window.
- **bundle allocation:** Explicit fraction of bundle proceeds attributed to an individual item.
- **conversion rate:** Defined paid outcome count over compatible exposure count.

## Comparability rules

- Do not equate listings with distinct developers.
- Do not equate free downloads or browser plays with purchases.
- Separate paid units from bundle allocations and voluntary tips.
- Distinguish gross revenue, settled proceeds, operating profit and hourly income.
- A representative zero-earner statistic requires a defined cohort and observation window.
- Use unknown rather than zero for unavailable demand, income or conversion statistics.
