# itch.io — platform policy, payment and business continuity risk

**Research snapshot: October 26, 2026.** This is a historical and contractual risk audit, not a report about a particular developer's account.

## Verified official policy timeline

- **July 24, 2025:** In an [official update](https://itch.io/updates/update-on-nsfw-content), itch.io reported deindexing adult NSFW material from discovery pages after payment-provider scrutiny. This records a category-specific disruption; it does not mean all games were removed or all creators lost payment access.
- **July 31, 2025:** In a [follow-up announcement](https://itch.io/post/13540035/view-in-topic), the operator described returning free adult NSFW listings to the index when No Payments was selected and stated that the paid category would be handled more cautiously. This is a **2025 historical state**, not proof of today's paid-content indexing status.

## Distinct failure paths

The [current quality guidance](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines) and [creator FAQ](https://itch.io/docs/creators/faq) describe different mechanisms: a published project can be deindexed, a merchant's payment capability can be constrained, an account can be suspended, and paid buyer access can be subject to refunds or enforcement. These are different outcomes and must not be collapsed into one word such as “banned”.

Current [terms](https://itch.io/docs/legal/terms) and [payment models](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments) make marketplace and processor obligations independent. Collected versus direct payments also differ in merchant of record and chargeback liability. Therefore commercial robustness is not proven merely because a game remains downloadable by direct link.

## Measured versus unknown

Known: dated official announcements, categories affected by those specific historical decisions, and the existence of current policy enforcement mechanisms. **Unknown:** number of affected developers, lost sales and profit, probability that an ordinary game will be deindexed, and the precise current paid adult-content reindexing state without further direct verification.

No user accounts, content classification changes, private payout histories, or experiments were performed. The [machine risk audit](policy-risk-2025-2026.json) keeps all unmeasured outcomes null and ties each historical event to its original dated announcement.
