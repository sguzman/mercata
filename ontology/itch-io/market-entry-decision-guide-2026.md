# Selling an independent game on itch.io — evidence-based entry decision guide

**Research snapshot: 26 October 2026.** This guide answers what commercial game publishing on itch.io enables and what evidence is needed before concluding that it can generate sustainable earnings. It is **not a prediction that an individual game will sell**. [Machine decision model](market-entry-decision-guide-2026.json).

## What market you actually enter

The game directories are large, free and paid uploads coexist, and most browse counts measure **listed pages, not actual purchases or paying people**. The October 2026 public browse snapshot recorded approximately **1.78 million all-price game listings**, **49,588 filtered paid games** and **12,691 listings at the paid-and-2D intersection**. These are dynamically indexed and differently filtered catalog observations, not first-time commercial seller populations. Developer attention and money-making outcomes depend on a distinct discovery funnel; simply publishing a public page does not guarantee Search & Browse indexing. Official indexing review may delay a first-time paid seller's visibility. [Marketplace rules](commercial-rules-2026.md) · [Catalog](catalog-snapshot-2026.md).

## Choose the game's economic object

| Route | What people can obtain | What actually counts as revenue |
|---|---|---|
| **Premium downloadable** | Full downloadable game after positive-price payment | Actual retained paid orders after refund/fees |
| **Free browser HTML5 / donations** | Browser play without compulsory payment | Only voluntary cash donations, not view/play counts |
| **Free demo + premium download** | Free sampling and distinct paid full build | Full-game paid orders, not demo downloads |

These modes can coexist in a publisher portfolio. HTML5 embedded gameplay is not itself a mandatory paid-browser entitlement. The documented [pricing guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/pricing) and [HTML5 guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/html5) establish platform mechanics, not relative revenue performance.

## Cash receipts and labor are different economics

The official [payment guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments) illustrates a $10 sale leaving **$8.41** after a configurable default 10% platform share and typical per-transaction processor fee, **before other costs**. That number is an example, not a guaranteed merchant price or personal take-home profit. The payout model and seller-country eligibility also matter. 

The developer-publicized [Mixolumia puzzle-game postmortem](commercial-postmortems-2026.md) reported 1,776 actual itch.io paid units; by contrast, [Rose Seed Replica and AraCore release accounts](release-cost-labor-cases-2026.md) show that poor first-week revenue or apparent small cash profit can coexist with large development and living costs or roughly 1,500 uncompensated hours. These selected reports must not be averaged into a market mean. Use the [explicit break-even model](break-even-scenarios-2026.md) with actual prices, cash spending and valued labor to determine a needed order count; then treat the probability of obtaining that order count as **unknown** until measured.

## What a first release would have to verify

First, verify seller and payout eligibility, account and tax compliance, and who is merchant of record. Separately verify rights in the game's assets and whether the actual executable works on every claimed platform; [release-format rules](release-format-access-2026.md) and [distribution constraints](distribution-build-2026.md) describe necessary checks, not proof that a specific game passed them. Then build a truthful page with relevant genre labels, screenshots, and a realistic plan for attention outside the store.

After launch, track the **same time period and same game** for page exposures, visits, free downloads, paid orders, refunds, seller receipts and actual work hours. If a demo or discount is used, preserve an appropriate comparison rather than claiming the mechanism caused new purchases. A first-time seller should measure 30, 90 and 365 days, **including games that sell nothing**, to estimate outcomes. See the [privacy-preserving cohort protocol](transaction-cohort-protocol-2026.md).

## What the current research can and cannot recommend

Three formats are feasible under different product goals. If revenue must be required rather than voluntary, a **positively priced downloadable entitlement** matches that requirement better than an open HTML5 browser page. If the main goal is immediate accessibility and play, **free HTML5 with optional donation** is compatible. A **separate demo and paid build** is technically possible when additional QA/maintenance cost is acceptable. None can be called the most profitable without comparable paid game cohorts.

The current study still cannot determine the median income of an eligible first-time positive-price game seller, the share making no paid sales, probability of recouping labor, or the true number of paying 2D-game buyers. Self-selected creator analytics and voluntary game postmortems are valuable examples but biased samples. [Community study limitations](community-analytics-sample-2026.md) · [Unresolved research evidence](research-synthesis-2026.md).
