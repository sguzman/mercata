# One developer's release automation workload (2022)

**Reviewed October 27, 2026.** In an [August 16, 2022 developer post](https://dpmaker.itch.io/queens-brothel/devlog/416783/devlog-31-frequent-game-updates), the author of *Queen's Brothel* describes the cost of manually exporting several game builds, packaging them, uploading them, and updating distribution links. This is **one voluntarily reporting creator**, not a survey, timed benchmark, or current average for paid indie games.

| Activity | What the source reports | What it does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|
| Previous manual releases | “A few hours” to export/upload multiple versions | Exact minutes, task allocation, or a reproducible baseline |
| Scripted Godot export | About 2 minutes for the multi-platform engine export stage | Time to upload, test, announce, and support an entire release |
| Bug after publication | Possible repeated fix, packaging, and redeployment work | Frequency of bugs, average hours per issue, measured post-launch liability |
| Automated delivery | Scripted ZIP/upload staging and client-based patch distribution | Universal update delivery across Android, browser, offline installs or customers |

The publisher explained a planned and partially deployed script-based workflow and referred to itch.io and Steam update tooling. **Steam full-game availability was prospective** in the dated post; its inclusion in an automation plan is not a recorded Steam release or sales outcome.

Economically, multi-build export and distribution is a real workload category, and automating one stage may avoid repeat manual labor. But **no observed end-to-end before/after time difference** or hour valuation was published, so this case does not support a calculated number of saved hours, maintenance expenses, release ROI, or future game profit. The game also had a free demo, which must not be treated as evidence of paid-unit sales.

This strengthens the existing maintenance-cost task by replacing one hypothetical duration with a narrowly attributed developer report. The generalizable costs of engines, SDKs, hosting, support and security work remain unknown without authorized timesheets or invoices.
