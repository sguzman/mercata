# October 2026 indexing change and seller visibility channels

Research reviewed **October 26, 2026**. [Machine data and measurement contract](discovery-indexing-and-buyer-contact-2026.json).

On [October 19, 2026](https://itch.io/t/7172464/october-updates-on-indexing), itch.io's operator reported releasing **approximately 215,000 previously flagged project pages** into indexing eligibility over the previous week. The operator reported **well over two million indexed pages** across the platform, described a less restrictive review-classifier trial, and reported enforcement against more than 10,000 spam/malware accounts. These figures are **pages and accounts**, not newly published paid games, approved merchants, people buying games, or sales.

## What appears where

The [indexing guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/getting-indexed) distinguishes a project made public by URL from a project eligible for Search & Browse. A public page generally needs a cover, actual playable/downloadable/purchasable content, and accurate project settings to be indexed; a new paid publisher may still face staff review. Direct links and profile pages may work before indexing.

The [quality guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines) says the original **Most Recent** opportunity occurs on first public publication and is not renewed by ordinary page edits. Recreating a nearly identical page to gain another slot is prohibited and may break ratings and buyer access. An inaccurate OS platform label, unrelated tags, misleading thumbnails, mislabeled language, or irrelevant classifications can remove the page from discovery. Suggested accurate genre/device/language tags identify *supply categories*, not search-query volumes.

## Updates, followers and paid buyers are different audiences

The [follower documentation](https://itch.io/docs/accounts/followers) explains that new releases can produce email notifications depending on follower settings, while devlogs enter follower feeds. The [Interact guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/interact) allows direct buyer emails **after the first sale**, at most **one per day** and with an unsubscribe route. Neither follower count nor an email-recipient count is a transaction, unique buyer, or repeat paying customer.

| Route | Event | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Search & filtered Browse | Eligible indexed project display | Qualified buyer demand or money |
| Most Recent | First-publication listing opportunity | Renewed exposure from merely editing |
| Followers' feed / devlogs | Notice or activity display | Incremental visitors or paid orders |
| New-game follower email | Notification subject to settings | Delivered attention or purchase |
| Direct buyer email | Permitted post-sale contact | A second purchase or external marketing consent |
| Public direct link | Visit to a page even when unindexed | Search ranking or discoverability |

## Measurement needed before commercial claims

Fix the game, relevant release date, exact tag/filter query, rank method, public-index status, language, device and price. Record **30/90/365-day same-release-age cohorts**, including zero-order and formerly unindexed titles. The October indexing reform is a **structural break**: a simple 2025-versus-late-2026 organic traffic comparison does not isolate content popularity.

For any claimed update or follower sales lift, merchant-authorized aggregates must align page impressions, attributable visits, notification exposures, paid orders, refunds and development/marketing hours. A high rank, follow, tag appearance, player rating, devlog read or buyer email is not proof of a positive-price sale. None of those actual event series or causally matched controls was observed here.

**Completed scope:** documented listing metadata, official visibility pathways, buyer-contact mechanics, and pre/post-indexing comparison rules. **Still unknown:** actual query demand, visitor-to-purchase conversion, follower lift, evergreen paid sales and causal ranking effects.
