# Devlog timing and ongoing game traffic: three selected creator charts

**Reviewed October 27, 2026.** [Amulya Mohan's May 17, 2026 qualitative study](https://relma2.srht.site/blog/use-your-itchio-devlogs/) (updated September 27) reviewed creator-shared itch.io downloads/visits and available Steam wishlist curves for **three selected games**. This is a curator-selected comparison, **not** a random sample of premium 2D game releases, an audit of purchaser cohorts, or an experiment.

| Game | Devlog exposure | Source-described traffic pattern |
|---|---|---|
| *Checkmage!* | Approximately weekly from July 21, 2025 | Small recurring download events near most posts and some persistent attention; much larger Steam wishlist spikes coincided with externally viral YouTube trailers |
| *Psycutlery* | Five listed posts from January 2024 to August 2025 | The author interpreted one early post as sustaining activity, while the three later posts showed no obvious extra itch activity; the game was free |
| *Color Coordination* | Released December 26, 2024, with two early follow-ups | The source estimated about **1.39 download events/day** as downloads continued without corresponding subsequent devlogs; a later April spike had no established devlog explanation |

The **qualitative timing evidence is mixed**. It is consistent with occasional devlog discovery and with continued audience activity that does not depend on new devlogs. But the charts do not identify which visiting people were newly reached by a post, who received follower-feed notices, whether prior marketing would have produced the same traffic, or whether any person bought a positive-price game.

The study is especially limited as an economic measurement: at least two featured games were available free, major traffic for one other game coincided with YouTube promotion, and the itch.io plots shown cannot cover all years in one chart. The blog author's suggestion that a post *prevented* traffic from disappearing is **an interpretation without a matched no-update control**, not demonstrated causality. The reported 1.39/day likewise must not become a marketplace-wide visitor or buyer rate.

This complements Mercata's existing [2026 review-driven follower-feed referral case](follower-feed-review-referrals-one-game-2026.md), but those are different exposure mechanisms: third-party review posts are not the game author's own devlogs. With no randomized or owner-verified matched visitors, **new visitors and paid orders caused by devlogs remain unknown**.
