# itch.io paid-game success and cross-channel funding examples

Two developer postmortems illustrate different commercial situations, not representative market outcomes. [Machine records](commercial-postmortems-2026.json).

In the [Mixolumia June 2021 postmortem](https://mixolumia.com/postmortem/), its developer reported **1,776 itch.io paid units and $17,906 gross**, compared with **713 Steam units and $6,202 gross**. A subsequent update gave **$14,980 itch.io** and **$3,680 Steam** received after applicable chargebacks, VAT/sales tax, platform and processing fees. Neither figure deducts all game development costs and labor. The itch release was August 8, 2020, while Steam followed November 30, 2020; their launch exposure and settlement timing therefore differ. The developer continued doing contract work. This is a counterexample to claiming itch.io can never earn money, but **not evidence that a new publisher will earn similarly**.

In the [Trouble Comes Twice postmortem](https://teamfoxglove.itch.io/trouble-comes-twice/devlog/766093/trouble-comes-twice-postmortem), the team reported around **€85,000 associated with the game**, but combined Kickstarter, Patreon, pre-sales and actual sales. Expenses, freelancers and reinvestment reduced personal proceeds. The source does not separate itch.io transactions, so Mercata leaves **itch.io game revenue and personal net profit unknown** for this case.

One successful puzzle game and one multi-channel visual novel, both voluntarily disclosed, cannot identify typical game sales, market-wide mean or median profits, or the effect of choosing itch.io rather than Steam. They do establish why **paid units, store gross, after-fee cash receipts, external funding, and developer profit require different fields**.
