# A YEAR OF SPRINGS — five-year cumulative itch.io payment continuity

The developer's [original first-week report](https://npckc.itch.io/a-year-of-springs/devlog/255726/week-1-numbers-for-a-year-of-springs-how-i-did-didnt-do-promotion-for-the-game) recorded **324 payments and 458 downloads** following the game's May 14, 2021 itch.io launch. The same developer's [five-year retrospective](https://tnywndr.cafe/office/2026-05-14-five-years-of-springs/) reported **1,789 cumulative itch.io payments and 5,929 cumulative downloads** as of May 2026.

The difference between these two consistent *payment-event* measures is **1,465 additional payments after launch week**, or **81.9% of the five-year cumulative total**. This is a real example of post-launch payment activity, not a platform-wide expected long-tail yield. The other **18.1%** of recorded payment events were already counted in the launch week. Neither number counts unique buyers, estimates individual net income or tells us in which of the intervening years those payments happened.

The author had earlier games and an audience before this paid game; more translations, updates and storefront releases followed. That evolving business context prevents crediting later itch.io payments to organic itch.io search, the release calendar, or another storefront as a causal force. First-week and five-year download events also include community copies and are **not compatible paid-purchaser denominators**.

This single case advances the measurement of post-launch economic activity while leaving unchanged the missing representative first-time game seller cohort, median earnings, player acquisition cost and labor-adjusted profit.
