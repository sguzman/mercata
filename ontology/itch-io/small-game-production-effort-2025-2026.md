# Small indie games: free browser prototypes and jam production hours

Three developer-published itch.io postmortems provide **specific production workload examples**, not a representative sample of games for sale. [Machine crosswalk](small-game-production-effort-2025-2026.json).

The creator of [Come Closer, It's Cold](https://itch.io/blog/1561059/come-closer-its-cold-postmortem-my-first-game-in-2-weeks) reported building and releasing a free nine-minute browser game in about **two calendar weeks** in June 2026. The launch-day post gives **263 page view events and 101 browser play events** after five posts on Reddit. The game was free, so neither events nor posts can prove paid buyer acquisition or game revenue. The author did not give a person-hour production ledger.

The solo creator of [#No_Hacks](https://michellerakar.itch.io/no-hacks/devlog/929349/no-hacks-devlog-5-postmortems) reported about **140 hours over a month** for a small free/optional-payment puzzle game entered in a jam, versus about **102 hours** for their earlier jam project. This is a creator-reported labor estimate, not an hourly income or proof of the expected time for any other game's design or engine.

A separate [Spring Cleaning team jam postmortem](https://itch.io/devlog/1515448/devlog-00-gamejam-postmortem.amp) records **15 hours ideation, 12 blocking, 16 production, and 8 finishing**. Their sum is 51 reported project-phase hours, **not 51 team person-hours**, since the number of concurrent contributors and total effort were not documented sufficiently for that inference. Gameplay was designed to last under ten minutes, but the reported game-jam team workload is not a commercial manufacturing cost or profit forecast.

These cases support a bounded first-game scope: a small focused interactive loop can be shipped with weeks of calendar time or over a hundred solo hours in a specific creator's history. They cannot establish **median development hours, price sensitivity, a jam-to-paid conversion rate, developer wages or commercial viability** for itch.io titles. A first-game business plan still needs explicit time budgeting and a real seller cohort including zero-earning projects.

No game build, participant account, purchase, independent production-time experiment or private developer files were inspected.
