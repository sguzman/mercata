# Tabletop receipts, annual comparability and mispriced first-game zeros (2024–2026)

**Reviewed October 27, 2026.** [Structured records](2024-2026-tabletop-receipts-and-listing-price-confounds.json). Four first-party public reports cover three different developers, **not a random seller sample**. Two reports refer to the **same** publisher in successive years, not two independent sellers. No owner accounts, buyer records, transaction exports or copyrighted images were retrieved.

Copper's download events increased by **2,418** between the two reporting years. These are not matched unique buyer counts. The 2024 after-platform-share amount is quoted in **USD**, the 2025 figure in **GBP**, and product composition varied. No nominal monetary growth percentage, inflation adjustment, paid-order conversion percentage or seller-profit trend is computed.

For the 2025 Copper report, **£118.26** itch income, **£63.13** convention sales and **£24.80** DriveThruRPG receipts sum arithmetically to the stated **£206.19** all-channel TTRPG earnings figure. The **17 convention copies**, **35 itch payments** and **22 positively paid DriveThruRPG events** have different transaction semantics; DriveThruRPG also counted 257 'purchase' events that included zero-price uses. These counts cannot be pooled as independent paying customers.

Plane Sailing's **£1,055 total business loss** includes printing, travel and other expenses. It does **not** establish £1,055 in itch-only losses, because channel cost allocation is absent.

## A 2026 zero-sale case where the intended checkout price was wrong

On **September 10, 2026**, the creator of a first digital game [acknowledged zero sales and an unintended price presentation](https://itch.io/blog/1659443/wow-i-am-sorry). The developer said optional donations had been configured as additive prices rather than the tier choices expected, apparently inflating the game price. The creator intended the multiplayer game's price not to exceed **US$9.99** and announced a sale.

This is a *listing configuration confound* for any zero-order launch study. The author's statement does **not** verify the actual checkout price, whether buyers tried checkout, that the mispricing solely caused zero sales, or that sales appeared after the fix. The record belongs to a different digital-video-game category than the tabletop cases and should not enter their financial cohort.

## Study implications

- For paid demand, require **positive payment events** with a named account, denominator, calendar period, price model and gross-versus-after-fee distinction. A download count is not that denominator.
- For annual volatility, compare **the same publisher** only within the limits of its changed product mix and measurement method. Do not use unlike USD/GBP receipt values to produce a percent income change.
- For a proposed pilot, a zero-sale outcome is not interpretable without first confirming the live listed paid/download/donation configuration and verifying checkout eligibility. This published case is a reason to enforce existing accuracy preflight checks, **not** permission to launch or spend.

The fixed ledger's existing detail tasks advance conservatively: **paid-purchase demand 70→71** and **sales-volatility evidence 93→94**. The 244-task inventory is unchanged. Zero-sale incidence, seller mean/median, paid game checkout conversion, payment-channel profit, and actual full market purchase demand remain **unknown**.
