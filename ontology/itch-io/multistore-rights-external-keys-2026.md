# Game ownership, multistore releases and external-key limitations

**Reviewed October 26, 2026.** [Machine rules](multistore-rights-external-keys-2026.json). The [platform Terms](https://itch.io/docs/legal/terms) currently display an April 15, 2023 update date. This is public policy research, not a review of any particular publisher's contracts.

## Can a publisher also distribute a game on another store?

Yes, **itch.io's own publisher-content license is non-exclusive**. Section 4 of the Terms says creators retain ownership of submitted content while licensing itch.io to host, reproduce and promote it. This does **not** give itch.io sole distribution rights. The [creator FAQ](https://itch.io/docs/creators/faq) also says the owner can remove their content and that the service does not add DRM to uploaded files.

The narrower, defensible conclusion is that itch.io does **not itself impose blanket exclusive distribution**. It does not certify that a particular publisher has permission to publish each third-party asset, source dependency, recording, font, image, name or contracted work on a second storefront. Nor does it override other independently signed agreements, hardware/store rules or jurisdiction-specific legal restrictions. Those individual grants remain unknown until actually reviewed.

## Steam or other external keys are a separate entitlement

The [official download-key guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/download-keys) permits sharing an optional outside activation key with a verified itch.io owner. But an ordinary indexed itch.io paid project is expected to contain directly downloadable files; a bare third-party storefront key is **not a normal substitute**. Individual exceptions require platform support review. External key stock can run out, and some bundles intentionally disable external keys. The [quality guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines) also caution against submitting only keys or links to a different store.

One itch.io purchase followed by a Steam-key redemption still represents **one observed storefront purchase**, not a second independently paid Steam sale. Free demos and ordinary skipped-payment downloads do not automatically establish paid ownership. Keys can be manually issued, gifted or claimed in special 100%-off bundles, so an external-key redemption count is not a count of new paying buyers.

## DRM and offline availability

The FAQ says itch.io distributes the game files as uploaded without **adding its own DRM**. This does not certify that the developer's original binary contains no DRM, that a game's multiplayer server will remain online, or that a particular operating-system build works without a launcher or network authentication. The crosswalk deliberately records per-game DRM, offline operation and compatibility as unknown.
