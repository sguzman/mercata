## An actual comparable design, not an invented seasonality result

Use the same cohort of **mandatory positive-price games**, aligned by original release age and observation horizon, in equal-length **pre-event, during-event and post-event** windows. Record actual transaction timestamps in a normalized time zone, paid-unit order counts, refunds, discounts, price floor, source of buyer acquisition and developer after-fee receipts. Count each storefront project once across overlapping sale and jam labels. A project added to a jam and a sale contributes **one game**, potentially multiple event memberships, and an unknown number of successful purchases.

Creator Day is a distinct **retained-fee treatment** and should not be pooled with days offering game-specific discounts: it can affect net proceeds per transaction even when customers make no more orders. Sale Explorer launched during the 2026 summer event, further confounding naive multi-year discovery comparisons. Jam judges' ratings and submission totals are not sales and may refer to free or unfinished prototypes.

The 2025 Halloween and Black Friday windows are real historic dates but there are no matched per-game transaction cohorts for those periods in public source material. The 2026 summer sale and jam likewise do not supply a paid buyer census. This work **completes the fixed task to select comparable jam/sale/holiday windows and define valid units**. Actual seasonal sales effects, release-day advantage and buyer demand remain `null`, not zero.
