# Indie-game offer testing: evidence, variants and cost limits

Reviewed October 26, 2026. [Structured pre-release experiment protocol](indie-prelaunch-offer-testing-2026.json). This defines the scope of **two existing Mercata strategy tasks**; it does not commission an actual game or marketing experiment.

A [2026 qualitative study by Bjarnason, Linåker and Fagerholm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06004) interviewed **ten indie game-company representatives**. Seven companies had fewer than five employees. The interview locations spanned Sweden, the Netherlands, Switzerland and Ukraine. It describes how companies consider goals, test artifacts, participant recruitment, test design and feedback execution. This is **not** a random sampling of itch.io game sellers or real buyer transactions.

## Predeclared commercial offer experiment

**One game, two hypothetical variants.** Variant A offers the same positively priced downloadable full game without a demo. Variant B adds a clearly separate free playable demo while keeping full-game paid entitlement and price constant. Any actual execution is contingent on owner authorization, working build rights and seller/experiment eligibility; neither variant has been published or tested for this research.

**Audience and allocation.** Define a consented, eligible audience and record whether impressions arise from external referrals or platform browse. If genuine independent randomization is not technically or legally possible, classify staggered variant releases as descriptive observations, not a causal A/B experiment. A valid sample-size target would require a baseline paid-order rate and minimum detectable difference that are **currently unknown**; do not invent 100 visitors or another arbitrary numeric target.

**Exposure and payment measurements.** Within matched 30-, 90- and 365-day observation windows, collect authorized *aggregate* impression and page-view events, free demo downloads and plays, completed positive-price orders, refunds, settled receipts and actual paid marketing effort. A page visit is not a unique buyer. A free download is not a paid order. Refunds must reduce retained units and cash rather than vanish from the denominator.

**Costs and evaluation threshold.** Explicitly account for build/demo engineering and QA time, marketing hours, licensed creative work, any paid advertising, processor/platform deductions and refunds. Value developer hours under a stated rate or report unpriced labor. A defensible acceptance criterion is a **positive incremental refund-adjusted contribution after added cash and valued labor**, supported by an adequate comparable sample; otherwise record an inconclusive result. Establish cash and time caps before any experiment. No authorized spending caps or baseline success probabilities are available.

The [itch.io pricing](https://itch.io/docs/creators/pricing), [creator payment](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments) and [indexing](https://itch.io/docs/creators/getting-indexed) references support the platform mechanics, not likelihood of positive demand.

## Acceptance boundary

The existing task to **specify testable offer variants, costs, audience and evaluation threshold** now has a complete, explicitly bounded operational specification. The separate task to **measure acquisition traffic, conversions, work hours and cash costs** remains incomplete, because no merchant experiment or real measurement has taken place. Do not use qualitative developer interviews as paid-customer rates or estimate causal effects from a single retrospective.
