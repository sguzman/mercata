# From game jam entry to paying game customer: distinct events

Reviewed October 26, 2026. [Structured measurement crosswalk](jam-attention-to-paid-game-funnel-2026.json). Gamedev.js Jam 2026 ran April 13–26, with ratings ending May 9. The [current overview](https://itch.io/jam/gamedevjs-2026) displays **482 entries**, a [ranked-results snapshot](https://itch.io/jam/gamedevjs-2026/results/innovation) displays **486 entries and 483 rated entries**, while the [May 10 organizer retrospective](https://gamedevjs.com/competitions/gamedev-js-jam-2026-winners-announced/) reports **495 submissions** and 2,782 joined developers. All are kept as separate recorded statements.

The result snapshot and organizer both report **10,642 rating events**. There is no reliable synchronized project-count denominator across the cited pages; even dividing ratings by one selected entry count is not a defensible longitudinal engagement rate. The inconsistent sources are not silently corrected or added together.

## Commercial steps remain unmeasured

The [jam host guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/game-jams) distinguishes a game project page, a jam submission page, eligible voters, ranked results and configurable rating queues. A **submission** is a contest entry. A **vote** is a jam signal. A **ranking** is a relative contest score. None is a positive-price buyer or a verified paid game unit.

A valid future commercialization cohort would have seven separate events:

1. Pinned eligible jam project sample, including zero-rating titles and exclusions.
2. Authorized aggregate jam-directory exposure.
3. Attributable page visits.
4. Browser play or free demo download.
5. Paid checkout attempts for a truly priced game.
6. Completed retained paid game orders, net of refunds.
7. Seller cash proceeds after fees and measured production/maintenance labor.

Specify a single version, price, time window and reference channel. For post-jam income, observe the same projects at **30, 90 and 365 days**, disclose later delisting and right-censoring, and compare against games with defensible non-jam exposure where a causal effect is contemplated. A headline *game jam's number of submissions* cannot establish a monetization probability, first-time paid-seller income, or buyer demand.

The existing research covers **the method and measured jam participation aggregates only**. Actual later paid orders, authorized exposures, creator profit, buyer count and causal conversion remain unavailable. No real game or experiment was launched for this task.
