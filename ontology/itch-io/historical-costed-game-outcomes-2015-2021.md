# Historical game postmortems — costs, cross-store sales and exposure

**Reviewed October 26, 2026.** Two voluntarily disclosed, selected historical releases. [Machine evidence](historical-costed-game-outcomes-2015-2021.json).

## Lemma — 2015 multi-store release

The [June 25, 2015 developer postmortem](https://gamedev.net/blogs/832/entry-2261203-the-poor-mans-postmortem-lemma/) explicitly separated **46 itch.io sales / USD 701 gross before its then 5% marketplace share** from **3,171 Steam sales / USD 43,554 gross before its then 30% share**. The same author counted **1,896 itch demo downloads** and **10,126 Steam demo downloads**, including **277 Steam demo conversions** under the author's definitions. Those Steam conversions **cannot** be assigned to itch.io or calculated by simply dividing itch sales by demo downloads.

The author separately listed a **USD 30,000 game-wide budget**, an estimated **USD 80,000 opportunity cost**, **three years part-time plus 1.5 years full-time** of work, a core team of one and six contractors. The writer later clarified in comments an approximate **USD 30,000 before-tax income figure**, but its precise reconciliation with gross totals and costs was not presented. The reported game-wide budget must **not** be treated as though incurred solely to earn the USD 701 itch.io gross. None of these figures provides platform-specific final profit or today's average income.

## Sex Robot Road Trip — 2017 release, retrospective around 2021

The [developer's own postmortem](https://yossarianiii.itch.io/srrt/devlog/123207/postmortem-sex-robot-road-trip) stated **zero launch-day sales**, only about **four purchases at a USD 6.99 regular price**, and roughly **20 lifetime sales increasing to around 40** during a later press-attention interval. A brief 100%-off promotion produced many more downloads but only about **USD 2 in tips** according to the author. The post does not report complete comparable traffic and purchase data, so it establishes no causal press or discount lift.

The author called a figure of **USD 222** “gross profits”, then subtracted **USD 20** for music and **USD 100** for a Steam license that had not been used, arriving at **USD 102**. These labels are inconsistent with formal cash-flow accounting. In particular the unused Steam license is not an itch.io payment-processing deduction, and the quoted remainder does not price development labor or other unidentified costs.

## Scope

These cases supplement, rather than replace, the existing paid-game economics study. Both titles are selected author narratives from older market conditions, not a full cohort of 2026 first-time publishers. They illustrate why transactions, demos, promotion, game-wide expenditures, storefront-specific receipts and profit require different fields. They **do not** estimate representative zero-sale frequency, 2026 conversion rates, genre premiums, mean or median income, or what a new release is likely to earn.
