# Co-op bundle contracts: participant approval, weight splits and game entitlements

Reviewed October 26, 2026. [Machine rules and carefully fictional arithmetic](coop-bundle-consent-and-splits-2026.json) · [Offline consent/weight checker](../../site/itch-coop-bundle-review.mjs).

The official [itch.io cooperative-bundle guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/bundles) describes the host and participant relationship. The organizer chooses which projects and creators take part, a sale price, dates and each creator's **relative revenue weight**. **Every participant must approve** before the bundle can be made public. After the first approval, the game list, price, participating publisher shares and dates are locked, preventing a host from silently changing the deal. Resetting approval before first publication forces participants to review revised terms again. This is a real participation/permission requirement, not an implied license to add any third-party game.

Relative weights define proportional **allocations**: in a purely illustrative **2:1** arrangement with **$30 before deductions**, two recipients would be allocated **$20/$10 before applicable fees, taxes, payouts, discounts or tier differences**. The source does not prove either creator received those amounts in any real sale. Tiers may exclude some titles and require adjusted splits, and some distributions involve approved charity recipients.

For a co-op bundle splitting money across accounts, all participating recipients must be eligible for itch.io's **collected payouts system**. Existing direct-payment merchants may have to complete payout onboarding to receive their shares. A participation accepted by a developer is not proof their country, identity, tax record or payout provider has been approved. A rejected or non-responsive participant can delay the promotion. Optional pruning of non-approvers requires conditions including that pending projects are no more than **15% of the bundle's projects**, so it cannot be casually promised.

## Buyer access and non-sales units

For larger bundles containing **more than 15 projects**, download keys are deferred until individual projects are claimed, though purchase grants access to all included projects. An unclaimed entitlement is not a second customer, and claim counts are not bundle purchases. The [official key guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/download-keys) warns that some large bundles suppress external platform keys. A Steam activation key resulting from an itch.io bundle is not evidence of an additional paid Steam checkout.

The offline review checks that participants approve, have at least one project, have valid weights and payout eligibility, and that a simple **single-tier hypothetical gross** amount can be evenly apportioned in cents. It rejects malformed or incomplete participation, but cannot authorize a real sale or certify legal/licensing clearance. No bundle was actually created, no real revenue was measured and no buyer data was accessed.
