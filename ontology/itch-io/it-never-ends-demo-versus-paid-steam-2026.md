# Free itch.io demo plays versus separately reported Steam sales

**Reviewed October 27, 2026.** A [public October 22 developer retrospective](https://www.reddit.com/r/IndieDev/comments/1xcrt0m/two_months_after_launching_my_first_steam_game/) for *I.T. Never Ends* gives two distinct observations: **more than 20,000 cumulative browser-play events** for a **free itch.io demo** and **3,042 copies sold, $37,917 gross** for the **paid Steam full game** roughly two months after Steam release. This is a **single voluntarily reporting developer**, not a sampled games population or independently inspected publisher ledger.

| Reported observation | Platform and product | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| More than 20,000 | itch.io free demo | Cumulative browser-play events, **not** unique people |
| 3,042 | Steam full game | Self-reported copies sold over approximately two months |
| $37,917 | Steam full game | Self-reported gross receipts before unknown deductions |
| Approximately three front-page appearances | itch.io demo | Creator-recalled promotional placements, not purchases |

The creator says the demo received front-page promotion and suspects that few itch players became Steam purchasers. **No quantified source-matched checkout or wishlist cohort was published.** The Steam figures refer to the paid game's sales, not sales made on itch.io. The report does not disclose when browser-demo measurement began, how repeat plays were counted, or which Steam purchasers had played the demo.

Dividing the Steam copies by the itch browser plays would produce a **mathematically computable but economically invalid cross-platform ratio**: the numerator and denominator are not joined to the same people, event window or offer. Neither the creator's impression of poor transfer nor their concern about potential free-demo cannibalization establishes a causal effect. This case illustrates exactly why public play counters must not be converted to buyer probabilities without authorized, matched source-to-order measurements.

**Research state:** The existing browser-play-conversion question gains another actual publisher observation but remains incomplete. Verified cross-platform paid-order attribution, refund-adjusted cash, seller costs, net profit and any representative game conversion rate remain **unknown**.
