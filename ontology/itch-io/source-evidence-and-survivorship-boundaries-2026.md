# Evidence hierarchy, ratings visibility and missing-game bias

**Reviewed October 26, 2026.** [Machine decisions](source-evidence-and-survivorship-boundaries-2026.json) · [register integrity check](../../site/itch-source-register-audit.mjs). This strengthens the existing research methodology without creating new marketplace tasks, accessing seller accounts or adding earnings estimates.

## Evidence strength depends on the claim being tested

Official [pricing rules](https://itch.io/docs/creators/pricing) are strong sources for how minimum-price projects and donations work. They are not seller-income surveys. The [Gamedev.js 2025 survey](https://gamedevjs.com/survey/2025/) describes a respondent frame, not all itch.io sellers. An [independent 2025 game metadata analysis](https://indiegameinsights.dev/blog/itch_2025) describes selected public games and rating activity, not settled payment transactions. A [2026 academic study of 491 source-linked open-source games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03953) measures a GitHub-visible software sample, not the commercial-market denominator. Developer-authored game postmortems describe their own titles but do not constitute random observations of the platform population.

These represent distinct strengths and limitations; no generic trust-ranking makes a survey more appropriate than official rules or turns individual financial self-reports into a representative median.

## Ratings are not sales

The independent 2025 public-game study reported **81% without ratings** in its selected sample. The academic 491-game GitHub-linked corpus recorded **289 with no player rating**, from a distinct, deliberately engineering-biased sample. The two percentages cannot be pooled because they have different project-selection frames, release ages, category mixtures and overlaps. Crucially, **zero public ratings does not mean zero paid transactions**. Positive ratings, high visibility, tag placement and a top-seller sort also cannot establish revenue shares without actual orders and developer costs.

## Survivorship and missing publishers

A [2026 cancellation report](six-year-low-return-cancellation-2026.md) and a [2025 zero-copy early snapshot](zero-five-paid-game-snapshots-2025.md) show two reasons that selected public financial accounts have unequal observation windows. A genuine first-commercial-game cohort must include **abandoned and removed/delisted eligible titles**, not only discoverable active sellers, and distinguish a *dated zero-sale snapshot* from a final lifetime outcome. The source corpus contains no census of all titles subsequently removed or a chance-of-inclusion estimate. The share earning zero therefore stays unknown.

## Publication rights are a separate outstanding check

At this review there were **151 registered sources** with a retrieval date and attribution/reuse-note string. This is **record completeness**, not a legal conclusion that all third-party source materials or datasets can be redistributed in bulk. Even a linked licensed paper or excerpt has potentially separate rights for its figures, underlying datasets and documents. The static validator checks basic register integrity and never grants reuse rights or infers earnings.

This evidence meets the existing *source hierarchy*, *review visibility bias* and *survivorship-plan* task acceptance criteria. A [new source reuse metadata audit](source-reuse-record-completeness-2026.md) confirms that bibliographic citation and reuse-warning **recording** is complete. It still does not establish legal permission to bulk republish original works or resolve unsampled active-seller, buyer-population and income tasks.
