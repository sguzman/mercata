# First-game pricing misconfiguration and a dated zero-sale observation

**Reviewed October 26, 2026.** [Machine case](first-game-pricing-misconfiguration-2026.json).

A [September 10, 2026 first-person creator note](https://itch.io/blog/1659443/wow-i-am-sorry) described a publisher's first game on itch.io, with **zero sales as of the post date**. The author reported mistakenly treating donation and paid minimum-price settings as independent, alternative tiers, leading to what they described as an excessively high displayed offering. The developer intended to price the multiplayer product at **no more than USD 9.99** and mentioned a 50% promotion while correcting the problem.

The independently published [itch.io pricing guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/pricing) distinguishes **Donate / $0**, **Paid minimum**, **No payments**, and separately priced file entitlements. Donations may be voluntary when the project permits free access; a positive project minimum is not a suggested donation. Independently priced bonus files have their own access thresholds. These mechanisms are not safe to treat as mutually exclusive sale tiers without verifying the actual buyer-facing checkout and each file's entitlement.

The author did **not** publish the exact erroneous checkout calculation, detailed game-price configuration, visits, paid purchase attempts, release date or subsequent unit sales. Therefore it is **not possible to conclude the pricing error caused zero sales**, estimate demand at $9.99, or infer a platform-wide first-game failure probability from this single account.

For the existing first-commercial-release checklist, independently verify what a prospective buyer can obtain for **$0, the stated project minimum, and any paid-file threshold** before publishing a price or measuring sales. Never mark a real project's pricing as tested until its actual publicly presented options are checked. This case documents the type of risk, not a successful live seller-account test or income.
