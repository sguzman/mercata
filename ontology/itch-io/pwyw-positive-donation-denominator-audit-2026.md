# Pay-what-you-want payments are conditional — not a free-to-paid conversion rate

**Reviewed October 27, 2026.** [Machine denomination audit](pwyw-positive-donation-denominator-audit-2026.json). Operator [June 20, 2015 marketplace report](https://itch.io/blog/2/running-an-indie-game-store-2015) and current [pricing rules](https://itch.io/docs/creators/pricing).

| Observable category | Published historical number | Denominator |
|---|---|---|
| A tip above the minimum on a previously paid purchase | About **30%** of paid purchases included extra money | Already-paid transactions; not free downloaders |
| Extra cash among positive-price purchasers | **$3.67** mean additional amount | Relevant excess-payment observations |
| A donation to free content | **$3.68** mean conditional positive donation | People who donated, excluding free $0 access |
| Donation frequency among all free game downloads | **Unknown** | No complete $0 download-event denominator |
| Actual 2026 paid-game tip distribution | **Unknown** | 2015 report is dated and mixes categories |

In the [2026 self-reported first-month free game case](https://itch.io/docs/creators/pricing), one publisher described a **$49.99** optional gift, more than 100 page-view events, but did not report unique visitors or the full download denominator. One large gift cannot define a modern average or the conversion probability for a typical free game. The developer's cash deductions and later spending are separately recorded in [the existing cash waterfall](voluntary-donation-versus-game-sales-2026.json).

A game with a **zero minimum price** permits downloading without purchase; a payer may gain ownership while an unpaid downloader may not. A **positive minimum price** requires a paid order for ownership, and adding money above that price does **not** create a second game order. Embedded HTML5 games support donations, not mandatory paid browser access through the standard embed.

## Repeat payment versus repeat customer

The existing [repeat-buyer protocol](repeat-buyer-and-membership-measurement-2026.json) defines a returning paid customer by a **second, distinct, retained game order** tied to an appropriately authorized cohort. Another tip within one checkout, an entitlement key, an optional free download, and an external membership renewal are all different event units. No live buyer deduplication, repeat paid customer rate, or Patreon churn measurement was undertaken.

**Conclusion:** the dated operator percentages quantify conditional historical amounts, not 2026 free-to-paid conversion, average premium game success, median publisher earnings or longitudinal customer retention. Those values remain unknown.
