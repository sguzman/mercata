# Follower-feed referral observation after third-party reviews

**Reviewed October 27, 2026.** Source: Neiffarious, [“One Month Later: Success?!”](https://itch.io/devlog/1525163/one-month-later-success.amp), published **May 16, 2026**. Sample: **one voluntarily reporting game creator**, `Hey, Moon Eyes`, and one highlighted day. Unit: game-page **view events attributed to itch.io’s My Feed referrer**, not distinct customers.

The creator reports that two reviews appeared on **May 5, 2026** from other followed creators whose audiences were roughly 1,600 and 5,600 accounts. In the author’s retrospective, **approximately 500 of that day’s game-page views came from My Feed**. The same author reported approximately 215 added followers during the month, nearly 100 of them during the week after those reviews. All numbers are **rounded personal dashboard descriptions**, not an independently replicated data export.

The temporal order is relevant: **other accounts’ reviews → follower-feed exposure → self-reported project views**. It is **not** an experiment isolating the impact of publishing an author devlog or shipping a game update. The source also reports indexing around April 25–26 and changes to normal traffic; these and other features confound attribution. Even if both reviewers had 7,200 followers in arithmetic total, that does not identify distinct eligible impressions or unique people (audiences can overlap or be inactive).

This source narrows the existing follower-channel question by providing an actual public referrer-event observation; it **does not close** the original devlog/update visitor-lift question. The article supplies no paid order count, buyer deduplication, refund-adjusted revenue, causal conversion lift or profit. The account’s commercial offer status is unverified, and no buyer identities or screenshot assets are republished.
