# Game jams: submission gates, source rights and later commercialization

**Reviewed October 26, 2026.** [Machine crosswalk](jam-submission-commercial-rights-2026.json) · [offline submission check](../../site/itch-jam-submission-audit.mjs). This is a platform-policy review, **not** evidence that a real game has been submitted or may legally be commercialized later.

## Submission and deadline requirements

The [itch.io jam guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/game-jams) distinguishes a jam's submission window from its later rating window. Ranked jams can lock file edits during voting, but project-page presentation may still change. Hosts may issue a late-submission link, and a jam entry has a dedicated page distinct from its underlying game project.

On **September 14, 2026**, itch.io [announced configurable automatic submission requirements](https://itch.io/t/6943956/jam-submission-requirements-and-fields-update). A host can require particular platform builds, a free download or an entirely free project, a build/project created after jam start, a completed AI-use declaration, or a project not submitted to another jam. For platform matching, a selected **browser build must be actually playable**; a video or screenshot is not an HTML5 game. These rules are checked **when the participant submits**; a host's later changes do not retroactively reject earlier submissions.

A game with a free downloadable demo can meet a host's *at least one free file* condition without being an entirely free title. These are different price classifications. Some jams also ask entrants to provide source code or disclose reused assets using required custom fields. Actual individual jam rules must be read in full; not every jam selects every supported gate.

## Ownership, assets and after-jam releases

The [creator quality guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines) direct organizers not to use jams to solicit uncompensated work or make winners surrender their intellectual-property rights. Participants normally retain ownership of their creations. That is **not blanket clearance to sell every asset**: outside music, art, fonts, code and sponsor-required licenses remain separate. Nothing in these platform pages establishes whether a particular event's complete rulebook permits a specific commercial sequel, remaster, or paid port.

The [itch.io Terms](https://itch.io/docs/legal/terms) govern uploaded materials, while publisher-specific additional commitments or asset restrictions may apply. Therefore the crosswalk marks **post-jam commercial permissions for a particular game as unknown** until the specific jam rules and component licenses are actually reviewed.

## Jam popularity is not paid demand

Votes and ranks apply to eligible participants and chosen criteria. Hosts can restrict who votes, and public voting can be gamed or affected by large existing audiences. A high-ranked free submission, a completed jam, a showcased project or a later paid build does not prove that the competition generated any paid orders. The [offline validator](../../site/itch-jam-submission-audit.mjs) works only with synthetic declared jam/project metadata, not seller accounts or purchase records.

This completes the existing fixed *jam submission and later commercialization rules* research task, but not the distinct tasks measuring actual jam-to-paid conversion, paid customers, income or the commercial value of jam exposure.
