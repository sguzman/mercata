# Web-game developer earnings surveys — strictly contextual to itch.io

As-of date: 2026-10-26. This investigation compares two publicly published Gamedev.js community surveys, without treating them as estimates of itch.io income. See [machine crosswalk](web-developer-survey-crosswalk-2026.json).

| Measure | 2024 survey | 2025 survey |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total submitted survey results | 620 | 445 |
| Reported share with zero game-development income | 58.2% | 63.0% |
| Reported share below USD $1,000/year of game-development income | 17.7% | 15.4% |
| Reported share USD $100,000+/year | 2.8% | 2.3% |
| itch.io or similar publishing (combined category) | not recorded here | 67.4% of 439 responses |

Sources: [Gamedev.js 2024 survey](https://gamedevjs.com/survey/2024/) and [Gamedev.js 2025 survey](https://gamedevjs.com/survey/2025/). The reports distinguish each *annual game-development income* question from platform-publishing questions and from unrelated wage income. The [full January 2026 report, page 40](https://gamedevjs.com/dl/gamedevjs-survey-2025-report.pdf) states **435 answers** to the annual game-development income question, compared with **445 total survey submissions**. The survey ran December 8–19, 2025, and the report appeared January 27, 2026. The 2024 question-specific denominator remains unverified here.

## Why these figures cannot be used as itch.io success rates

Respondents joined voluntarily from the web-game-developer community. Games may be free, unpublished, contract work, ad-supported, sold elsewhere, or released on multiple stores. The platform answer combines itch.io **or similar sites**, not exclusive itch.io users. The income question is about *all game-development earnings*, not income from itch.io sales. A respondent with zero game-development income is not necessarily a paid-game publisher who made zero sales. Therefore the 2025 **63% cannot be applied** to all paid itch.io developers, nor multiplied by the platform's public game-listing count.

The descriptive zero-income share of these two independent annual surveys differs by **4.8 percentage points**; unknown selection effects and possible respondent overlap prevent interpreting that difference as a verified trend among web developers. The target of Mercata remains an eligible first-commercial-release cohort with genuine paid order and developer profit data (including zero-sale titles), which is still unavailable.

The crosswalk leaves every itch.io-specific income mean, median, profit and zero-sale fraction as **null** rather than converting these contextual data into an answer the surveys do not support.
