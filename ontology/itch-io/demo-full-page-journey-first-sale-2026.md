# One paid 2D game's demo-to-full-page journey is not demo-to-sale conversion

**Reviewed October 27, 2026.** Independent developer Komatsu.KIKAKU posted [a first-purchase disclosure on September 26](https://komatsukikaku.itch.io/gear-loot-battler-full/devlog/1677476/devlog-1-gear-loot-battler-is-out-and-someone-bought-it) and [demo/full-page navigation analysis on September 29](https://komatsukikaku.itch.io/gear-loot-battler-full/devlog/1682950/devlog-3-making-the-store-pages-tell-the-truth). The separately listed full edition is a 2D-tagged RPG with a current **$4.70 minimum offer**; this is not proven to be the price the initial purchaser paid.

The creator reported an initial sold copy and separately claimed **roughly one in five browser-demo participants proceeded to view the full edition's project page**. No underlying numerator, denominator, event identity, measurement period or buyer-level funnel is published. The approximated 20% is a self-reported **demo-to-full-page navigation relationship**, not a validated probability that a distinct browser player purchased the game.

The posts **do not link the first paid purchase to a demo participant**. The author also describes receiving most launch traffic through itch.io's newly released listings without purchasing promotion; attribution and discovery remain uncontrolled. Reading a paid page, triggering an impression, downloading a free demo, and settling a positive-price order are separate economic events.

**Research acceptance:** an observed but unverified publisher-level funnel *step* is now documented, with the actual demo-to-paid-order relationship, fee-adjusted merchant cash, refund rate, developer profit, and representative paid-2D-game buyer conversion still unknown. Neither account access nor private buyer data was used.
