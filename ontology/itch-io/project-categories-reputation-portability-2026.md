# Project eligibility, reputation and migration economics on itch.io

Reviewed October 26, 2026. [Machine role and event crosswalk](project-categories-reputation-portability-2026.json). This covers documented **platform policies**, not platformwide game sales or the success of a particular merchant.

## One marketplace, multiple product populations

The official [creator FAQ](https://itch.io/docs/creators/faq) and [first-page guide](https://itch.io/docs/creators/getting-started) list video games, tabletop/physical games, software/tools, comics, books, soundtracks, game assets and custom project types. Those are **not** all commercial video-game titles. A free demo is a file within a page, not another paid game. An HTML5 play is a play event, not a completed purchase. A cooperative bundle purchase can grant multiple game entitlements but should not be counted as one independently paid unit for each item. A bonus priced file is also not necessarily a separate game listing.

**Upload category availability does not mean every content type can be sold.** The [creator FAQ](https://itch.io/docs/creators/faq) limits certain sexual content, requires compliance with payment intermediaries, and reserves case-by-case review. The [quality guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines) require adult-content labeling and accurate tags; they disallow misleading/impersonating uploads and certain gambling-like or paid contest mechanics. These can affect indexing or account payments differently. The [2025 UK restrictions](https://itch.io/t/5133739/our-update-on-the-uk-online-safety-act) are a **time-bound geography-specific** response, not a generic all-country content classification.

## Buyer trust is partly page-bound

Through the [Interact documentation](https://itch.io/docs/creators/interact), creators can see and sort game ratings, enable threaded comments, choose voting rules, and run message boards with moderators. Ratings are **not a transaction dataset**: a rating event does not prove a paid sale. Platform-level review and indexing sanctions are separate from the creator's own comment-board moderation.

The [quality guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines) explicitly warn that ratings, reviews and user collections are **not transferred** when a creator recreates a nearly identical project page. Previous purchasers also do not automatically gain an entitlement to a newly created replacement page. A new page to simulate a discovery boost risks platform penalties.

## What survives switching distribution channels?

| Asset or relationship | Portable? | Economic consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Independently owned game binaries and licensed media | Conditional: original publisher rights remain, but exact third-party asset permissions govern | New packaging, hosting, QA and support work |
| DRM-free downloaded files | Buyers receive uploaded files unchanged under the operator's published rules | A local copy can remain with a buyer; it does not migrate purchase entitlement or store reviews |
| Native itch.io project purchase/key | Tied to the relevant entitlement and project page | New storefront buyer access is **not automatically granted** |
| Ratings, reviews and collections | Not transferred to a new itch.io project page by ordinary recreation | Trust signals and saved collections can be lost |
| Buyer messaging | [Interact](https://itch.io/docs/creators/interact) supports at most one buyer email/day after a sale, with opt-out | Messaging access is **not** authorization to export personal emails or migrate them to outside services |
| Account or publisher team roles | No general transfer guarantee established in the public documents inspected | Transfer needs its own terms, permission and new seller-payment checks |

**Measurement boundary:** the case describes unit, geography, sources and 2026 review period. The study observed no actual buyer migrations, transfer attempts, merchant login, moderation incident, download exports or commercial outcomes. It cannot estimate the causal cost of losing reviews, the number of excluded sellers or the fraction of paid buyers switching stores.
