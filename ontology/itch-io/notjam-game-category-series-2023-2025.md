# itch.io — one developer's game-category attention versus money, 2023–2025

This **is not a representative paid-game income study**. One itch.io creator, Not Jam, publicly released three annual retrospectives: [2023](https://itch.io/blog/656364/2023-in-review), [2024](https://itch.io/blog/858848/2024-in-review), and [2025](https://itch.io/blog/1284030/2025-in-review). Each contains **lifetime cumulative** activity by product category, including games alongside fonts, soundtracks and assets. The same author is already one case in Mercata's income disclosures and must not be counted again as three independent developers.

| Games category, cumulative | End 2023 | End 2024 | End 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Page views | 15,345 | 21,174 | 52,217 |
| Download events | 3,639 | 3,992 | 4,763 |
| Browser plays | 3,432 | 5,871 | 19,548 |
| Reported game revenue (USD) | $0 | $0 | $24 |

The published 2023 retrospective explicitly said **all projects were free/donation-based at that date**. Subsequent pricing-model eligibility is unverified. These observations illustrate that **game attention and game-category cash receipts are different dimensions**; they cannot identify unique buyers, mandatory paid units, game profit or effective hourly income.

Within the account's own cumulatively reported totals, game views rose by 5,829 between the 2023 and 2024 snapshots and by 31,043 between the 2024 and 2025 snapshots. These are **differences between snapshots of an evolving portfolio**, not calendar-year comparable sales cohorts. The creator also reports substantial receipts from fonts and other non-game products, which must never be credited to commercial game sales.

No game-level revenue distribution, refund ledger, gross-versus-net audited reconciliation, unique buyer count or labor time is disclosed. The [machine time series](notjam-game-category-series-2023-2025.json) therefore sets those market estimands to null. Treating this voluntary anecdote as the probability that a newcomer earns nothing would be misleading.
