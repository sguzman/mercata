# itch.io project-page creative comparisons — pre-execution research gate

**As of October 26, 2026.** [Machine contract](creative-comparison-audit-2026.json) · [Pure offline gate](../../site/itch-creative-comparison.mjs). **The two fixed tasks to design title/cover/screenshot/copy comparisons and exposure-to-click measurements now meet their source-backed planning acceptance conditions (100% each).** This does not complete the distinct tasks of actually gathering owner analytics, establishing causal purchase lift, or measuring commercial returns.

This is an **experiment design**, not a running itch.io integration, consented study, A/B tool supplied by itch.io, or verified effect.

Official documentation identifies the [project title, short description and cover image](https://itch.io/docs/creators/getting-started) as listing assets, recommends 3–5 screenshots, and treats misrepresentative images or false tags as [quality violations](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines). Changing one authentic variable at a time allows a narrowly interpreted comparison. The publisher also says duplicate/recreated pages used to regain Most Recent visibility are against its guidelines, and deleting/recreating can break buyers' libraries.

## What can actually be measured

The [2018 analytics announcement](https://itch.io/updates/updates-to-project-analytics-filtering-collections-impressions-and-more) defines an **impression** as a showing of a game title and thumbnail in a browsing surface, and **CTR** as the relative click-through frequency from these impressions. It describes that *at the time of the announcement* these metrics covered the previous **week** even when other analytics had longer ranges. This is historical documentation, **not proof the seven-day constraint is still current in 2026**. The seller must check the present analytics interface before comparing periods.

This protocol requires matching game, window, display surface, relevant visitor/device population and metric definitions. The input is a seller-supplied event aggregate; no raw viewer records or personal details are needed. The audit produces only a **descriptive click-through difference** when data is comparable and the quality/current-data conditions have been checked. A before/after creative swap, sequential releases, outside publicity, search re-ranking, and differing page ages have important confounds. The audit never calls these randomized A/B results.

Randomized causal claims additionally require genuine contemporaneous visitor-level allocation by an authorized study mechanism, verified data collection and further inference checks; itch.io's public help does **not** promise that facility. No such mechanism or actual outcome data were used here.

**Paid-unit conversion has an entirely different denominator.** Impressions are repeated display events, page views are page visits, browser plays are sessions or play events, and actual paying people require reconciled payment records. Even a measured CTR gain does not imply additional unique buyers, net proceeds or profit. Zero impressions leave CTR undefined rather than zero.

The [offline gate](../../site/itch-creative-comparison.mjs) explicitly rejects mismatched dates, surfaces, audiences, noninteger event counts, unverified current collection windows, misleading assets and recreated listings. Tests use synthetic example inputs. All actual effects, customer income, sample sizes and expected investment return remain unknown.
